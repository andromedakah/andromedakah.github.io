## The story — a servant you never named is one you cannot govern: as the workforce goes non-human and outnumbers your people, give every agent a first-class, scoped, revocable identity of its own

### The event — the non-human workforce is nameless (the datable hook)

- Okta, "AI Agents at Work 2026: Securing the agentic enterprise" — https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/ai-agents-at-work-2026-agentic-enterprise-security/ (via search summary — **[Consolidating source: 91% of organisations already use AI agents; only ~10% have a well-developed non-human-identity strategy / identity systems fully equipped to manage NHIs; only 34% apply the same security controls to AI agents as to human workers]**)
- Okta, "Securing the agentic enterprise starts with identity" — https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/securing-the-agentic-enterprise-starts-with-identity/ (via search summary — non-human identities can outnumber human users by as much as 45 to 1; "you can't secure what you can't see, and you can't govern what you haven't given an identity to")
- Okta, "Global CISO Insights 2026" — https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/global-ciso-insights-2026/ (via search summary — **[Source: survey of 306 CISOs / senior security executives; just 31% feel fully aligned with C-suite and board on acceptable AI risk; fewer than half say the board sees AI security as a business enabler rather than a compliance checkbox]**)
- Okta, "Secure and Govern Non-Human Identities (NHIs) at Scale" — https://www.okta.com/solutions/protect-non-human-identities/ (via search summary — the "apply human policies to non-human agents / shared credentials with broad permissions / ad hoc access" failure pattern; discovering and bringing "Shadow NHIs" under governance)

Claims sourced here (as reported): Okta's "AI Agents at Work 2026" and Global CISO Insights 2026 material reports that non-human identities (AI agents, bots and service accounts) now outnumber human users by as much as 45 to 1; that only around 10% of organisations have identity systems fully equipped to manage and secure them (also framed as ~10% with a well-developed NHI strategy); that 91% of organisations already use AI agents; that only 34% apply the same security controls to an AI agent as to a human worker; and that just 31% of CISOs feel fully aligned with their C-suite and board on acceptable AI risk. Okta frames the failure pattern as applying human identity policies to non-human agents, relying on shared credentials with broad permissions, and managing agent access on an ad hoc basis. Carried here as "the livery": a non-human workforce that outnumbers the family, dressed in one shared coat and carrying one shared key, named by almost no one.

- Dataconomy, "Only 16% Of Enterprises Effectively Govern AI Agent Access To Core Systems" (26 Jan 2026) — https://dataconomy.com/2026/01/26/only-16-of-enterprises-effectively-govern-ai-agent-access-to-core-systems/ (via search summary — corroborating the 71% reach / 16% governance figures)

Claims sourced here (as reported): the Cybersecurity Insiders 2026 CISO AI Risk Report ("The Ungoverned Workforce," survey of 235 large-enterprise security leaders) found that 92% lack full visibility into their AI identities, 86% do not enforce access policies for those identities, and 71% report that AI systems have access to core business platforms (ERP, CRM and financial systems) while only 16% govern that access effectively. Carried here as the concrete shape of the identity gap: reach is running ahead of the roll.

### The standards floor — where an identity is issued and revoked

Claims sourced here (as reported): the EU AI Act high-risk record-keeping, traceability and human-oversight duties, the penalty ceiling, and Recitals 99–100 on chains of agents. Carried here as the regulatory floor: no identity, no traceability.
