## 1 · Executive Summary (90-second read)

For a month this radar has argued one thesis — *the model commoditizes; own and govern the layer around it* — and for weeks a single countdown has run beneath every edition: **EU AI Act enforcement, 2 August.** This week the countdown **split.** The **Digital Omnibus on AI,** the EU's long-negotiated simplification package (Commission proposal **19 Nov 2025;** provisional trilogue deal **7 May;** Parliament endorsed **16 June;** Council final sign-off **29 June;** published in the Official Journal **24 July,** in force **27 July,** as reported), redrew the calendar — and the market is reading it wrong. The near-universal headline is *"EU delays the AI Act to 2027."* That is true of exactly **one half** of the 2 August date: the **high-risk obligations** in Chapter III — the conformity assessments, technical documentation, registration and human-oversight paperwork for **Annex III** standalone systems (recruitment, credit scoring, biometric ID) — slip to **2 Dec 2027,** and high-risk AI embedded in **Annex I** regulated products (medical devices, machinery, toys) to **2 Aug 2028.** But the **other half did not move.** On **2 August — 7 days from today —** **Article 50 transparency** becomes enforceable across all 27 member states (chatbot and agent disclosure, machine-readable marking of AI-generated content, deepfake disclosure, public-interest AI-text disclosure), and the Commission's **GPAI enforcement powers** switch on: authority to investigate and **fine general-purpose-model providers up to €15M or 3% of global turnover,** applicable **retroactively to violations dating to August 2025.** As Morgan Lewis counsel put it (as reported), the amendments are *"extra time to finish compliance work, not a softening of underlying obligations."* An extension to file is not an extension to pay. This lands the same week the standing threads converge: **Kimi K3's full open weights publish tomorrow (27 July, 1 day)** — yesterday's contested masterwork, now with an independently-measured **~51% hallucination rate** Moonshot omitted from its charts — and **MCP's final spec goes live 28 July (2 days).**

1. **The AI Act's 2 August deadline split in two — and the half that binds almost everyone did not move.** The Digital Omnibus deferred the **high-risk filing** (Annex III → **Dec 2027,** Annex I → **Aug 2028**) but left **Article 50 transparency** and **GPAI enforcement** live on **2 August (7 days).** If you run a customer-facing chatbot, generate AI content, or deploy a foundation model, your obligation was **not** postponed — and the penalty clock (**€15M / 3%**) reaches back to **August 2025.** Reading the headline and standing down is the single most expensive misread available this week.

2. **A reprieve on paperwork is not a reprieve on liability — separate the obligation that moved from the one that stayed.** The relief is real: sixteen extra months to build conformity files for high-risk systems is a genuine gift, and the market pressure that produced it (DIGITALEUROPE welcomed the simplification; critics from EDRi to Parliament's Social Democrats called it *"unacceptable deregulation"* and *"a huge step backwards"*) is a signal that timelines can slip again. But the discipline is to **read which of your obligations moved.** The transparency and GPAI duties are *materially unchanged;* only some high-risk deadlines shifted. Treat the extension as time to file the deferred returns *correctly,* not as permission to stop paying what's due next week.

3. **Own the calendar, not just the compliance — build a governed inventory that answers "what's due when" per system, per obligation, per jurisdiction.** A deadline that can split once can split again, in either direction. The estates that will glide through 2 August and 2 December 2027 alike are the ones whose **model-and-system inventory** already tags each deployment with its obligation class (transparency vs high-risk vs GPAI), its live date, and the evidence owed — on infrastructure they own, portable across whichever way the next omnibus moves the dates.

**Bottom line:** yesterday's lesson stands — demand the chain of custody, ready your AI Act evidence — but today rewrites the deadline you were racing toward. The 2 August date did not vanish; it **sorted.** The high-risk *filing* moved to 2027; the transparency-and-GPAI *payment* still lands in 7 days, with interest accruing since last August. Read the notice, not the headline: an extension to file is not an extension to pay.

## 2 · Allegory of the Day — "The Extension"

*Topic: This week the EU's Digital Omnibus on AI — a simplification package negotiated over 2026 (Council final sign-off 29 June) and, as reported, published in the Official Journal on 24 July and entering into force 27 July — became binding law, redrawing the AI Act's 2 August 2026 deadline. Coverage overwhelmingly framed it as "the AI Act delayed to 2027." But the deferral applies only to the high-risk obligations of Chapter III: Annex III standalone systems move to 2 December 2027, and Annex I embedded products to 2 August 2028. Article 50 transparency obligations (chatbot disclosure, AI-content marking, deepfake disclosure) and the Commission's GPAI enforcement powers (fines up to €15M or 3% of global turnover, retroactive to August 2025) still take effect on 2 August 2026. As Morgan Lewis counsel put it, the amendments give "extra time to finish compliance work, not a softening of underlying obligations." The lesson for the enterprise: an extension to file is not an extension to pay — read which obligation moved and which still binds.*

Every treasurer learns the difference the hard way once, and never forgets it. The tax authority sends a letter granting a **six-month extension,** and there are two people in the room. The amateur reads the first line — *extension granted* — exhales, closes the folder, and tells the board the problem is solved until autumn. The professional reads the whole notice, and finds the sentence the amateur skipped: this is an extension **to file the return,** not an extension **to pay the tax.** The paperwork can wait; the money cannot. The liability was fixed on the original due date, the payment is still owed on the original due date, and — the cruelest clause — **interest accrues from the original due date** whether you file early or late. The extension moved one obligation and left the other exactly where it was. The relief is genuine, and it is also a trap for anyone who mistakes it for the thing it is not.

Here is why the trap catches good people. **The two obligations wear the same date,** so it is natural to assume that moving the date moves both. It doesn't. A filing extension buys time to *assemble the evidence* — to do the return properly, gather the receipts, get the schedules right. It buys **no** time on the underlying debt, and it stops **none** of the interest already running. The disciplined treasurer does three things the relieved amateur skips: reads the notice to the last line to learn *which* obligation moved; **pays what is still due now,** on the original date, to stop the meter; and uses the extension for its actual purpose — to file the deferred return *correctly,* not to pretend the whole liability went away. The one who celebrates the headline discovers, on the original due date, that the money was always owed, the penalty clock never stopped, and the "extension" bought nothing but a false sense of time.

This week the enterprise received exactly this letter, in regulation. The EU's Digital Omnibus granted a real and substantial extension — sixteen extra months to file the **high-risk conformity paperwork,** the technical documentation and registrations for systems that screen a job applicant or score a loan. That is the return, and the deadline to file it genuinely moved to December 2027. But the same notice left the **payment** untouched: the transparency duties that touch every chatbot and every piece of AI-generated content, and the enforcement powers that let regulators fine the makers of foundation models, still fall due on **2 August** — and the penalty clock has been running since **August 2025.** The headline says *delayed.* The notice says *one half delayed, the other half due next week, with interest.* Read the notice.

**The moral:** welcome the extension — sixteen months to file a hard return correctly is a real gift, and pressure that moved a deadline once may move it again. But an extension to file is not an extension to pay. Read the notice to the last line; separate the **obligation that moved** (the high-risk filing) from the **liability that stayed** (transparency, GPAI enforcement, and a penalty clock already ticking); pay what's due now, on the original date; and spend the borrowed time doing the deferred work *right,* not convincing the board the debt is gone. The estate that reads only the first line pays the interest.

**The question it forces:** *We were just handed sixteen months — but on which obligation? Do we read the whole notice, pay the transparency-and-GPAI bill that still falls due in seven days, and use the extension to file the high-risk return properly — or do we read the headline, tell the board we have until 2027, and let the interest run on a debt we never actually deferred?*

- Credit scoring and creditworthiness assessment are **Annex III high-risk** — the part that moved to Dec 2027. **Have we confirmed which of our AI systems are high-risk (deadline slipped) versus customer-facing chatbots under Article 50 (deadline held)** — and are we resisting the temptation to treat the reprieve as covering everything?

### 🏭 Manufacturing / Industrials
- AI in **machinery and safety components** falls under Annex I — deferred to **2 Aug 2028.** **Is our AI supply-chain map precise enough to place each embedded model on the right deadline,** rather than assuming a blanket "2027" that leaves a transparency or GPAI obligation exposed next week?
- Suppliers will cite "the AI Act was delayed" to defer their own evidence. **Do our procurement terms pin each supplier to the specific obligation and date that actually applies** to the AI they ship us?

### 🛒 Retail / Consumer
- A storefront **chatbot** and AI-generated marketing copy fall squarely under **Article 50 — live 2 August, not deferred.** **Have we shipped the disclosures** (users told they're talking to AI, generated content marked, deepfakes labeled) that come due in 7 days, regardless of the high-risk reprieve?
- Note the fine print: watermarking for content **already on the market** at 2 August gets a grace period to **2 Dec 2026.** **Do we know which of our AI content is covered by that four-month grace and which must be marked from day one?**

### 🏛️ Public Sector / Regulated
- Biometric identification and categorization are **Annex III** — deferred to Dec 2027 — but the **transparency and GPAI regime holds.** **For any citizen-facing deployment, have we separated what the Omnibus actually moved from what it left standing,** so we don't under-comply on the half that's live next week?
- Critics called the Omnibus *"unacceptable deregulation"* and a *"rollback";* industry welcomed it. **Whatever our view, is our governance built to survive a deadline moving in either direction** — tightening or loosening — without a rebuild each time?

## 4 · Technical Deep-Dive — The Calendar That Split

Read this week's story as one lesson about **reading the regulatory notice to the last line,** in three parts — the extension (what genuinely moved, and why the relief is real), the fine print (what did *not* move, and why it touches almost everyone), and the ledger (the governed inventory that lets you answer "what's due when" per system without a fire drill each time the dates shift).

- **The extension (the relief is real — for one class of obligation).** The **Digital Omnibus on AI** — the EU's simplification package, negotiated across 2026 (provisional deal **7 May,** Parliament **16 June,** Council **29 June**) and, as reported, published in the Official Journal **24 July,** in force **27 July** — deferred the **high-risk obligations of Chapter III.** Standalone **Annex III** systems (recruitment, credit scoring, biometric ID, essential-services eligibility) move from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 December 2027.** High-risk AI embedded in **Annex I** regulated products (medical devices, machinery, toys) moves to **2 August 2028.** For firms drowning in conformity paperwork, that is sixteen-to-twenty-four months of genuine runway — time to build the technical file, the risk-management system and the human-oversight design *properly.*
- **The fine print (what did not move — and why it lands on nearly everyone).** The Omnibus **did not touch** two things that switch on **2 August 2026 (7 days).** First, **Article 50 transparency:** any AI that interacts with a person must disclose it's AI; generative outputs must carry machine-readable marks; deepfakes and public-interest AI text must be disclosed — enforced by national authorities, fines up to **€15M or 3% of global turnover.** (One narrow grace: Article 50(2) watermarking for content already on the market at 2 August is pushed to **2 Dec 2026.**) Second, **GPAI enforcement:** the Commission and its **AI Office** gain formal power to investigate and fine general-purpose-model providers **€15M or 3%,** and — critically — that power reaches **retroactively to violations dating from August 2025.** The Commission's own guidance is blunt: *"the transparency rules of the AI Act will come into effect in August 2026."*
- **The ledger (the discipline that makes a shifting calendar survivable).** You cannot control when the EU moves a date, and this week proves it can move — hard, and in halves. What you control is a **governed model-and-system inventory** that tags every deployment with its **obligation class** (transparency / high-risk-Annex-III / high-risk-Annex-I / GPAI), its **live date,** the **evidence owed,** and the **owner** — on infrastructure you own. With that ledger, "the AI Act was delayed" becomes a per-row field update, not an all-hands scramble, and no single misread headline can make you under-comply on the half that's due next week or over-invest against a deadline that just slipped two years.

The strategic core: **a compliance deadline is not one number; it is a table.** After this week, "the AI Act deadline is 2 August" (or "it's 2027") is not a complete answer; *"each of our systems has an obligation class, a live date and an evidence file, and we can regenerate that table the day a regulation moves"* is.

### The trap vs. the discipline

| The trap — read the headline | The discipline — read the notice |
|---|---|
| "The AI Act is delayed to 2027" — stand down the program | Separate the obligation that moved from the one that stayed |
| Treat one date as covering every obligation | One system = one obligation class, one live date, one file |
| Assume the reprieve stops the penalty clock | Know the GPAI clock runs retroactively to Aug 2025 |
| Rebuild the compliance plan each time a date shifts | A governed inventory you regenerate as a field update |
| Use the extension to relax | Use the extension to file the deferred return *correctly* |

### Why a split deadline is a governance test, not a compliance footnote

Every force this radar tracked all month — commodity models, open weights, agentic autonomy, provenance (yesterday) — assumes a stable regulatory clock to prepare against. This week that assumption broke in the most instructive way: the clock didn't stop, it **split,** and the market is misreading the split. That is exactly the failure mode a governed estate is built to prevent. The firm that treats "2 August" as a single monolithic date is now equally exposed to two opposite errors — standing down on transparency because "it's delayed," or over-spending on high-risk conformity that just bought two years. Only a per-system ledger of obligation, date and evidence tells you which is which. A regulator moving a deadline in halves, the same week two of your open-weight and interoperability threads go live, is the signal that **the compliance calendar is itself a governed object,** not a sticky note on the CISO's monitor.

On legacy estates the danger is a single spreadsheet cell that says "AI Act: 2 Aug 2026," now "corrected" to "2027" by whoever read the headline — and a customer-facing chatbot that quietly misses its live transparency duty in seven days. The retrofit is the ledger: tag every model and system with its obligation class, its real live date, and the evidence owed, on infrastructure you control, so a regulation that moves in halves is a field update, not a scramble.
