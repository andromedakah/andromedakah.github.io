Claims sourced here (as reported): The Digital Omnibus on AI (the European Commission published its proposal 19 November 2025; provisional trilogue agreement 7 May 2026; European Parliament endorsement 16 June 2026; Council of the EU final sign-off 29 June 2026; published in the Official Journal 24 July 2026 and entering into force 27 July 2026, reported as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) defers the high-risk obligations of the AI Act's Chapter III: standalone Annex III high-risk systems (e.g. recruitment/employee management, creditworthiness assessment, biometric identification and categorization) move from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027, and high-risk AI embedded in Annex I regulated products (medical devices, machinery, toys) moves to 2 August 2028. The AI Office is given expanded/exclusive competence over AI systems built on GPAI models where model and system share a provider, and over AI integrated into very large online platforms/search engines. The exact entry-into-force and OJ-publication details are relayed as reported and should be treated as provisional pending the confirmed EU text.

- beam.ai, "EU AI Act 2026: GPAI Enforcement & 3% Fines Begin" — https://beam.ai/agentic-insights/eu-ai-act-enforcement-august-2-2026-gpai-fines

Claims sourced here: On 2 August 2026, Article 50 transparency obligations become enforceable across all 27 member states — AI systems that interact with people must disclose they are AI; generative outputs must carry effective, machine-readable marks; deepfakes must be disclosed; and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be disclosed unless subject to human editorial review — with fines up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover, enforced by national market surveillance authorities. Also on 2 August 2026, the European Commission's GPAI enforcement powers become applicable: the Commission (via the AI Office) can investigate and fine general-purpose-model providers up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover, applicable retroactively to violations dating to August 2025. A narrow grace period pushes Article 50(2) watermarking obligations for content already on the market at 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2026. Prohibited-practice breaches sit in the higher penalty tier (up to €35 million or 7% of turnover). The Digital Omnibus deferral applies exclusively to high-risk obligations in Chapter III; Article 50, GPAI enforcement powers and the penalty regime take effect on 2 August 2026.

### Reaction and interpretation (quotes as reported)

- Morgan Lewis (counsel commentary, via coverage) — the amendments give "extra time to finish compliance work, not a softening of underlying obligations." Relayed via July 2026 coverage (see Latham & Watkins and Covington analyses above; characterization as reported).
- European Digital Rights (EDRi), "Digital Omnibus is a major rollback of EU digital protections" — https://edri.org/our-work/commissions-digital-omnibus-is-a-major-rollback-of-eu-digital-protections/
- Liberties.eu, "AI Omnibus: Fast-Tracking Deregulation, Weakening Digital Rights" — https://www.liberties.eu/en/stories/ai-omnibus-ep-vote/45715
- ECNL, "AI Omnibus: deregulation at the expense of fundamental rights" — https://ecnl.org/news/ai-omnibus-deregulation-expense-fundamental-rights
- DIGITALEUROPE (industry association) — broadly welcomed the simplification package; relayed via coverage (as reported).

Claims sourced here (as reported): Morgan Lewis counsel characterized the amendments as "extra time to finish compliance work, not a softening of underlying obligations." Digital-rights groups (EDRi and others) called the Omnibus "a huge step backwards for a more accountable tech environment in Europe" and a "rollback"; Social Democrats in the European Parliament called the package "unacceptable deregulation." Industry associations, including DIGITALEUROPE, broadly welcomed the simplification.
