The Free Table

Sunday, 16 August 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

This week the radar walked the value up the stack — engine, pilotage, last mile. Today it asks the question underneath all of them: how does the thing you depend on actually get paid? On 15 August, OpenAI began turning ChatGPT's Free and $8 Go tiers into ad inventory in the EEA and Switzerland — the ad-free tiers are the ones you pay for. And on 14 August, Reuters reported Anthropic's IPO valuation hinges on a $190–200B 2028 revenue forecast, against a ~$47B run-rate today. One rents your attention; the other is priced on a promise. The board's question: for every AI vendor we lean on, do we know how it gets paid — and have we priced that into our data policy and our vendor risk?

1 · Executive Summary

For a month this radar has argued the model is a commodity and the durable advantage is the layer you own around it. Today the map turns to the question underneath every layer: how the vendor you depend on gets paid — because how it gets paid decides what it does with you.

1. "Free" is a price, not a gift — and it just reached ChatGPT in Europe. On 15 August, OpenAI began notifying Free and $8 Go users in the EEA and Switzerland that ads arrive "later this month," extending a program already live in the US, UK, Mexico, Brazil, Japan and South Korea. Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu stay ad-free — the tiers you pay to opt out of being the product. Guardrails per CPO Fidji Simo: "Ads will not influence the answers ChatGPT gives you," labeled and separated, conversations private from advertisers, data not sold.

2. The frontier lab you standardize on is priced on a promise. On 14 August, Reuters reported (exclusive) that Anthropic's IPO valuation — potentially one of the largest on record — hinges on a $190–200B 2028 revenue forecast, against a ~$47B run-rate today (up from ~$9B end-2025 and a $30B run-rate in April after "80×" growth). The market is paying now for revenue not yet earned — a reason to price vendor durability and your exit into every dependency.

3. The engine keeps commoditizing beneath the business-model layer. Open-weight GLM-5.3 and Qwen3.8 shipped 14 Aug; Grok 4.6 (12 Aug) tied GPT-5.6 Sol Max at $2/$6; Grok 3 was retired 15 Aug. The brain is a swappable, cheapening part. The trust gate holds: EU AI Act transparency live since 2 Aug (why ChatGPT ads in the EEA is a labeling event); AITSC launched 12 Aug.

Bottom line: the model is a commodity; the layer you own is the moat; and under every layer sits the vendor's business model — the thing that quietly decides what happens to your data, your spend and your continuity. This week one big lab monetized the free user through ads and another was priced on revenue it hasn't earned. Follow the money for every AI vendor — because a free plate is bought with your custom, and a house sold on a promise must someday collect.

2 · Allegory — The Free Table

Picture an inn at the busiest crossroads in the country, famous for a thing no other house dared offer: a free table. Anyone could sit and eat and pay nothing, and so the whole road came. But the innkeeper was no saint. The free diners were the crowd, and the crowd was the asset — the paying rooms filled because the common room was full, and merchants paid handsomely to set their wares beside the free plates and to be told what the diners reached for. The diner ate for nothing and was, without noticing, the thing being sold. The plate was free; the diner was the inventory. The wise traveler was not scandalized — but he kept his contracts and his trade secrets off the free table, because someone is always listening, and being listened to is how the plate is paid for.

Down the road, a rival house was bought — valued not on tonight's takings but on the grand feast the syndicate swore it would serve three years hence. A house sold on a promise must keep it: every season it would be under quiet pressure to raise prices, bundle the bread with the wine, find new coin — because the strongbox had to grow toward the number already paid. And a steward now stood at the door, requiring every merchant's placard beside the free plates to be plainly marked as paid — the payment disclosed and labeled, so a diner could tell a paid word from an honest one.

The question it forces: the most-used AI product on earth just turned its free tier into ad inventory, and the lab everyone benchmarks against is valued on revenue it won't earn until 2028. For every AI vendor we depend on, do we know how it gets paid — by our subscription, by our attention and data, or by a promise to its investors — and have we priced that into what we say at its table, what we let it keep, and what happens when the bill comes due?

3 · Technical — Follow the Money, Not Just the Model

The engine — paid per token, cheap: open-weight GLM-5.3 & Qwen3.8 (14 Aug), Grok 4.6 tying GPT-5.6 Sol Max at $2/$6 (12 Aug), Grok 3 retired (15 Aug), atop Opus 5, Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4-Pro. The rails — agent infra (free): MCP's 2026-07-28 spec went stateless, SDKs past 1B. The business model — where the market showed its hand: OpenAI monetizes the free user through advertising (your attention is the product); Anthropic is valued on a forward promise ($190–200B 2028 revenue vs ~$47B run-rate). The durable exposure isn't the token price — it's how the vendor gets paid, because that determines what it does with your data and your spend.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Write down how every AI vendor gets paid — and treat it as a risk line. Record the revenue model (subscription, tokens, attention-and-data via ads, or a forward-priced promise) beside the capability and price, and report it to the board. A vendor paid by ads, by subscription, and one priced on a 2028 forecast are three different risks to your data, spend and continuity.

2. Fence the free tier — make the ad-free enterprise SKU the sanctioned surface. Put a policy between confidential work and any consumer/Go plan, tell your people plainly that "free" means their prompts and attention are the product, and route anything sensitive to a contracted, ad-free agreement that bars advertising and training use of your data. Pay for the table where you can safely spread your ledgers.

3. Price vendor durability and your exit into every standardization. A lab valued on a two-year-out forecast has strong incentives to reprice and monetize before it arrives. State the switching cost, keep the engine swappable, secure price and data-use terms for the life of the program, and hold the exit and data-portability rights.
