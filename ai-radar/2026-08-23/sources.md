These are the "dam" figures: capital and compute impounded at record height.

Claims sourced here (as reported): McKinsey's State of AI survey (≈1,993 organizations) finds only about 39% of organizations report any measurable EBIT impact from AI, and only about 6% qualify as "high performers" with at least 5% of EBIT attributable to AI; roughly 23% have scaled agent deployments beyond pilots. Gartner expects more than 40% of agentic-AI projects to be cancelled before production by the end of 2027; only about 17% of organizations have deployed AI agents to date; and 40% of enterprise applications are expected to embed task-specific agents by year-end 2026 (up from under 5% in 2025). IDC/Microsoft report an average return of $3.70 per $1 spent on generative AI, with a median time to positive ROI of about 14 months and only roughly 25% of initiatives delivering the ROI executives expected. These are the "field" figures: the harvest that lags the dam.

- Kiteworks, "Stanford AI Index 2026: Why 62% Say Security Blocks Agentic AI Scaling" — https://www.kiteworks.com/cybersecurity-risk-management/stanford-ai-index-2026-agentic-ai-security-governance/
- Cybersecurity Insiders, "Stanford AI Index 2026: Security Is Now the #1 Scaling Barrier" — https://www.cybersecurity-insiders.com/stanford-ai-index-2026-security-is-now-the-1-scaling-barrier/
- SAPinsider, "Stanford AI Index 2026: What Business Leaders Need to Know About AI Readiness, Governance, and Risk" — https://sapinsider.org/blogs/stanford-ai-index-2026-enterprise-ai-readiness-governance-risk/
- ComplexDiscovery, "Stanford's 2026 AI Index highlights rapid growth and widening governance gaps" — https://complexdiscovery.com/stanfords-2026-ai-index-highlights-rapid-growth-and-widening-governance-gaps/

Claims sourced here (as reported): Stanford's 2026 AI Index finds that security-and-risk is now the #1 barrier to scaling agentic AI, cited by 62% of organizations — a 24-point margin over the next concerns (technical limitations 38%, regulatory uncertainty 38%, responsible-AI-tooling gaps 32%). Related concern levels rose sharply: inaccuracy concern from 60% to 74%, cybersecurity from 66% to 72%, and regulatory compliance from 63% to 72%. This is the "rusted gate": the trust problem quantified across the enterprise, tying this edition back to the "year zero" security framing of the 22 August edition ("The Locksmith").

## The gate — the trust layer is live

- European Commission, "Commission starts enforcing AI Act rules and new transparency requirements on 2 August" — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august
- European Commission, press corner — https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1714
- Gibson Dunn, "EU AI Act Omnibus Agreement — Postponed High-Risk Deadlines and Other Key Changes" — https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/
- Trussed AI, "EU AI Act Enforcement August 2026 Guide" — https://trussed.ai/resources/eu-ai-act-enforcement-august-2026-guide

Claims sourced here (as reported): As of 2 August 2026, the European Commission's AI Office, with national authorities, began enforcing the AI Act, and Article 50 transparency rules began to apply (AI systems must disclose that a user is interacting with AI; AI-generated or altered content must carry machine-readable marks). Active enforcement of general-purpose AI (GPAI) rules now enables model inspections and market restrictions, with fines up to the higher of €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. High-risk obligations were deferred to December 2027 and 2028 under the Digital Omnibus on AI. Cited as the trust-gate in statute — the plumbing that lets an approved system reach production.
