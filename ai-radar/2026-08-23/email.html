The Reservoir

Sunday, 23 August 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

Yesterday the radar found the agentic blade is two-edged and named trust "year zero." Today it follows the money and finds the two halves of the same story: the largest pool of capital ever aimed at one technology, and a harvest so thin the boards are asking where it went. Gartner puts 2026 AI spend at $2.52 trillion (+44%), ~55% of it in infrastructure — the dam. Yet McKinsey finds only 39% of firms see measurable EBIT and just 6% are high performers; Gartner expects 40%+ of agentic projects cancelled by 2027. And the gate everyone needs is rusted shut — Stanford's 2026 AI Index names security-and-risk the #1 barrier to scaling agentic AI, cited by 62%. The board's question: did we mistake impounding the water for watering the wheat?

1 · Executive Summary

For a week this radar walked the value up the stack — engine, pilotage, last mile, business model, the crossing, the acting agent, the ballast, the watchman, the guardrail, and yesterday the two-edged blade. Today it turns to the number the whole stack must answer to: the gap between what enterprises spend on AI and what they can prove it returned. The governing tension is no longer "can an agent act?" or "is it safe?" but "we have impounded a record ocean of capital — why is only a trickle reaching the P&L, and what is the valve that's stuck?"

1. The dam has never been higher. Gartner puts 2026 worldwide AI spending at $2.52 trillion, up 44% year-over-year, with roughly 55% (~$1.43T) in infrastructure — the physical dam — atop NVIDIA's platforms with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to mobilize over $500B of third-party capital, compute an "investable asset class." The water impounded is real, and vast.

2. The fields are still thin. McKinsey's State of AI (≈1,993 orgs) finds only 39% report measurable EBIT impact and only 6% are high performers (≥5% of EBIT from AI); ~23% have scaled agents. Gartner expects 40%+ of agentic projects cancelled before production by end-2027, with only 17% having deployed agents. IDC/Microsoft's $3.70-per-$1 carries a 14-month median to positive ROI and only ~25% hitting expected returns. Damming a river is not watering the wheat.

3. The rusted gate is trust. Stanford's 2026 AI Index names security-and-risk the #1 barrier to scaling agentic AI — 62%, a 24-point margin over technical limits (38%), regulatory uncertainty (38%) and tooling gaps (32%); inaccuracy concern jumped to 74%, cybersecurity to 72%, regulatory to 72%. The engine keeps cheapening (Opus 5, Grok 4.6, GPT-5.6/Luna, Gemini 3.6 Flash, GLM-5.3, Qwen3.8); the EU AI Act's transparency + GPAI enforcement, live since 2 Aug, enables inspections (fines up to €15M/3%).

Bottom line: the model is a commodity, the capital is abundant, and the moat is the unglamorous plumbing that turns impounded spend into a living yield — the channels (integration, data, adoption) and the trust-gate (security, governance) that carry water to a root. A record dam over cracked fields is a cost waiting to be written down. Stop admiring the reservoir. Dig the channels to a named P&L line, meter the flow, and oil the trust-gate open — because the height of the dam is not the harvest.

2 · Allegory — The Reservoir

A valley city, weary of drought, threw its whole treasury at a single work: a dam and, behind it, a reservoir vast enough to water every field for a century. The building was the wonder of the age; each season the citizens climbed the ridge to watch the water rise, and told one another the drought was beaten. But down in the fields the wheat came up thin — because damming a river is not the same as watering wheat. Between the reservoir and the root lay the humble work no one climbed the ridge to admire: the channels to each field, the sluices, the gates. The city had built the grandest source of water in the world and had not dug the ditches — and impounded water waters nothing until a channel carries it to a living root.

And one gate stood worse than un-dug: rusted shut. Even where a channel reached a field, many farmers would not open their headgate — no way to know the water was clean, no fence against a flood, no one to answer if it ruined the ground. They did not trust it enough to let it in. So the assessors found the bitter sum: for all the ocean impounded, barely a third of the fields drank at all. The cities that thrived treated the dam as a beginning, not an end: they dug the channels to each field, metered the flow so none drowned or starved, and above all oiled the trust-gate open — proved the water clean, fenced the flood, named who answered — so a farmer could at last let the water in.

The question it forces: we built the highest dam in history and the fields still crack — and when we ask the dry farms why, the answer is a ditch we never dug and a gate we never trusted enough to open. Have we mistaken the height of our spend for the size of our harvest? Have we dug the channels to a named P&L root, metered the flow, and oiled open the trust-gate that 62% of us say is holding the water back?

The rusted gate: Stanford 2026 AI Index — security/risk the #1 barrier to scaling (62%, 24-pt margin). The value gap and the security gap are the same gap seen from two sides. The dam is not the harvest — the channels and the trust-gate are, and almost no one is funded to build them.

Reservoir discipline: dig the channels (spend → a named P&L root; fund integration, data, adoption); meter the flow (stage-gate and kill dry projects early — 40%+ are heading for cancellation anyway); oil the trust-gate (treat security/governance as the sluice that lets value through, not a brake); clear customs (EU AI Act live). Keep the engine swappable — the model is the commodity, the plumbing from spend to a harvested root is the moat.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Put the harvest on the same page as the dam. Build one dashboard that sets AI spend against measurable EBIT impact, use case by use case, and compute your throughput: what fraction of spend reached a named P&L root. If you cannot answer it, that is the finding — McKinsey's 39%/6% says most of your peers can't either.

2. Dig the channels — and kill the dry ones. For your top AI investments, name the P&L line each is meant to move and fund the integration, data and adoption work that carries it there. Meter the flow: with 40%+ of agentic projects heading for cancellation anyway, kill the ones that will never water a field early, and pour the freed water into the channels already running green.

3. Oil the trust-gate — treat security as the sluice, not the brake. Stanford says 62% name security-and-risk the #1 barrier to scaling. Fund the security, governance and audit plumbing as an enabler of throughput — prove the model clean, fence the failure mode, name who answers, meet the EU AI Act's live duties — so a proven pilot can reach production. The height of the dam was never the point; the green in the furrow is.
