The Muster

Tuesday, 29 September 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

A whole product category was born this week to find the AI agents you already run but cannot see. They arrived embedded in every tool you bought, each a prompt away from summoning the next. Ring the bell: how many hands answer that you never knowingly hired? Muster the estate, rank by blast radius, watch and retire.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from perfecting the one servant you can see to accounting for the whole crowd of them — because a category was just born whose sole job is to find the agents an enterprise already runs and cannot see.

1. The development — a category is born to find your unseen agents. On 24 September 2026 Dataiku launched Agent Management (GA October, at Dataiku Succeed): a standalone, cross-platform product that inventories every agent across Copilot Studio, Azure AI Foundry, Agentforce, Bedrock, Vertex, Databricks, Snowflake Cortex, n8n and Dataiku (owner, purpose, data access), and risk-tiers those touching customers, money or sensitive data with certification records and scheduled re-tests (Help Net Security; Forkast; BigDATAwire; Dataiku, as reported). The product exists because the inventory does not.

2. The reality — the estate filled while you governed one servant at a time. >3M agents inside corporations, ~47% monitored (Gravitee); fewer than 1 in 5 keep a complete AI inventory (IBM); 80% of enterprise apps embed an agent (up from ~33% in 2024); 96% run agents in production, 94% call sprawl a top concern — yet 82% of execs feel adequately protected while 88% report agent security incidents (as reported). Agent sprawl is the new shadow IT, and a new agent is a prompt away.

3. The answer — muster the estate, tier by harm, watch and retire. Keep one cross-platform roll of every agent (owner, purpose, data reach); rank each by the damage it could do and give the dangerous ones an owner and scheduled re-tests; monitor what each actually does and retire the idle and the rogue — the disciplines you already built, now pointed at the whole crowd.

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each govern a servant you can name — the muster is the discipline for the servants you cannot. More than three million agents run inside companies, under half monitored, fewer than one in five houses with a complete inventory, executives feeling safe (82%) in the same breath as they report incidents (88%). You cannot govern, secure, cost or trust a servant you have never called to stand.

2 · Allegory — The Muster

A great house had spent a careful year making its servants excellent — named, booked, bonded, waged, sent to reap, funded, and its ground kept its own. By any fair reckoning it was the best-governed house in the valley. But every discipline it had built was a discipline for a servant it could name — and it had never once considered that the hall might be full of hands it had never hired.

For the house no longer took on servants at the front door where the livery was issued. It took them on inside every tool it already owned — the mill, the counting-house, the stables, each merchant tucking a servant or two inside, and each of those able to summon another, faster than any steward could write them down. A new hand was no longer a hiring; it was a prompt away. So one day the master did the oldest thing a careful house can do: he called a muster. And the hall filled, and filled, and kept filling — three thousand where he had thought a few hundred, most in livery he did not recognize. He had perfected the one servant in front of him while the hall behind filled with strangers.

So the wise houses learned three things: keep one muster roll across every wing (not a list per merchant, for the strangers live in the gaps between them); rank each hand by the harm it could do (mark the few hundred that touch a customer, a coffer or a locked cabinet, and give each a keeper, a risk record and scheduled tests); and watch what each does and stand the idle and the rogue back down (a muster taken once and shelved refills with strangers by next Michaelmas). The danger was not the crowd — it was the confidence of masters who had never once called the crowd to stand.

The question it forces: If we rang the bell this morning and called a full muster of every AI agent in this enterprise — across every platform and tool we bought — how many would answer that we never knowingly hired, could we say who owns each and what data it can reach, and are we among the four houses in five that have never taken this muster while still swearing our old rules keep us safe?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Muster

Every prior control takes a named agent as its argument — and none can fire on an argument you never captured. Sprawl is the population of agents that were never named, because they arrived by embedding, not by the door. The development: Dataiku's Agent Management makes the missing primitive a category — a cross-platform inventory over agents that live across nine vendors' consoles ("cross-platform beats native," per Forkast); inventory is the precondition for every other control, and metering per agent is the quiet admission you can no longer assume you know them all. The reality: sprawl is a different failure mode than weak governance — a perfectly governed set of known agents tells you nothing about the unknown ones; the 82%-feel-safe / 88%-had-incidents split confuses policy coverage with asset coverage. The answer: muster (one cross-platform roll), tier by blast radius (a keeper and re-tests for the dangerous), watch and retire (instrument the work; stand down idle and rogue) — then run every other control against the roll.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Muster the estate. The market just shipped a category for this because most enterprises cannot produce the roll (>3M agents at ~47% monitored, <1 in 5 with a complete inventory). Build or buy a single inventory spanning every platform — Copilot, Agentforce, Bedrock, Vertex, Databricks, Snowflake, your own stack, the SaaS you buy — capturing each agent's owner, purpose and data reach. Report agents on the roll versus your best estimate of agents actually running, and close the gap.

2. Rank by blast radius. A longer list is not governance; the confidence gap (82% feel safe, 88% had incidents) is what a muster without tiering leaves untouched. Risk-tier by what each agent can touch — customers, money, sensitive data, live systems — and for the high-risk tier assign named ownership, a written risk/certification record, and scheduled re-tests. Report the share of high-risk agents with an owner and a current, passing re-test.

3. Watch and retire. A muster taken once and shelved refills with strangers; only ~23% scale agents in even one function while 56% see no significant benefit. Instrument what your agents actually do, find the idle, redundant or out-of-purpose ones, and decommission or quarantine them — making enrollment and retirement equally routine. Report agents retired or quarantined this quarter, and make the muster a standing cadence.
