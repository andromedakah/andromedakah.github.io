Claims sourced here (as reported): MIT's Project NANDA report "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business" finds that roughly 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots deliver no measurable P&L impact and only about 5% create significant value; the study base is approximately 52 executive interviews, ~150 surveyed leaders and ~300 analysed public deployments; pilots that paired internal teams with external expertise succeeded roughly 67% of the time versus about 22% for internal-IT-only builds; and the diagnosis is a "learning gap" in tools and organizations rather than the quality of the models. The original MIT NANDA report predates 2026 and is carried here as the standing, widely-cited evidence base for the enterprise-AI value gap; the quotation ("The core issue is not the quality of the AI models — it's the learning gap, for both tools and organizations") is relayed from secondary coverage and should be re-verified at source before republishing.

- Gravitee, "State of AI Agent Security 2026" (via VentureBeat, "The enforcement gap") — https://venturebeat.com/security/most-enterprises-cant-stop-stage-three-ai-agent-threats-venturebeat-survey-finds

- ITECS, "Shadow AI Agents 2026: The 144:1 Identity Security Crisis" — https://itecsonline.com/post/shadow-ai-agent-2026

Claims sourced here (as reported): Gravitee's *State of AI Agent Security 2026*, surveying 900+ executives and technical practitioners, reports that 88% of enterprises confirmed or suspected an AI-agent security incident in the past year; shadow-AI surveys (Anagram and others) find roughly 78% of employees use AI tools even where no company policy exists and about 58% admit to pasting sensitive company data into them; and a majority of organizations with agentic deployments lack agent-specific governance policies. Presented to support the point that stalled and shadow pilots are not harmless.

Claims sourced here (as reported): the EU AI Act's transparency duties have been enforceable since 2 August 2026 — including obligations to disclose that a user is interacting with AI and to machine-readable-label AI-generated or manipulated content — with penalties up to the higher of €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, and up to €35 million or 7% for prohibited practices. The AI Trust and Security Consortium (AITSC) launched on 12 August 2026 with a founding cohort of roughly 50 enterprise security and technology leaders.
