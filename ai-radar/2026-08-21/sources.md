Claims sourced here (as reported): Deloitte's State of AI in the Enterprise 2026 ("The Untapped Edge"), from the Deloitte AI Institute, surveyed 3,235 IT and business leaders across 24 countries, six industries, and roles from director to C-suite. Its headline finding is that AI agents are "scaling faster than their guardrails": roughly 74% (close to three-quarters) of organizations plan to use agentic AI at least moderately within two years — up from about 23% using it at least moderately today — yet only 21% (about one in five) report a mature governance model for autonomous agents, implying roughly 80% do not. Top AI risk concerns are data privacy and security (73%), legal/IP/regulatory compliance (50%), and governance capabilities and oversight (46%). "Mature governance" is characterized in the report as clear decision boundaries (which decisions an agent can make independently versus which require human approval), real-time monitoring that flags agent anomalies, and audit trails that capture the full chain of an agent's actions. Attributed lines: "Enterprises where senior leadership actively shapes AI governance achieve significantly greater business value than those delegating the work to technical teams alone"; "True governance makes oversight everyone's role, embedding it into performance rubrics so that as AI handles more tasks, humans take on active oversight"; and "In the AI era, governance is more than guardrails — it's the catalyst for responsible growth." Figures are relayed via the Deloitte report/press materials and multiple secondary outlets as reported (primary Deloitte pages were unreachable behind the egress proxy) and should be re-verified at source before republishing.

Claims sourced here (as reported): As of 2 August 2026, the European Commission's AI Office, with national authorities, began enforcing the AI Act, and Article 50 transparency rules began to apply (AI systems must disclose that a user is interacting with AI; AI-generated or altered content must carry machine-readable marks). Active enforcement of general-purpose AI (GPAI) rules includes fines up to the higher of €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. High-risk obligations were deferred to December 2027 and 2028 under the Digital Omnibus on AI. Cited as the live trust gate a working AI system in production must meet.
