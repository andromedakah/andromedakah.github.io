The Watch

Monday, 26 October 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

You named your agents (the passport); this month the market shipped the piece that reads the name at the door. ServiceNow's "AI Passport" and NVIDIA's Open Agent Safety Platform make runtime authorization a product — just as SailPoint finds 79% run agents in production but only 2% have identity built for them (40-to-1). Post the watch; read your register; keep the rulebook and the cord in your own hand.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from naming the agent (the passport) and owning the ground (the foundation) to the piece between them — the watch that reads the passport at every door and can stop a hand mid-reach. A passport is only ink until someone reads it; this month the industry made the watch a product, and the data made it urgent.

1. The reality — adoption outran identity, 40 to 1. SailPoint's fifth annual Horizons of Identity Security: 79% of organizations run AI agents in production, yet only 2% have identity security built for agents — a 40-to-1 gap; 65% opened agents to >25% of staff; 54% at the lowest agent-identity maturity tier (SailPoint; itwire; Channel Insider, as reported). A name no one reads at the door is just ink.

2. The collision — the vendors shipped the watch, all in one month. ServiceNow "AI Passport" (15 Oct) — runtime authorization for agents and MCP servers at every step, signed CMDB-grounded credentials, graduated enforcement + kill switch, with NVIDIA's Open Agent Safety Platform (OpenShell + Sentry, ~120 partners); plus Oracle AI Agent Studio (21 Oct) and SAP's 1,000 agents (ServiceNow; NVIDIA; Oracle, as reported). You can buy a watch by Friday.

3. The answer — post the watch, keep the warrant. Read the passport at every door at runtime; ground permission in a record you sign (not the agent's self-claim); keep the policy and the kill-switch in your own hand — a watch whose rules live in the vendor's console is the foundation problem again (ServiceNow; AI Business, as reported).

Bottom line: the livery named the servant and the foundation owned the ground; the watch stands between them — the sentry who reads the warrant at every door and can stop a hand before it closes. The month proved both that the control is needed (SailPoint's 40-to-1) and that it is for sale (ServiceNow, NVIDIA, Oracle, SAP). Both are good news — but a watch is a control plane, and a control plane you don't command is a foundation you didn't choose to pour. Read the passport at every door, sign your own warrants, and keep the watch yours.

2 · Allegory — The Watch

A great house had learned, at last, to name its servants — every hand a sealed passport saying what it could and could not touch — and had even dug down to own the ground the house stood on. But the master made a tired assumption: that a passport, once sealed, read itself. The house had not stayed small; it took on servants by the hundred, and crossed a line it never noticed. Four servants in five now worked the halls unwatched; for every fifty sealed passports, only one door had anyone trained to read the thing. The passports were real. The reading was imaginary.

A census-taker put the number to him: seventy-nine in a hundred work rooms no one guards, and two in a hundred is all the reading you have bought. "A name on a paper no one reads at the door is just ink," he said. "A passport does not stop a hand. A watch stops a hand." And that was the very season the merchants arrived selling watches — a sentry at every interior door, reading each warrant as the hand reached for the latch, checking the master's own sealed register, and closing on the wrist if a hand strayed. "Rent ours," they said. "We've thought of everything."

So the wise houses took the watch — gladly, urgently — but on three conditions. Read the passport at every door, not once at hiring. The watch reads the master's register, never the servant's word — else it is theatre. And the master keeps the rulebook and the cord — rent the lantern, but the rules the watch enforces and the cord that stops a hand stay in your own hand, portable to another watch tomorrow. For a sentry who alone knows the rules and alone holds the cord is the most powerful figure in the house; if the merchant who sold him writes his orders, you have billeted a garrison and handed over the keys.

The question it forces: We named our agents — but who reads the name at the door? For every runtime action tonight, is there a watch that verifies the warrant against a record we signed, at every step, with a cord we can pull — or are 79 in 100 working unread? And if we rent a watch, do we still write the warrants and hold the kill-switch, or has the vendor quietly become the captain of our guard?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Watch

Every prior control issues, counts, funds or grounds the servant; none reads the passport at the door while the hand is moving. That is the watch — runtime authorization — and confusing an identity artifact (the passport) with a runtime control (the watch) is how a house that proudly names its agents finds 79% of them acting with no one reading the name. The reality: issuance outran enforcement 40 to 1 (SailPoint 79%-vs-2%); the control that matters is runtime, not registry. The collision: ServiceNow AI Passport verifies agents and MCP servers at every step against signed, CMDB-grounded credentials, with graduated enforcement + kill switch; NVIDIA's OpenShell enforces out-of-process at the kernel and Sentry isolates a rogue agent "in milliseconds" — but the watch must verify your record, not the agent's claim, and these platforms "do not settle who defines agent authority." The answer: read the passport at every door, verify your own signed register, and keep the rulebook and the cord (own the policy + kill-switch, insist on portability).

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Read the passport at every door. Naming agents is necessary and insufficient; SailPoint's 79%-vs-2% says enforcement is the emergency. Put runtime, per-step authorization in front of your highest-risk agents and every MCP/tool call they make — redirect/restrict/kill on an out-of-scope action, not just a log entry. Report the share of agent actions authorized at runtime against a record you control — your own 40-to-1.

2. Verify your register, never the agent's word. A real watch reads "a verifiable permissions record rather than relying on the agent's own claims." Wire authorization to a source of truth you own (CMDB/IdP), checked out-of-process, and prove each right-of-access traces to something you signed. Report the share of permissions grounded in your signed record versus self-asserted.

3. Keep the watch yours. A watch is a control plane; these platforms "do not settle who defines agent authority." When you adopt ServiceNow AI Passport, NVIDIA OpenShell/Sentry, or a studio, write the architecture so your policies and kill-switch live in a form you control and can port to another enforcer. Report whether you could change enforcers without rewriting every warrant.
