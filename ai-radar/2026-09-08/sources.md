## The story — a locksmith that can open any lock and mend it: get to the defenders' side of the gate, and mend your own locks faster than an adversary can pick them

### The pattern — three frontier labs, one covenant (a public model + a gated cyber twin for vetted defenders)

- The Hacker News, "Google, Anthropic, and OpenAI Unveil Cyber AI Models, Safeguards, and Access Programs" (Sep 2026) — https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/google-anthropic-and-openai-unveil.html (egress-blocked to direct fetch; relayed via search summary — **[Consolidating source for the cross-lab pattern; corroborated across the lab-specific sources below]**)

Claims sourced here (as reported): three frontier labs — Google, OpenAI and Anthropic — have independently landed on the same architecture, a safeguarded public model paired with a gated, more-capable "cyber twin" released only to vetted defenders. Carried here as "the locksmith": the same tool that finds and fixes a vulnerability can find and weaponise one, so the labs gate the sharp edge to the watch first.

- TechTimes, "Google Launches Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber: AI Patches 2.6x Faster, Restricted to Vetted Defenders" (3 Sep 2026) — https://www.techtimes.com/articles/326403/20260903/google-launches-gemini-38-flash-cyber-ai-patches-26x-faster-restricted-vetted-defenders.htm (via search summary)

Claims sourced here (as reported): on 2 September 2026 Google introduced Gemini 3.8 Flash and its cybersecurity-focused sibling, Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber — described as its most capable cybersecurity model. On CyberGym, the standard industry benchmark for finding vulnerabilities, it demonstrates frontier-level performance in autonomous vulnerability discovery, surpassing both the prior 3.5 Flash Cyber and significantly larger frontier models; Google's Chrome Security team found it produced 2.6× more correct patches to Chrome vulnerabilities than the best, far larger commercial models. It is not on open release: it reaches trusted defenders (governments, healthcare providers, telecommunications services) through the Fairwind Program, working with 650+ partners globally including CrowdStrike, Datadog, Menlo Security, Palo Alto Networks and Snowflake, and is paired with the CodeMender harness to find, verify and fix vulnerabilities at agentic scale. Palo Alto Networks is reported as having found the model exceptionally fast and above its model class on cybersecurity tasks including vulnerability hunting. Carried here as "the tool": autonomous discover→validate→patch as a gated product.

Claims sourced here (as reported): OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra (1–3 September 2026) is the first OpenAI model to meet the "Critical" cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework — defined as being able to identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in many hardened, real-world critical systems without human intervention, or to devise and execute end-to-end novel cyberattack strategies against hardened targets given only a high-level goal. It is materially more capable than GPT-5.6 Sol at vulnerability identification and exploit development, and ships with hardened safeguards: on cyber-jailbreak evaluations it refuses 91.5% of requests, versus 59% for GPT-5.6 Sol. Access to its advanced cybersecurity capabilities is restricted; a limited group of companies in OpenAI's application-based cybersecurity program, Daybreak, gets first access. Carried here as "the threshold": a frontier model assessed as capable of the full offensive chain autonomously, released like arms control.

Claims sourced here (as reported): Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5.1 (1 September 2026) is its most capable model for cybersecurity defense and life-sciences research (threat intelligence, vulnerability discovery, red teaming, drug discovery, biodefense screening). It shares an identical underlying architecture with the public Claude Fable 5.1 and differs only in the strength of its safeguards. Because the capability is explicitly dual-use, Mythos 5.1's reduced-safeguard form is gated to vetted organisations through Anthropic's trusted-access programs — a Cyber Verification Program (reduced cyber safeguards for defensive security work) and an invite-only Life Sciences Verification Program (reduced biology safeguards) — currently limited to a set of US organisations. Anthropic also announced a $35M open-source fund. Carried here as "the convergence": the third lab independently arriving at defenders-first gating of the same dual-use frontier.

Claims sourced here (as reported): an autonomous AI agent discovered 21 previously unknown vulnerabilities in FFmpeg for roughly $1,000 in compute costs (CSA); capable exploit development has fallen from weeks and ~$50,000 to hours and ~$2,200–$15,700 (CSA / Unit 42); Microsoft's June 2026 Patch Tuesday carried a record 206 unique CVEs (Dark Reading); CISA's full-remediation rate on its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog dropped from 38% to 26% in a single year while the critical-vulnerability count rose ~50%; IBM's 2026 Cost of a Data Breach Report puts the global average breach at $4.99M ($11.5M in the US), with AI-driven breaches costing about $1M more on average; and Unit 42 reports that for more than half of zero-day exploits, attackers are moving roughly seven days *before* a patch exists. Carried here as "the stakes": the technique is already loose at commodity prices, and the patch window is closing to machine speed. These specific figures are search-extracted and should be treated as soft until verified against the primary publications.

### Enforcement is live — the EU AI Act GPAI / systemic-risk regime (standing)

- European Commission, "Enforcement of the AI Act" — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/enforcement-ai-act (via search summary — **[Corroborated for 2 Aug 2026 enforcement]**)
- EU Artificial Intelligence Act, "Enforcement of Chapter V under the EU AI Act" — https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/enforcement-of-chapter-v-under-the-eu-ai-act/ (via search summary)

Claims sourced here (as reported): EU AI Act enforcement powers over general-purpose AI models became operative on 2 August 2026; models above the 10²⁵-FLOP systemic-risk threshold owe model evaluation, adversarial red-teaming, serious-incident reporting and cybersecurity duties — exactly the obligations that bite a cyber-capable frontier model. Penalties are tiered: up to €35M or 7% of global turnover for prohibited practices; up to €15M or 3% for GPAI non-compliance. Carried here as context: the labs' defenders-first gating is, in part, a response to this live regime.
