## 1 · Executive Summary (90-second read)

For a long year the radar built the governed house and then owned the ground it stands on — name it (the livery), keep one book (the steward), put your name on the bond (the surety), check its wage (the second wage), send it to reap (the two harvests), fund it (the purse), own the floor it plugs into (the foundation). **This morning the axis turns again: not what the servant is, serves, harvests, spends or stands on — but whether you would know, in the moment, if it had been turned against you.** This week the whole enterprise-AI industry converged on one answer, and it is a discipline, not a product: **prove your own guard.**

**The week — the agentic enterprise got its security layer, all at once.** At **Microsoft Ignite (17–20 Nov 2026)**, **Agent 365** was unveiled as a **control plane** that extends an enterprise's existing identity, security, governance and management to its agents, reported to manage **more than 50 million agents** already, with a companion "Business Analytics" layer described as "an **ERP system for AI usage**," and new **MAI Code 2** agentic coding models in the Foundry stack (Microsoft Ignite 2026; Redmond Channel Partner; Windows Forum, as reported). In the same days **Cisco** shipped "**Frontier AI Red Teaming and Agent Observability**," adding Anthropic's **Claude Mythos** to its *adaptive* red-teaming engine — a model that plans the tests and generates the attack ideas — with **Splunk** agent-observability enhancements (a **Tokenomics** view of cost and quality, private preview in December), every engagement **authenticated and tied to a named enterprise operator** (Cisco newsroom; CXOToday; Network World, as reported). **Zscaler** (20 Nov) tied frontier models into agent defence to **stop AI-driven attacks** (Zscaler via GlobeNewswire, as reported). The common verb across all three is not *deploy* — it is *secure, observe, and attack-test.*

**The threat it answers — the danger is not the lazy servant but the turned one.** An agent is a **non-human identity** that, unlike a human, **authenticates once and then acts continuously**; a compromised or deceived agent "keeps acting until someone detects it," and its actions can "look legitimate in the logs and at the API level" even as they add up to a breach (World Economic Forum; Okta; Cloud Security Alliance, as reported). These identities proliferate fast and are routinely **over-privileged** — "broad, persistent access without the safeguards typically applied to humans." Gartner turns that into an actuarial forecast: by **2028, 25% of enterprise security breaches will trace back to AI-agent abuse** due to unmanaged machine privileges and legacy credential practices, and it has already named the next accountable officer — by **2030, 80% of enterprises will designate the CIO (or CAIO) the "evidence custodian" for responsible AI** (Gartner, as reported). The prior controls name, scope, fund and ground the servant; **none of them tells you the morning your own servant has been quietly turned.**

**The discipline — the Proving: attack your own guard, watch its every hand, govern the fleet as one.** Three moves, and they re-use everything you have built. **First, red-team your own agents** — adversarially test them (goal hijack, tool misuse, prompt injection, identity and privilege abuse) *before* an attacker does, continuously rather than once a year, with every exercise authenticated and scoped; a wall you have never attacked is not a wall you can trust. **Second, post a watch** — per-action observability over every agent, in real time and priced per token, not a quarterly audit over a workforce that acts every second. **Third, govern the fleet, not the agent** — a **control plane you own** that extends your identity, security and governance to every agent, with every non-human identity inventoried, least-privileged and set to expire. Patel's sentence is the whole brief: **you cannot scale what you cannot secure, govern, or observe.**

## 2 · Allegory of the Day — "The Proving"

*Topic: This week the enterprise-AI industry turned, in near-unison, to securing, observing and adversarially testing the agentic workforce. At Microsoft Ignite (17–20 November 2026) Agent 365 was unveiled as a "control plane" extending identity, security, governance and management to agents, reported to manage 50M+ already, with Nadella framing autonomous AI as needing "governance, observability, security, and measurement"; Cisco shipped "Frontier AI Red Teaming and Agent Observability" (adaptive red-teaming with Claude Mythos, plus Splunk observability), with Jeetu Patel's "you cannot scale what you cannot secure, govern, or observe"; and Zscaler tied frontier models into agent defence to stop AI-driven attacks. Gartner forecasts 25% of enterprise breaches will trace to AI-agent abuse by 2028 (all as reported). The Proving allegory is the radar's own illustration.*

There was a great house that had, by long and careful work, made its servants excellent. It had named every hand and cut it a scoped key, kept one honest book, put the master's own name on the bond, asked whose second coin each advising servant took, sent the servants into the fields to reap, handed each a scoped purse, and refused to let the merchant who rented it the cheap stove pour the foundation beneath it. By any fair reckoning the house was well run, and the master was proud — as he should have been.

And then, on a grey morning, an old soldier who had kept more houses than the master had servants asked him a single question over the gate, and the master could not answer it. The soldier did not ask whether the servants were named, or paid, or scoped, or loyal. He asked: *"If one of them were turned against you tonight — bribed, or deceived, or simply made to believe a lie by a stranger at the postern — how long would it be before you knew?"* And the master opened his mouth, and shut it, because the honest answer was: *I would not know at all, until the silver was gone.* Every control he had built told him what each servant **was**, and what it was **allowed** to do. Not one of them would ring a bell the night a trusted hand quietly began working for someone else.

The soldier explained the thing that made this new. A human servant you can read — he tires, he hesitates, he must present himself at the door each morning and be let in. But these new servants were not like that. **They presented themselves once, were given a key, and then worked without pause, through the night, at a speed no human hand could match** — and a servant that never sleeps and never re-presents itself is a servant whose turning you will not catch by watching his face, because you never see his face again after the first morning. Worse, a turned servant of this kind does his treachery **in the open ledger**: every entry looks ordinary, every errand looks like an errand, and only when you add the day's work together do you see that the house has been robbed by a hand you trusted, moving exactly as a loyal hand moves. *"You have given keys,"* the soldier said, *"to a workforce you cannot see working, and you have never once tried to break in."*

Two roads, as always. The easy road was to trust the locks — they were good locks, bought from good merchants, and the ledger looked clean. The wiser road, which the careful houses took, had two halves the soldier had learned the hard way. **The first half was a watch.** Not a steward who reviewed the books each quarter, but a watchman in the great hall who could see **every servant's every hand, as it moved, by day and by night** — and who counted not only what each did but what each cost, so that a servant suddenly spending the house's coin ten times faster than its errand required lit a lamp at once. **The second half was stranger, and it was the half that gave the morning its name.** The master hired a trusted captain — a man of his own, sworn and named — and paid him to **attack the house.** To bribe the guards. To whisper lies at the postern and see which servant opened it. To forge an errand and see which hand carried it. Not once, as a formality, but again and again, as the world and the house changed — *"because,"* the captain said, *"you cannot trust a wall you have never attacked, and the enemy will certainly attack it, so better that the first assault your defences ever see is the one you ordered yourself."* The careful houses called this grim and costly exercise **the Proving**, and they held it to a rule: every mock assault was **authorised, scoped, and signed in the master's own name**, so that the captain's attack could never be mistaken for a real one, nor become a door of its own.

Here is the part that should keep a master awake. The grandest trading company in the valley arrived that very season offering to be the master's **watch and his captain at once** — a single great engine that would govern every servant, see every hand, and stage the mock assaults, all from one ledger. And it was a good offer, and the master was right to take much of it — one hall, one watch, over all his servants, is far better than a dozen blind under-stewards. But the careful houses remembered the lesson of the foundation: **a watch you rent and cannot dismiss, over a workforce you cannot see without its leave, is only the turning you fear wearing a merchant's coat.** So they took the engine and kept the mastery — the control plane theirs, the evidence theirs, the captain sworn to the house and not to the company that sold the watch.

**The moral, stated plainly:** the model is a rented stove; your knowledge is the larder; the foundation is the ground you must own; the gate is the one door; the passport names each agent; the census counts them; the inspector asks for the roll; the keyring is which key each holds; the locksmith reads your own locks; the provenance is the papers that trace the work; the errand bounds what a servant can reach; the bell rings when it slips; the livery is why you name every hand; the steward is why you keep one book; the surety is why your name is on the bond; the second wage is why you ask whose bread it eats; the two harvests is why you send it to reap; the purse is why you fund it like an employee — and **the Proving is why, having made the servant excellent and the ground your own, you must now assume it can be turned, and act accordingly: post a watch that sees every hand as it moves and what each one costs; hire a sworn captain to attack your own house, again and again, before the enemy does; and keep the watch and the captain yours, over a fleet you govern from one hall you command. For a servant that authenticates once and then works forever, unseen, is not made safe by being named and paid — it is made safe only by being watched, and by being attacked, by you, on a morning of your choosing.** A house that admires its own locks and has never tried to break in has not been prudent; it has merely never been tested — and the day it is tested will be a day the enemy chose.

**The question it forces:** *We named our agents, funded them, and own the ground they stand on. But if one of them were turned tonight — compromised, or deceived by a crafted instruction — would a watchman in our hall see it in the moment, by what it did and what it cost? And have we ever, even once, paid a sworn captain of our own to attack our own house and tell us where the postern gives — or will the first assault our defences ever face be the one an enemy chose, on a morning that is not ours?*

## 3 · C-Level Engagement — Questions by Sector

### 🌐 Cross-sector (any boardroom)
- **Would we know, today, if one of our own agents had been turned?** An agent is a non-human identity that authenticates once and then acts continuously; a compromised one "keeps acting until someone detects it" and looks legitimate in the logs (WEF; Okta, as reported). **Do we have per-action observability over every agent — what it did, when, and what it cost — in real time, or only a quarterly audit over a workforce that acts every second?**
- **Have we ever attacked our own agents?** Cisco's thesis of the week is that "you cannot scale what you cannot secure, govern, or observe" (Jeetu Patel, as reported), and adaptive red-teaming is now a product you can buy. **Have we run an authorised, scoped red-team exercise — goal hijack, tool misuse, prompt injection, identity abuse — against a production agent, signed in our own name, or will the first real assault be the first attack our defences have ever seen?**
- **Who is the evidence custodian?** Gartner projects that by 2030, 80% of enterprises will designate the CIO or CAIO the "evidence custodian" for responsible AI (as reported). **Have we named the single accountable officer for proving — on demand — that our agents are governed, observed and tested, and does that person hold the control plane, or does a vendor?**

### 🏦 Financial Services
- Model risk, fraud and insider-threat discipline already assume a trusted actor can be turned. **Have we extended that discipline to our agents — treating each as a privileged, non-human insider that must be continuously monitored and periodically red-teamed — or do we still treat an agent as a tool that cannot, itself, be the breach?**
- Gartner forecasts 25% of enterprise breaches will trace to AI-agent abuse by 2028, driven by unmanaged machine privileges (as reported). **Can we produce, for our regulator, a live inventory of every agent's non-human identity, its privileges, and its last red-team and observability check — the way we already evidence access reviews for human staff?**

### 🧬 Healthcare / Life Sciences
- A compromised agent with standing access to clinical or patient systems can exfiltrate or alter records while every individual action looks routine. **Is every agent touching patient data under per-action observability with alerting on anomalous volume or cost, and has it been adversarially tested for goal hijack and tool misuse before it was trusted with that access?**
- Validation and safety here are evidence-based by law and custom. **When the regulator or the ethics board asks how we know an agent has not been manipulated, do we have a signed, scoped red-team record and a continuous observability trail — or an assurance that the vendor's platform is secure?**

### 🏭 Manufacturing / Industrials
- You already run planned failure testing — stress tests, destructive tests, fire drills — on physical systems precisely because you will not trust what you have never tried to break. **Have we applied the same reflex to our agents, scheduling recurring adversarial red-team exercises against the agents in our operations and supply chain, rather than trusting them because they have not yet failed?**
- An over-privileged agent on an OT or logistics system is a single turned hand with plant-wide reach. **Is every agent least-privileged and set to expire, with a watch on its actions — or have non-human identities sprawled across our systems with broad, persistent access no human would be granted?**

### 🛒 Retail / Consumer
- Your agents touch checkout, pricing, inventory and customer data at machine speed, and a turned one would do damage in minutes, not quarters. **Do we have real-time, per-action observability on customer- and money-facing agents, with cost and volume anomaly alerts — the "Tokenomics" view Cisco/Splunk is shipping (private preview December, as reported) — or would we learn of abuse from the chargebacks?**
- Peak season concentrates both value and risk. **Before our biggest sales day, have we red-teamed the agents that run it — and can a watchman cut off a single misbehaving agent in flight without taking down the fleet?**

### 🏛️ Public Sector / Regulated
- Public accountability makes an unexamined, unobserved agent a governance failure, not just a security gap. **Under the duties you already owe (EU AI Act high-risk regime, live since 2 August 2026), can we evidence that every agent in a critical public service is under observability and has been adversarially tested — and that the logs and red-team records are ours, producible on demand?**
- Citizens' data and continuity of service raise the cost of a turned agent. **Are our agents' non-human identities inventoried, least-privileged and expiring, and is the control plane that governs them one we own and command — or one we rent and cannot dismiss?**

## 4 · Technical Deep-Dive — From the Foundation to the Proving

Read the stack once more, now from the seat of the executive who has stopped asking *is this agent named, scoped, logged, loyal, reaping, funded and standing on ground I own?* and started asking the one question none of those controls answers: *if this agent were turned against me tonight, would I know — in the moment, by what it does and what it costs — and have I ever tried to turn it myself, on purpose, to find out where it gives?* The livery names it; the steward keeps its book; the surety binds the master; the second wage asks whose interest it serves; the two harvests sends it to reap; the purse funds it; the foundation keeps the ground yours. **Every one of those governs what the servant is, may do, serves, brings in, spends, or stands on. The Proving governs whether you would catch its betrayal — and this week the industry made the distinction a product category: Microsoft's control plane, Cisco's red-teaming and observability, Zscaler's agent defence all answer the same sentence, "you cannot scale what you cannot secure, govern, or observe."** The point is blunt: **an agent is a privileged non-human identity that authenticates once and then acts continuously, unseen, at machine speed — so a governance programme that stops at "named and scoped" has secured the hiring and never once watched the work or tested the loyalty.**

- **The threat — why an agent is a new kind of insider.** **A human authenticates per session and must re-present; an agent "authenticates once and operates continuously," so a compromised agent "can keep acting until someone detects it," and its actions "look legitimate in the logs and at the API level" even when they sum to a breach (Okta; Cloud Security Alliance, as reported).** Agents spawn **non-human identities** — API keys, service accounts, tokens — "in security blind spots," routinely granted "broad, persistent access without the safeguards typically applied to humans" (World Economic Forum, as reported). The named attack classes are now codified — OWASP's Top 10 for Agentic Applications lists **Agent Goal Hijack, Tool Misuse, and Identity and Privilege Abuse** among them (as reported). Gartner's actuarial read: **25% of enterprise security breaches will trace to AI-agent abuse by 2028**, driven by "unmanaged machine privileges and legacy credential practices" (as reported). The engineering consequence: the agent itself is in the threat model now, not merely the model or the prompt — and authentication alone, as SandboxAQ's Jack Hidary put it, is "a false sense of security" without real-time visibility into what the identity is doing (WEF, as reported).
- **The discipline, part one — red-team your own agents, adaptively and continuously.** **Cisco's "Frontier AI Red Teaming" adds Anthropic's Claude Mythos to an *adaptive* red-teaming engine that uses the model's reasoning to plan tests and generate attack ideas; the customer sets the target and objective, Cisco's agentic framework runs the engagement within those bounds, and *every engagement is authenticated and tied to a named enterprise operator* (Cisco newsroom; Network World, as reported).** Three engineering points follow. First, **adaptive beats static**: a one-off annual pentest cannot keep pace with agents whose behaviour, tools and prompts change weekly, so red-teaming must be continuous and reasoning-driven, probing goal hijack, tool misuse, injection and privilege abuse as the system evolves. Second, **the red-team must itself be governed** — authorised, scoped, logged, and signed in the house's name — or the proving becomes a backdoor; "authenticated and tied to a named operator" is the guardrail that keeps a mock assault from being mistaken for, or becoming, a real one. Third, **you red-team to generate evidence, not comfort**: the output is a producible record that a given agent was attacked, how, and what held — exactly the artefact an "evidence custodian" (Gartner, as reported) will be asked to show.
- **The discipline, part two — post a watch (observability), and govern the fleet (a control plane you own).** **Cisco/Splunk's agent-observability enhancements focus on cost *and* quality — a "Tokenomics" view in private preview in December, GA early 2027 — within what Patel calls a "continuous trust loop" that protects agents from compromise while tracking the business value of tokens spent; Microsoft's Agent 365 is a *control plane* extending existing identity, security, governance and management to a reported 50M+ agents, with analytics billed as "an ERP system for AI usage" (Cisco; Microsoft Ignite 2026, as reported).** Observability here is **per-action and per-token, in real time** — not a quarterly log review — because a turned agent betrays you at machine speed and only an anomaly in *what it does and what it costs* will catch it before the damage lands. Fleet governance is **one hall over every agent**: each non-human identity inventoried, least-privileged, and set to **expire**, so a forgotten key cannot become a standing door. The caution travels with the capability: a control plane "governs agents but does not control their internal behaviour" (as reported) — it is the watch and the muster, not a guarantee of the servant's heart — which is precisely why the watch and the red-team are two halves of one discipline, and why both must be yours, not only rented.

The strategic core: **the agentic enterprise has, in one week, been handed its security layer — a control plane to govern the fleet, an observability layer to watch it, and adversarial red-teaming to test it — and the board task is to adopt all three as a discipline you own, not to admire your existing governance and skip the attack.** The market makes the temptation concrete: it feels finished to have named, scoped, funded and grounded your agents, and it feels grim and expensive to pay someone to break into your own house. But a wall you have never attacked is not a wall you can trust, and an agent you cannot watch act is one whose turning you will learn of from the loss. The board translation: **assume the trusted servant can be turned, and prove it cannot — by watching every hand and attacking your own guard, on a morning of your choosing.** The failure mode is exact: **a house that names, governs, funds and grounds its agents — and never once watches them work or tries to break in — will pass every governance review and discover, the first time a trusted agent is turned, that the first real assault was also the first attack its defences had ever seen.**

### Why "the Proving" is the discipline that matters now

For a long year the worry was whether the servant was named, scoped, logged, loyal, reaping, funded and grounded. **This week the industry moved the question past all of that: it assumes the trusted agent can be turned, and sells you the watch, the muster and the mock assault to find out before the enemy does.** Microsoft's control plane, Cisco's red-teaming and observability, and Zscaler's agent defence are three answers to one sentence — you cannot scale what you cannot secure, govern, or observe. Governance that stopped at the hiring will not catch the betrayal. The estates that keep their standing will assume the servant can be turned and prove it cannot — by watching every hand as it moves, by attacking their own guard on a morning of their choosing, and by keeping both the watch and the captain their own.

### How it lands on legacy estates

Same seam this radar keeps returning to — **be able to account for, and act on, what you run** — now sharpened to *be able to see your agents work, and to attack them yourself before an enemy does.* On a legacy estate the instinct, after a hard year of governance work, is to feel finished: the agents are named, scoped, funded, and the ground is owned, so the project is done. Under the Proving that is exactly the reflex to check: a governance programme that has never watched an agent act or tried to break in has secured the paperwork and left the work unobserved and the loyalty untested.
