The Proving

Saturday, 21 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

The agentic enterprise got its security layer this week, all at once — Microsoft's Agent 365 (a control plane over a reported 50M+ agents), Cisco's frontier AI red-teaming and agent observability, Zscaler's agent defence. An agent authenticates once and then acts forever, unseen; a compromised one looks legitimate in the logs. The discipline: red-team your own agents, watch every action, govern the fleet you own.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from what the servant is, serves and spends to whether you would know, in the moment, if it had been turned against you — and this week the whole industry converged on one answer: a discipline, not a product. Prove your own guard.

1. The week — the agentic enterprise got its security layer. At Microsoft Ignite (17–20 Nov), Agent 365 is a control plane extending identity, security, governance and management to a reported 50M+ agents (analytics billed "an ERP for AI usage"), with new MAI Code 2 models; Cisco shipped Frontier AI Red Teaming (adaptive, powered by Anthropic's Claude Mythos) and Splunk agent observability ("Tokenomics," private preview December); Zscaler tied frontier models into agent defence to stop AI-driven attacks (Microsoft; Cisco; Zscaler, as reported). The verb is secure, observe, attack-test — not deploy.

2. The threat — the turned servant, not the idle one. An agent is a non-human identity that authenticates once and then acts continuously; a compromised one "keeps acting until detected" and looks legitimate in the logs, and NHIs are routinely over-privileged (WEF; Okta; CSA, as reported). Gartner: by 2028, 25% of enterprise breaches will trace to AI-agent abuse; by 2030, 80% of enterprises name the CIO/CAIO the "evidence custodian."

3. The discipline — the Proving. Red-team your own agents (adaptive, continuous, scoped); post a watch (per-action, per-token observability); govern the fleet (a control plane you own, every NHI least-privileged and expiring). "You cannot scale what you cannot secure, govern, or observe" (Jeetu Patel, Cisco, as reported).

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each secured what the servant is, does and stands on — the Proving asks whether you would know, today, if a trusted servant had been turned. Microsoft, Cisco and Zscaler answered in near-unison: the agentic enterprise needs a control plane, an observability layer and adversarial red-teaming. Admire your governance and never attack it, and the first real assault will be the first attack your defences have ever seen.

2 · Allegory — The Proving

A great house had, by long work, made its servants excellent — named, booked, bonded, waged, sent to reap, funded, and standing on ground it owned. Then an old soldier asked a single question the master could not answer: "If one of them were turned against you tonight — bribed, or deceived by a stranger at the postern — how long before you knew?" And the honest answer was: not until the silver was gone. Every control told the master what each servant was and was allowed to do; none would ring a bell the night a trusted hand began working for someone else.

These new servants were not like men: they presented themselves once, took a key, then worked without pause, through the night, at a speed no human hand could match — and a servant you never see again after the first morning is one whose turning you will not catch by watching his face. Worse, a turned servant does his treachery in the open ledger: every entry looks ordinary, and only by adding the day together do you see the house was robbed by a hand that moved exactly like a loyal one.

So the wise houses learned two halves of one discipline. A watch — a watchman in the hall who sees every servant's every hand as it moves, and what each one costs, so a servant suddenly spending ten times too fast lights a lamp at once. And a sworn captain, a man of the house, paid to attack it — to bribe the guards, whisper lies at the postern, forge an errand — again and again, each assault authorised, scoped and signed in the master's own name, because you cannot trust a wall you have never attacked. And when the grandest company offered to be the watch and the captain at once, the careful houses took the engine but kept the mastery — the control plane theirs, the evidence theirs.

The question it forces: We named our agents, funded them, and own the ground they stand on. But if one were turned tonight, would a watchman in our hall see it in the moment, by what it did and what it cost? And have we ever paid a sworn captain of our own to attack our own house — or will the first assault our defences face be the one an enemy chose, on a morning that is not ours?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Proving

An agent is a privileged non-human identity that authenticates once and then acts continuously, unseen, at machine speed — so a governance programme that stops at "named and scoped" has secured the hiring and never watched the work or tested the loyalty. The threat: a compromised agent "keeps acting until detected" and looks legitimate in the logs; NHIs are over-privileged; OWASP's Top 10 for Agentic Applications names goal hijack, tool misuse, and identity & privilege abuse (Okta; CSA; WEF, as reported). Red-team it: Cisco's adaptive engine (Claude Mythos) plans tests and generates attacks within customer-set bounds, every engagement authenticated and tied to a named operator — adaptive beats a once-a-year pentest, and the output is producible evidence. Watch it and govern it: per-action, per-token observability in real time (Cisco/Splunk "Tokenomics," private preview Dec), and one control plane over the whole fleet (Agent 365), every NHI least-privileged and expiring — rent the engine, keep the mastery.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Post a watch. A turned agent betrays you at machine speed and in a clean-looking log; only an anomaly in what it does and what it costs will catch it. Stand up a single live view of every production agent's actions and token cost, with alerting on anomalous volume or spend, and name the on-call owner who can cut off one misbehaving agent in flight without taking down the fleet. Report the share of production agents under real-time, per-action observability.

2. Attack your own guard. A wall you have never attacked is not a wall you can trust, and adaptive red-teaming is now a product. Pick one production agent and run an authorised, scoped red-team — goal hijack, tool misuse, prompt injection, identity and privilege abuse — signed in your own name and tied to a named operator, and keep the producible record of what broke and what held. Report the finding and a dated plan to make red-teaming recurring and adaptive.

3. Govern the fleet and own it. Gartner puts 25% of breaches on AI-agent abuse by 2028 from unmanaged machine privileges. Inventory every agent and its non-human identity, strip standing privileges to least-privilege, set tokens and service accounts to expire, and consolidate governance into one control plane you own — and where you buy a vendor's engine, keep the mastery and the evidence. Report the count of agents, the over-privileged or non-expiring NHIs you closed, and who holds the control plane.
