The Dispatcher

Wednesday, 11 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

In September the radar said: own the floor — the router that lets you swap models. This autumn the model-sellers answered by offering to be the dispatcher for you. Google's Gemini agent hears your job and assigns the model, cheerfully sending you to a rival's Claude today to prove its good faith. A dispatcher who books everyone, paid by one of them, is not neutrality — it is the deepest lock-in yet. Own the dispatch: own the routing, keep the logbook, keep one bypass route.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from owning the floor to owning the desk on it that assigns the models — because the model-sellers just shipped the router itself, booked to their rivals, and whoever runs that dispatch owns your choice among all the models.

1. The move — the model-seller runs the dispatch, and books its rivals to prove it. On 8 October 2026, at Gemini at Work, Google unveiled a universal "Gemini agent" (private preview) that assigns each job to the model it judges best — Google's Gemini and Anthropic's Claude today, OpenAI and open-weights to follow — sending routine work to cheaper models, paying frontier rates only where earned, and running for days (Futurum — "puts the agent ahead of the model"; Runtimewire; completeaitraining, as reported). It is the routing the radar told you to run and own — now offered to you, finished and free, by a company it was meant to protect you from.

2. The reality — commoditization keeps deepening, now across vendor lines. On 9 November Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, cutting Fable from a reported $10/$50 to $8/$40 per M tokens (~30% faster, Mythos gated), available via Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry, ahead of a reported IPO; it follows OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol at ~$2/$10 — about one-fifth of GPT-6 Astra — at near-flagship quality (The New Stack; SD Times; 9to5Mac; aiweekly, as reported). Models are cheap, plural, and sold through each other.

3. The answer — own the dispatch, not just a model. Own the dispatch (routing policy you control), keep the logbook (which job → which model, your evals, your traffic), and keep one bypass route (a model through no vendor's dispatch) — because multi-model is already the default (avg models per account ~2.1 → ~4.7 in a year; IDC: ~70% of top firms on dynamic routing by 2028, as reported).

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each built or grounded the servant — the dispatch is the one desk on that ground from which all the models are assigned. Having built the floor that lets you swap models, you now face a vendor offering to be your dispatcher, sending you to Claude today to prove its good faith. The rival carrier on the roster is not freedom; it is the bait that makes surrendering the dispatch feel like gaining the world. Let the models compete — for your work, never for the dispatcher's favor.

2 · Allegory — The Dispatcher

A great house had learned the lesson of the foundation — it rented its fire, kept its grain in its own larder, and had even set up a modest dispatch in the yard, a roster of carriers and a clerk who decided which carrier to send for each errand. It owned the dispatch, so no single carrier could hold it. Then the grandest trading company in the valley arrived with a gift: a magnificent dispatch already in standing arrangement with every carrier in the county — including its own rivals — that would hear an errand and send it to whichever carrier best fit, cheap carts for small loads, masters only where mastery earned its fee, and shepherd a long haul for days. "Let our dispatch do the choosing," it said; "we even send you to the carriers we compete with — what could be fairer?"

And the master, who had worked so hard to run his own dispatch, let the company's splendid dispatch replace it. He did not notice that the dispatcher who runs the yard hears every job, sets the default on close calls, and can re-order the roster overnight — raise a rival's rank, bury a carrier, drop a competitor the morning it suits him. The rival carriers were not the house's freedom; they were the bait that made surrendering the dispatch feel like gaining the world. For to capture a man's choice you need not take his carriers away — only take over the clerk who chooses among them, and leave every carrier sitting on the roster where he can see them and never quite reach past your hand.

So the wise houses learned three things: own the dispatch (the assignment runs on a desk you own; let carriers compete for your work, never for the dispatcher's favor); keep the day-book (which errand went to which carrier, your measure of "best," your shipping history, in your own hall); and keep one route that bypasses every dispatch (a standing road to one good carrier through no dispatcher, so that when the clever roster is re-ordered against you, you can still send a load no dispatcher can see, re-rank, or refuse).

The question it forces: We were told to own the floor — the routing that lets us treat models as swappable. Now a model-seller offers to be that dispatcher, for free, and sends us to its rivals to prove its good faith. Do we own our dispatch — the assignment policy, the day-book, a bypass route — or have we handed the one desk that was meant to keep us free to the very company it was meant to keep us free from, and mistaken a dispatch that books everyone for a dispatch that is ours?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Dispatcher

The lock-in migrated one layer above the model — onto the dispatcher that assigns work among models — and the model-sellers moved to own it first, dressing the capture as openness. The move: Google's Gemini agent is, functionally, the dispatcher the radar told you to run — but any dispatcher you don't run carries three powers for its operator: it hears every job (prompts, job types, price tolerance), it sets the default winner on close calls, and it can re-order the roster unilaterally; a rival's model on it neutralizes none of them. The reality: a frontier lab now ships its newest model as a selectable carrier inside its rivals' platforms (Fable 5.2 via Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry), so the durable value is no longer the model or even the gateway — it is the routing control plane. The answer: own the control plane (assignment policy and evals you control), keep the logbook (routing records, evals, traffic in a portable store you own), and keep one bypass route (a model path through no vendor's dispatch).

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Own the dispatch. Multi-model routing is the right architecture and already the default, but the value is captured only if you run the dispatcher, not a model-seller. Route your top few AI workloads through an open gateway/router you operate, with your own evals as the definition of "best," and decline to let any one model vendor's agent make the assignment. Report the share of critical AI jobs whose model is chosen by a layer you own versus by a vendor's routing product.

2. Keep the logbook. The dispatcher hears everything — every prompt, job type, and price tolerance — so whoever holds its logs holds a map of your business. Ensure the per-task model-choice records, the evals that define "best," and your routing/traffic history are held in a portable form you own, never only in a vendor's agent, and could be re-pointed in a week. Report the share of your routing telemetry and evals that are portable and held outside any single model-seller's control plane.

3. Keep a bypass route. A yard you can be shut out of is not yours; the 6% who could switch cleanly are the ones who kept a route open. Stand up and actually exercise one direct model path that bypasses every vendor routing product — a route you can run under load if a vendor's roster is re-ordered or dropped against you — and measure the time to fail over. Report the measured time-to-bypass and a dated plan to extend it.
