## The story — after counting its agents (the census) and facing the authority that asks it to produce the roll (the inspector), the enterprise learns what the missing roll costs: the ungoverned, "shadow" agent is the open door a break-in walks through, and this week the industry put a hard price on it

- Kiteworks, "The IBM 2026 Cost of a Data Breach Report Proves AI Governance Failure, Not AI Itself, Is Driving Costs" — https://www.kiteworks.com/cybersecurity-risk-management/ibm-2026-breach-report-ai-governance/

- Shattered.io, "Shadow AI: 20% of Breaches, $670K Cost [2026]" — https://shattered.io/shadow-ai-breaches-670k/

Claims sourced here (as reported): IBM's *Cost of a Data Breach Report 2026* finds shadow AI (unsanctioned AI tools adopted without IT or security oversight) implicated in 43% of breached organizations, up from 20% a year earlier; a breach involving shadow AI averaging ~$5.39M and adding as much as ~$670K to the average breach cost, disproportionately exposing customer PII and intellectual property; a record global average breach cost of ~$4.99M (up ~12%); 68% of breached organizations with no AI governance policy in place; ~97% of organizations with an AI-related breach lacking proper AI access controls (a ~92% figure appears in some coverage); and one in four malicious breaches AI-enabled (a ~56% increase), adding roughly $1M each. Carried here as the "break-in": the ungoverned/shadow agent is the open door — governance failure, not the technology, drives the cost. Note: the IBM report was released mid-2026 and is re-circulated this week in board-level commentary; the specific figures are relayed via the security-trade coverage above and should be re-verified against IBM's primary report before republishing.

### The kill-switch — what enterprises cannot do (why it is a board problem)

Claims sourced here (as reported): Kiteworks' 2026 Data Security and Compliance Risk forecast/survey reports 80% of organizations experienced at least one security or AI-related incident in the past 12 months; a 65% AI-agent incident rate (among AI-agent-driven incidents: 61% data exposure, 43% operational disruption, 41% unintended actions in business processes, 35% financial losses, 31% service delays); 63% of organizations unable to enforce purpose limitations on AI agents and 60% unable to terminate a misbehaving AI agent; 65% having discovered employees using unapproved AI tools with sensitive organizational data; and 63% experiencing a compliance outcome (audit finding, required remediation, board escalation, contractual penalty, or formal regulatory investigation). Carried here as the "kill-switch": you cannot stop what you never enrolled — the missing ability to name, scope and terminate an agent is the finding.

### The surface — non-human identity (the attack surface underneath)

- Cloud Security Alliance (Lab Space), "The Non-Human Identity Governance Vacuum" — https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-whitepaper-nonhuman-identity-agentic-ai-governance-v1-cs/
- Cloud Security Alliance (Lab Space), "The Shadow AI Blind Spot: Ownership Fragmentation as Enterprise Attack Surface" — https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/shadow-ai-governance-fragmentation-systemic-risk-v1-csa-styl/
- Okta Newsroom, "AI Agents at Work 2026: Securing the agentic enterprise" — https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/ai-agents-at-work-2026-agentic-enterprise-security/

Claims sourced here (as reported): a CSA analysis of token/identity sprawl finds non-human identities (service accounts, API keys, OAuth tokens, machine certificates, and the credentials wielded by AI agents) outnumbering human users by an average of ~45 to 1, reaching up to ~144 to 1 in cloud-native environments; more than ~16% of organizations not tracking the creation of AI-related identities at all; and, while ~91% of organizations already use AI agents (Okta), only ~10% having a well-developed strategy for managing non-human identities. Carried here as the "surface": every unrostered agent is a standing, non-human credential in an unwatched lock.

Carried here as the "inspector": the record that answers a regulator is the same record that contains a break-in.
