1 · Executive Summary

For a week this radar walked the value up the stack and, yesterday, found the moat is the plumbing between record spend and a harvested P&L root. Today it names the length of plumbing the whole market is racing to lay: the agent gateway — a single governed chokepoint through which every agent's request to a model, dataset or tool must pass, so the enterprise can finally see, permission, meter and record what its agents do. The shift is no longer "can an agent act?" or "who watches it?" but "through what single door does all of this pass, and have we built it?"

1. A hundred gaps in the wall. Gravitee (750 CIOs/CTOs, US + UK) finds 47% of ~3M deployed agents are not monitored or secured — ~1.5M "at risk of going rogue" — and 88% had or suspected an agent incident in twelve months. IBM's Think 2026 research finds only 18% keep a complete agent inventory. Below the agent, shadow MCP: adoption +400% in 2025, 315 vulnerabilities logged (14.4% of all AI vulns, +270% QoQ). Gartner: shadow AI a factor in 40% of AI failures by 2027.

2. The gatehouse crystallizes as infrastructure. At Black Hat 2026 Snowflake launched its Cortex AI Gateway — identity, policy and audit at the tool-call level, 100+ MCP servers, zero-copy boundaries, dynamic masking and real-time exfiltration safeguards before an agent touches a row; built on Natoma, seven identity partners (1Password, Aembit, Cyera, Linx Security, Okta, SailPoint, Saviynt). Forbes calls agent gateways "the control plane for enterprise AI"; the identity market prices it too (Cyera/Oasis ~$1B, Okta/Permiso ~$200M in one 72-hour window).

3. The gate is also the toll and the ledger. The same door meters agent spend (a defense against Gartner's cost-driven 40%+ agentic cancellation rate) and logs its actions — which the EU AI Act's Article 12 reconstructability logging and Article 50 transparency, live since 2 Aug (fines up to €15M/3%), make a hard requirement for regulated-data agents. The rail underneath standardizes: MCP → the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation; the 2026-07-28 stateless spec is the protocol the gate governs.

Bottom line: the engine is a commodity (the fastest month in AI history just shipped 11+ models in 20 days) and the plumbing is the moat — and the fitting the market is now installing is the gate: one governed point where every agent is named, permissioned, inspected, metered and logged. An enterprise that wired agents in through a hundred unrecorded gaps did not gain speed; it lost the wall. Stop counting agents and start routing them — build (or buy) the single gateway every agent must pass, and make "no gate, no access" the rule.

2 · Allegory — The Gatehouse

A walled city grew rich on trade, but the trade grew faster than the stone, and — impatient — it let the wall fall into a hundred small gaps. Through them the trade poured: a carter no longer queued at the gate, he slipped in through whichever gap was nearest and out again the way he came. The merchants marveled at the speed. But a wall with a hundred gaps is not a wall: no one asked a stranger his name, checked what his papers permitted, or weighed what he carried out against what he brought in — and when a warehouse was found empty or a fire started, no ledger anywhere could say who had passed. The city had lost the very act of knowing. It could not meter a toll it never collected, enforce a law at a threshold it did not man, or answer the magistrate who asked who did this.

The cities that thrived did not tear the wall down and turned no honest trader away. They mended the gaps and built one gatehouse, and made every cart pass through it: a traveler gave his name (identity); his papers were checked against what he could carry (policy); his cargo was inspected before it reached the market (exfiltration control); a fair toll was metered (cost); and a clerk wrote in the ledger who had passed and where they were bound (audit). Slower than a gap by a minute, richer and safer by a mile — because now the city could see, and a thing you can see you can permit, price and prove.

The question it forces: we wired our agents in for speed, and most now come and go through gaps no one records — 47% unmonitored, only 18% inventoried. Have we mistaken a wall full of holes for an open city? Have we built the one gatehouse every agent must pass — named, permissioned, inspected, metered and logged — or are we one breach, or one audit, away from finding we cannot say who walked through, what they took, or how to prove it?

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Take the census — inventory every agent and every MCP server. Only 18% of firms can see it. Produce one list of every agent that can reach your data or tools, and every MCP connector in your estate, and mark which pass a governed point and which walk in through a gap. If you cannot build the list, that is the finding — you are the 82%.

2. Build (or buy) the one gate — "no gate, no access." Route all agent traffic through a single control plane and put the five checks at that one door: identity (name), policy (papers), data-loss/exfiltration control (cargo), cost metering (toll), reconstructable audit (ledger). Then close the gaps: any agent reaching data another way is cut over or turned off.

3. Wire the ledger to cost and compliance. Set token/cost caps at the gate (a defense against the 40%+ of agentic projects cancelled on runaway cost) and make the logs Article-12-grade, so "who did this, when, on what authority" is answered from the ledger, not from silence. Report two numbers to the board: the share of agents that pass the gate, and the share of agent spend metered at it.
