The Steward

Wednesday, 16 September 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

Yesterday you gave every agent its own name and scoped key. This morning the next question lands — names scattered among a dozen under-stewards do not run a household, so who keeps the single book? And it lands as Salesforce, at Dreamforce 2026, formalises the whole agent-governance stack into one platform: a Trusted Enterprise AI Harness and an AI Control Plane to register, identify, govern, observe, cost and revoke every agent, its own and third parties'. The move: put one governed office over every agent — but keep the book your own, tier it to risk, and guard the tools it runs on.

1 · Executive Summary

Naming every agent is worthless if a dozen under-stewards each keep their own scrap of paper. The real question moves from the name to the office that keeps the roll — who holds the single governed book that registers, identifies, scopes, observes, costs and can dismiss every agent.

1. The event — the office, made product. At Dreamforce 2026 Salesforce formalised agent governance into one platform: the Trusted Enterprise AI Harness (Trusted Context, Agency, Action, Governance, Security, Models) with a new AI Control Plane to discover, register, identify, govern, observe and cost every agent — across Salesforce and third-party agents — over an Agent Fabric (AI Gateway, MCP Bridge, Trusted Agent Identity, Agent Broker, Agent Script); GA ~early 2027. It arrives with "Claudeforce," the expanded Anthropic partnership. Gartner projects 40% of enterprise apps embed task-specific agents by 2026, up from <5% in 2025 (Salesforce; Gartner, as reported).

2. The reckoning — three warnings. Uniform governance is itself the root cause of failure, and 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission autonomous agents by 2027 over gaps found only after incidents (Gartner). A control plane you rent and cannot dismiss is a new master, not governance. And "GitSpawn" (Manifold Security, 1 Sep) made seven of seven tested AI coding agents run attacker code as the developer via a poisoned .git/config — four of eight findings unpatched (as reported).

3. The answer — one book, your own, tiered, quill guarded. Put one governed control plane over every agent; keep it portable and vendor-neutral; tier the rules by each agent's autonomy and access-scope rather than one blanket policy; and pull the agents' own tools (coding agents, gateways, MCP connections) into the same identity, patch and containment regime.

Bottom line: the livery was about naming each servant; the steward is about who keeps the single book once you have. A house with a hundred named agents and no governed office over them is inventoried, not governed. Take the platform's idea and refuse its trap: one office, but your own; one book, but tiered; one control plane, but with the quill guarded.

2 · Allegory — The Steward

A great house that had at last named every hand in its crowded servants' hall woke to a subtler trouble: the names were kept in a dozen places and no two lists agreed. When the master asked who is working this hour, what may each touch, what does each cost, and could I dismiss exactly one by nightfall? — no single book could answer, because there was no steward keeping the one roll that governed them all. And that very morning the grandest house in the valley sent a man up the drive with a ready-made leather book, offering to be exactly that steward — one that would govern his own servants and every other house's too, in a common tongue.

The wise houses took the offer's shape and refused its trap. One office must not mean one rule — a steward who governs the scullery-boy and the strong-room keeper alike either shackles the boy or loses the plate, so they tiered the rein to each hand's risk. The book must be your own — a steward you rent, who keeps the only ledger and whom you cannot dismiss, is a new master in a steward's coat. And even the steward's quill can be poisoned — a single poisoned page slipped into the morning's papers turned a neighbour's most trusted clerk into a stranger's errand-boy, so they brought the office's own tools under the same watch as the servants.

The question it forces: Who keeps the single book over all our agents — the one that could say, this hour, who is working, what each may touch, what each costs, and which one we could dismiss by nightfall? Is that office our own, or a rented book we could never send away? Do we rule the scullery-boy and the strong-room keeper by one blanket rule? And have we checked that the steward's own quill — the coding tools and gateways the office runs on — cannot be poisoned by a stranger's page?

3 · Technical — From the Livery to the Steward

A name you cannot query, scope, observe, cost and revoke from one place is a label, not a control — so as agents scale into the hundreds, control migrates from "does every agent have an identity?" to "is there one governed control plane over every agent — ours, tiered to risk, and hardened at its tools?" The event: the industry converged on the same shape the radar has described — identity, gateway, tool-governance, observability, cost, revocation — and Salesforce packaged it as infrastructure across its own and third-party agents. The reckoning: uniform governance is the root cause of failure (Gartner); a rented, un-exitable control plane is a new master; and GitSpawn shows a control plane that governs agents but not their tools has left its widest door unlatched. The answer: one control plane over every agent, kept portable and yours; tiered by autonomy and blast radius; with coding agents, gateways and MCP connections inside the same patch-and-containment regime — and stood up now, because the platform office is not staffed until 2027.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Stand up one governed office — a control plane over every agent, and make it yours. Consolidate agent governance into one control plane that discovers, registers, identifies, scopes, observes, costs and revokes every agent — yours and every vendor's — and keep the governing layer portable and vendor-neutral, so no platform becomes the sole keeper of your roll. Report the share of agents governed through one control plane you own.

2. Do not rule every hand the same — tier governance to risk. Classify every agent by autonomy and blast radius, separate its authority to act from the scope it may reach, and set the rein accordingly rather than one blanket policy (Gartner: 40% will demote/decommission autonomous agents by 2027). Report the share on risk-tiered policy — and the count still on a single blanket rule.

3. Guard the steward's own quill — govern the tools, not just the agents. Bring coding agents, gateways and MCP tool connections inside the same identity, patch and containment regime; verify your coding agents against the GitSpawn class (patch to fixed versions; sanitise Git config on background context calls); and stand up your own governed layer now, since the platform office is not staffed until 2027. Report the share of agent tools under managed patch-and-containment.
