### The reckoning (1) — one office must not mean one rule (the uniform-governance trap)

- Gartner, "Gartner Says Applying Uniform Governance Across AI Agents Will Lead to Enterprise AI Agent Failure" (26 May 2026) — https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-26-gartner-says-applying-uniform-governance-across-ai-agents-will-lead-to-enterprise-ai-agent-failure (via search summary — **[Source: Shiva Varma, Senior Director Analyst — "Enterprises are treating AI agent governance as binary, either locked down or fully trusted, and that is the root cause of failure"; failures arise when organizations fail to distinguish an agent's ability to act from the scope of access granted; by 2027, 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission autonomous AI agents due to governance gaps identified only after production incidents; two failure modes: over-restriction of simple agents and under-restriction of high-risk agents]**)

Claims sourced here (as reported): applying one uniform governance across all agents is the root cause of failure; the error is conflating an agent's ability to act with the scope of access it holds; by 2027, 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission autonomous agents over governance gaps surfaced only after a production incident. The Shiva Varma quote is relayed via this release. Carried here as the first caution: one office is not one rule.
