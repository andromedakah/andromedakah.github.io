The Tool-Chest

Friday, 27 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

The same week agents finally got real working papers — Microsoft Ignite 2026 put Entra Agent ID at the centre, making agents first-class, owned, least-privilege identities — Microsoft's own official agent-framework PyPI package was poisoned with a credential stealer. A badge proves who holds the tool. It does not prove the tool. Seal the hand, inspect the chest, shrink the blast radius.

1 · Executive Summary

For a year the radar worked on the hand — who the agent is and what it may touch. This week two developments landed on top of each other, and read together they open a seam none of those controls closes.

1. The good news — the servant got real working papers. At Microsoft Ignite 2026, Entra Agent ID made agents first-class identities — a named owner, least-privilege scopes, lifecycle and access reviews — across Google, Salesforce, Databricks, Snowflake, Anthropic and OpenAI, and local agents like GitHub Copilot CLI and Claude Code, with Agent 365 GA since 1 May 2026 (Microsoft, as reported). It is the discipline the radar urged, and urgent: non-human identities outnumber humans ~45:1 (up to 144:1 cloud-native; Palo Alto 109:1, up from 82:1).

2. The hard news — the papered hand's tool was poisoned upstream. On 17 November 2026 Microsoft's own official agent-framework PyPI package was compromised — versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 carried a credential stealer for Azure managed-identity, Azure DevOps and GitHub Actions tokens; rated critical (GHSA-764m-3ppc-5m73); live under an hour, quarantined ~31 minutes; no matching code in the GitHub repo → a publishing-pipeline compromise (BleepingComputer; CSO; Help Net Security; SecurityWeek, as reported). The agent that opened that chest was perfectly papered — which is exactly what made the theft efficient.

3. The answer — guard both the hand and the tool-chest. Issue the papers (owned, least-privilege, short-lived identity); vet the chest (pin and verify the agent's supply chain; scan lockfiles, CI caches and mirrors, which stay poisoned after a quarantine); and shrink the blast radius so a poisoned tool can't empty the house in 31 minutes.

Bottom line: this week delivered the biggest instalment of agent identity yet — Entra Agent ID making agents first-class citizens — and, in the same breath, proved identity alone is not the whole defence: Microsoft's own agent framework was poisoned at the publishing pipeline, and for half an hour a perfectly-papered agent carried Azure, DevOps and GitHub keys to a thief. A badge proves who holds the tool. Only provenance proves the tool.

2 · Allegory — The Tool-Chest

A great house had spent a careful year learning to know its servants — named, passported, counted, bonded, funded. This week it finished the work: it struck real working papers — a genuine seal, one per servant, naming the servant and the master who vouched for it, listing the exact doors it might open, stamped with a review date. Even hired servants from the trading companies carried the house's seal. The steward surveyed the hall of sealed servants and was, for once, pleased. This was the thing he had argued for all year. This was good.

But a seal is fixed to a hand — and a hand must still reach for a tool. The house bought its tools, as everyone did, from the most famous toolmaker in the land, whose mark on a chest was taken as proof. A fresh chest arrived, and the house let the sealed servants reach in. What it could not see was that two tools had a thief's false bottom built in — far upstream, at the workshop, in the dark between the maker's bench and the cart. The chest looked exactly like every honest chest, because in every way the house knew how to check, it was the maker's chest.

For a bare half-hour, each time a servant lifted one, the false bottom opened and that beautifully-sealed, least-privileged hand carried the house's master-keys out the back door into the thief's sack. The seal was perfect, so the house never doubted the tool. Then the market-warden caught the scent and seized the two tools — but half an hour, in a house of forty-five servants per man, is many reaches, and the keys did not come back; and the warden could clear only the market, not the chests already carried inside, the tools already copied into the cellars and private workshops.

The three rules the wise houses wrote: issue the papers and keep them mean (a sealed hand on a short leash can carry out only a little); inspect the chest, not just the famous mark (record every honest tool, demand provenance, keep your own warden at your door); and assume a poisoned tool, and make the loss small (short-lived keys, one-door keys, no master-key in any working hand). A badge proves who holds the tool. It does not prove the tool.

3 · Technical — From the Passport to the Tool-Chest

Identity governs the hand; provenance governs the tool; and a perfectly-governed hand holding a poisoned tool is the most efficient thief a house can own, because every control you built tells you to trust it. The good news: agent identity became first-class (Entra Agent ID — named owner, least-privilege scopes, lifecycle, access reviews, across vendors and local agents), and it had to — NHIs outnumber humans ~45:1, up to 144:1 cloud-native; least privilege here is also blast-radius control. The hard news: the supply chain is inside the agent — the agent-framework releases ran a credential stealer with the agent's identity, so the better-credentialed the agent, the richer the theft; and a registry quarantine is not remediation (caches, mirrors and installs persist; exfiltrated credentials must be rotated). The answer: run identity and provenance as one discipline — pin dependencies to verified hashes, require signed provenance/SBOM, scan your own caches and mirrors, and pre-shrink the blast radius with short-TTL, single-purpose credentials and detection tuned to the ~30-minute window.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Issue the papers. The identity primitives are now first-class (Entra Agent ID), and at ~45:1-to-144:1 non-human-to-human you cannot run agents on shared or static keys. Enrol your highest-privilege production agents into an owned, named, least-privilege identity with short-lived credentials and a review date, and purge standing master secrets. Report the share of production agents with an owned, least-privilege, short-lived identity.

2. Vet the tool-chest. A trusted name is not proof, and a quarantine clears the store, not your caches. Pin every agent framework and dependency to a verified hash, require signed provenance and an approved-versions list, generate an SBOM, and scan your own lockfiles, CI caches and mirrors for the known-bad agent-framework versions (and rotate any credential a build could have touched). Report the share of agent dependencies that are hash-pinned and provenance-verified.

3. Shrink the blast radius. A poisoned dependency will come again; the variable you control is how much a sealed hand can carry out in the half-hour before anyone notices. Run a "poisoned dependency" tabletop on one critical agent: map which secrets its identity can reach, confirm they are short-TTL and single-purpose, and verify detection fires inside ~30 minutes. Report the measured 31-minute blast radius and a dated plan to shrink it.
