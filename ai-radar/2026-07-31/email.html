The Last Mile

Friday, 31 July 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

The month's countdown is now in hours: EU AI Act Article 50 transparency and the Commission's GPAI enforcement powers switch on Sunday, 2 August — two days out (€15M or 3% of turnover). And the research that landed this week names what that audit will find. On 29 July ChatSee published a study of 10,000+ enterprise AI failure events: hallucination is now under 10% of failures; the dominant failure family is resolution and escalation breakdowns (31.1%); execution and action failures are up 62%. As AI moves from answering to acting, the risk migrates from wrong words to unfinished work. The model's answer is a cheap, solved commodity; the last mile — the completed, escalated, resolved, disclosed action — is where value, the 88% pilot graveyard, and Sunday's auditor all live. Can we prove our agents took the right action, escalated at the right moment, and closed the loop — or is our last mile an ungoverned gap where the answer was right and the work never happened?

1 · Executive Summary

For a month one thesis: the model commoditizes; own and govern the layer around it. This week two facts snapped it into focus. First, the model's answer is now cheap, fast and solved: Opus 5 #1 at $5/$25, Sonnet 5 at $2/$10 through 31 Aug (then $3/$15), GPT-5.6 "Sol" at ~750 tok/s on Cerebras, Kimi K3 open weights. Second, on 29 July ChatSee published a study of 10,000+ enterprise AI failure events: hallucination is under 10% of failures; the largest family is resolution/escalation breakdowns at 31.1%, and execution/action failures are up 62%. CEO Sekhar Sarukkai: "The enterprise AI question is no longer only whether a model can answer correctly, but whether the AI system can retrieve the right context, take the right action, escalate at the right time, and bring work to resolution." When AI only answered, a wrong word was the risk; when AI acts, the risk is a wrong action, a missed escalation, an unresolved case — the last mile from answer to completed, governed outcome. The market's data is the same gap: MIT 95% no measurable impact, IDC 88% of PoCs never scale, Gartner 40%+ cancelled by 2027. And the last mile is what becomes enforceable in two days: Sunday 2 August, Article 50 transparency + GPAI enforcement (€15M or 3%); high-risk Annex III slipped to 2 Dec 2027. BCG calls it a "wake-up call" — and over half of firms have no AI inventory.

1. The answer is solved and nearly free — the last mile is where value and risk now live. A correct output is a commodity you rent by the token; what's scarce is the handoff from a good answer to a completed, escalated, resolved, disclosed action. ChatSee's data says that handoff, not hallucination, is where enterprise AI breaks.

2. The failure mode inverted: under 10% hallucinations, 31.1% escalation breakdowns, +62% execution failures. "The model was wrong" gives way to "the model was right and the work still didn't happen" — the customer who reports fraud, gets a compliant reply, and is never escalated. You don't fix that with a smarter model; you govern the last mile.

3. Sunday's audit is a last-mile audit — and most firms can't produce the record. Article 50 + GPAI land 2 August (€15M or 3%); the evidence is proof of disclosure and of what agents did and escalated — yet over half of firms have no inventory. Govern the last mile, or inherit both the unfinished work and the fine.

Bottom line: the model has won the argument nobody is having — it can answer. The decisive stretch is the last mile: turning a correct answer into a completed action, escalated to a human at the right moment, brought to resolution, and disclosed on the record. Own and govern the last mile, or keep shipping right answers that never become finished work.

2 · Allegory — The Last Mile

Ask any logistics operator where the money and the misery are and they'll say the same: not the ocean, not the rail, not the thousand-mile line-haul — the last mile. A container crosses the Pacific for pennies a kilo, all of it automated and reliable to four-nines. Then the parcel has to get from the local depot to your actual door — and that stretch, the shortest of the journey, eats most of the cost and nearly all of the failure. It's where the package that traveled flawlessly for a week is left at the wrong address, marked "delivered" while it sits in a lobby, handed to someone who never signed. The line-haul is a solved commodity; the last mile is where the whole journey is kept or lost.

The line-haul of AI is a solved, near-free commodity — a #1 model for five dollars, an open-weight colossus, an engine streaming 750 tokens a second. And ChatSee's 10,000 failures are the doorstep: fewer than one in ten is a wrong parcel (a hallucination); 31.1% are the parcel that arrived and was never handed over (the escalation that didn't happen); execution failures — the wrong door, the unsigned delivery — are up 62%. Sarukkai's banking customer is the parcel marked "delivered" in the lobby: she reported fraud, got a flawless compliant reply, and was never escalated for human review. The answer traveled a thousand miles perfectly and failed in the last hundred yards — and no faster plane fixes a doorstep problem.

The question it forces: For every agent we run, can we show it did more than answer — took the right action, escalated to a human at the right moment, brought the work to resolution, and disclosed that a person was dealing with an AI? Where is the last-mile record — the proof of delivery — and could we produce it on Sunday, 2 August? Have we built and governed the last mile, or are we shipping perfect answers into an ungoverned final stretch where the work quietly never gets done?

3 · Technical — Own the Last Mile, Not Just the Answer

The line-haul (commodity, solved): Opus 5 #1 at $5/$25; Sonnet 5 at $2/$10 through 31 Aug (then $3/$15); GPT-5.6 "Sol" at ~750 tok/s on Cerebras (10–15× typical); Kimi K3 open weights; MCP 2026-07-28 connector final. Answering correctly is table stakes. The doorstep (where the failure moved): ChatSee (10,000+ failures) — hallucination <10%; resolution/escalation breakdowns 31.1% (the parcel never handed over); execution/action failures +62% (the wrong door). Atop the production gap: MIT 95% no measurable impact, IDC 88% of PoCs never scale, Gartner 40%+ cancelled by 2027. The last mile (the layer to own): the four crafts — COMPLETE (define & measure "resolved"), ESCALATE (human handoff, tested/logged), BOUND (what actions, approval-gated), PROVE (per-action log + Article 50 disclosure).

On legacy: same seam — own what doesn't commoditize (6 Jul the meter … 29 Jul the pilot plant, 30 Jul the commonplace book). Yesterday named the memory that compounds; today names the stretch where a good answer becomes finished work — the last mile. The danger is a brilliant cheap model bolted onto a system-of-record, measured on answer quality, never instrumented for completion, escalation or resolution — so it answers beautifully and the work quietly stalls. The retrofit: define "resolved," make escalation a tested/logged step, bound the actions, record every one plus the Article 50 disclosure — before scale-up.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Measure the outcome, not the answer. For your top agents, stop reporting model accuracy and latency alone; measure completion, escalation and resolution — what share of cases actually finished, how often a human handoff fired when it should have, how many "answered" tickets were never resolved. ChatSee's data exists because the answer is solved and the doorstep isn't.

2. Govern the last mile — complete, escalate, bound, prove — your 2 August evidence. Define "resolved" per workflow and instrument it; make human escalation tested and logged; bound the actions and gate the consequential ones; record every action plus the Article 50 disclosure. That per-action record is what Sunday's transparency-and-GPAI enforcement asks for.

3. Own the last mile as a model-neutral asset — rent the answer, keep the delivery. Stand up completion, escalation and audit as infrastructure you own, so swapping Opus 5 for Kimi K3 for GPT-5.6 Sol changes the line-haul, not the doorstep. The moat is proof your agents took the right action, escalated at the right moment, and resolved the work.
