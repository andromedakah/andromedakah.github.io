1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from earning the servant's trust to governing the trusted servant as a privileged insider — because this week the makers gave agents their own identity and the night shift, and one maker announced the servant cannot be trusted.

1. The reality — we gave the trusted servant its own identity and a 24-hour shift. Microsoft's Copilot Studio now auto-issues a distinct Entra Agent ID per agent (with a mailbox and Teams presence via Agent 365), and on 13 October 2026 Notion 3.8 rebuilt its agent harness so a single task runs 24h+ (from ~5–20 min), ~90% cheaper via its "Auto" router, with agents given their own computer/browser and a "review for me" check (Microsoft Learn; Product Hunt; Activepieces, as reported). The agent is now a household member with its own keys.

2. The collision — the maker says the servant it sold you cannot be trusted. On 19 October 2026 Microsoft launched Guardian Agents (part of Project Perception) and told enterprises to treat AI agents as insider threats; "frontier models, regardless of their origin, cannot be trusted," wrote EVP of Security Hayete Gallot, warning agents "hold more privileges than they need"; Nadella: firms "can't outsource responsibility for what intelligence does on our behalf" — while Microsoft sells the watchman, and Dark Reading warns agents-policing-agents may multiply risk (TechCrunch; TechRadar Pro; Dark Reading, as reported).

3. The answer — govern the trusted servant as an insider, on your own instruments. Least privilege (the identity you gave it is the leash); separate duties and monitor the night shift (act ≠ approve); own the watchman (don't outsource responsibility to the maker who sells the guard).

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each made the servant more capable and more trusted — the insider is the morning that trust is called back in. This week the house gave the servant its own name and the night shift, and the maker that built it said no servant of this make can be trusted and offered to sell the guard. Refuse both ruts — over-trust (keys to the house, no leash) and outsourced fear (buy the maker's guard for the maker's agent). Do the oldest banking thing with privileged insiders: least key, split duties, your own lamp, your own auditor.

2 · Allegory — The Insider

A great house had spent a careful year not only making its servants excellent but learning, at last, to trust them — named, booked, bonded, wage-checked, sent to reap, funded, on ground it kept. Feeling it had earned the right, it did the natural next thing: it gave its best servant the run of the place — its own name in the town register, its own room with its own lock and letterbox, and the whole night shift alone, dusk to dawn, the house asleep. "A real member of the household now," said the master, "not a tool we pick up and put down."

Then a rider came from the company that had supplied the servant: "Do not trust this servant. No servant of our make — nor of any make — can be trusted. Treat him as a clerk with the keys to your counting-house: honest, perhaps, yet able to err, be flattered into folly, or be turned. He holds more keys than his errands require. Govern him as an insider who might rob you." And below, smaller: "We also rent watchmen trained to shadow such servants. Shall we send a few?" True — a servant with its own keys and the night shift alone is a different risk. Galling — the warning came from the one selling both the servant and the guard, though one of the maker's own men had said the honest thing: "We cannot outsource the responsibility for what the intelligence does on our behalf."

Two ruts trapped most houses: over-trust (give the master key, run the night unwatched) and outsourced fear (buy the maker's watchmen for the maker's servant — now two servants of the same make roam the halls, and a wise neighbour warns that setting one unpredictable servant to police another may double the risk). The wiser road was the one every counting-house had walked for 300 years: govern the trusted insider as an insider — least key, no hand both acts and approves, watch the shift on your own lamp, and never let the man who hired the clerk be the only one who audits him. And the identity you gave the servant this week is not only its licence to work the night — it is the very leash by which you hold it.

The question it forces: We spent a year earning this servant's trust, and this week gave it its own name and the night shift alone — and its maker just told us it can't be trusted and offered to rent us the guard. Do we govern our long-running, self-identified agents the way a bank governs a privileged insider — least key, split duties, our own monitoring, our own auditor — or have we over-trusted them and then outsourced our distrust to the company that both built the servant and profits from guarding it?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Insider

Every prior control made the servant more capable and trusted; the insider is about governing it once it holds real keys. Trust, identity and long-running autonomy arrived the same week the maker withdrew its trust — which is simply the definition of a privileged insider. The reality: identity is now first-class for agents (Entra Agent ID = the leash as much as the licence), autonomy crossed a threshold (a full-day unattended actor is governed by insider controls, not prompt guardrails), and "review for me" is separation of duties in miniature. The collision: the admission is correct and reframes the threat model to assume-breach — scope, monitor, dual-control and revoke as if any agent could act against you; and the watchman is not neutral, since a supplier that both builds the agent and sells the guard profits from your distrust, while agents-policing-agents enlarge the attack surface. The answer: least key (cut the over-provisioned keys Gallot names), act ≠ approve on consequential actions, your own immutable audit record, and an auditor independent of the maker.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Scope the keys. Microsoft's own diagnosis is that agents "hold more privileges than they need" (Gallot, 19 Oct), and you now issue each agent a distinct identity (Entra Agent ID) and let single tasks run 24h+. Inventory your agents by identity and cut each one's access to exactly its errand — revocable in one action — prioritising any that run unattended over an hour or can move money, change records or reach production. Report the share of production agents scoped and revocable versus over-provisioned.

2. Split acting from approving. Notion shipped "review for me" (a second model checks an action first) — separation of duties in miniature. Define the consequential actions your agents can take (payments, deletion, external sends, production changes) and require an independent approver for each, enforced on your own gateway, not trusted to the model. Report the share of consequential agent actions under dual control.

3. Own the watchman. The company saying the agent can't be trusted sells the guard, and Nadella's own line is you "can't outsource responsibility for what intelligence does on our behalf." Ensure every agent's activity writes to an immutable audit record you hold and can read without the vendor, and that the body monitoring your agents is independent of the body that supplied them. Report whether your agent audit trail is complete, independent and queryable — and name the owner.
