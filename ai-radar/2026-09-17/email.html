The Surety

Thursday, 17 September 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

Yesterday you built one governed office over every agent and kept its book your own. This morning the law puts the harder question on the table — when the reckoning comes, whose name stands surety for what your agents do? The EU AI Act's high-risk obligations and penalty regime are now live (the main body applied 2 August 2026), and they bind the deployer, not only the provider, by name. The move: rent the office where it serves, but keep the surety your own — a named internal owner, a record you hold, a contract that carries the liability without discharging your duty.

1 · Executive Summary

The discipline moves from who keeps the book to whose name is on the bond for what the book records — and here the market and the law pull opposite ways: the vendors sell a ready-made office and the comfort of delegating governance to it, while the law has just made governance a personal, non-delegable bond.

1. The reality — the accountability regime is live. The EU AI Act's central high-risk obligations applied from 2 August 2026 (into force 1 Aug 2024; prohibited practices from 2 Feb 2025; GPAI-model and governance/penalty machinery from 2 Aug 2025; embedded high-risk products from Aug 2027). A high-risk deployer owes risk management, automatic logging and record-keeping (Article 12), human oversight, data governance and accuracy/robustness — as duties distinct from the provider's — with fines up to €35M/7% (prohibited), €15M/3% (high-risk and other obligations) and €7.5M/1% (incorrect info to a regulator) (EU AI Act, as reported).

2. The collision — the office is for sale, the bond is not. At Dreamforce 2026 Salesforce formalised agent governance into one platform (Trusted Enterprise AI Harness; AI Control Plane; GA ~early 2027). But a provider's conformity does not discharge a deployer's duties, and a deployer who materially modifies or re-brands a system can become a provider in law. Meanwhile agents scale toward 40% of enterprise apps by 2026, and Gartner warns uniform governance is the root cause of failure, with 40% of enterprises set to demote or decommission autonomous agents by 2027 (Salesforce; Gartner, as reported).

3. The answer — name the surety, hold the evidence, contract the liability. Give every high-risk system a named accountable owner of your own; keep the logs, risk files and oversight records yours, portable and producible on demand; and make vendor contracts carry liability, audit and exit back-to-back — while treating a supplier's certificate as necessary, never sufficient.

Bottom line: the steward was about who keeps the single book; the surety is about whose name is on the bond when the book is called for. A house that rents a fine office and assumes the accountability went with it has bought someone to point at, not safety — and the law does not accept the gesture. Rent the office where it serves; keep the surety your own.

2 · Allegory — The Surety

A great house that had named every hand and hired a fine steward to keep one book — and was weighing the ready-made office the grandest house in the valley had come to sell — was visited one grey morning by a rider in the crown's colours. He carried not an offer but a law: the conduct of every house is now the master's personal bond; his own name stands surety for what his servants do, and when the assize sits the book must be laid open, whole and true, on pain of a share of the whole estate's yearly income.

The master sent for the steward and for the man who had sold him the office, and told them the bond was theirs to carry between them. But the steward bowed and said the bond was not his — he was a servant, and it was the master's name the crown had written. And the great house spread its hands: it could sell the office, it said, but not the surety. The master understood, too late, that he had bought someone to keep his book and someone to point to, and no one to answer in his place.

So the wise houses learned three things. Name a surety of your own blood — a person of your household, not the hired steward or the great house, over every grave act. Keep the evidence in your own hand — your own true copy of the book, producible within the week, whichever office you rent. Make the contract carry what a contract can, and no more — bind the great house back-to-back, but never mistake its certificate for your own seal on the bond.

The question it forces: When the assize sits, whose name is on the bond? Is there a hand of our own household over every high-risk system, or have we assumed the platform we bought carries the accountability it never could? Could that person lay our own record open this week without a vendor's leave? And have we bound our suppliers back-to-back without mistaking their certificate for our own bond?

3 · Technical — From the Steward to the Surety

Accountability does not travel with a purchase order — so once the law attaches a personal, non-delegable liability to what your agents do, control migrates from "do we have a governed control plane?" to "is there a named owner of our own who can produce the whole record on demand, and does the liability stay ours whichever office we rent?" The reality: a high-risk deployer owes risk management, Article 12 logging, human oversight, data governance and accuracy/robustness — duties distinct from the provider's, and evidentiary, so producing them is an engineering property of your estate. The collision: the platforms sell the office (Salesforce's Trusted Enterprise AI Harness; AI Control Plane), but a provider's conformity does not discharge a deployer's duty, agents scale toward 40% of apps by 2026, uniform governance is the root cause of failure (Gartner), and 60% still cannot terminate a misbehaving agent (Kiteworks) — so the very record the law demands is what most cannot yet produce. The answer: name a surety of your own, hold the evidence producible and portable, and contract the liability back-to-back while treating a certificate as necessary, never sufficient.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Name a surety of your own — a person, not a platform — for every high-risk system. Assign a single accountable owner inside your own house to every high-risk AI system and material agent; make "who answers for this?" a field with a named person in it. Report the share of high-risk systems with a named, internal accountable owner — versus those where accountability is assumed to sit with a vendor.

2. Hold the evidence yourself and prove you can produce it. Run a fire drill: pick two high-risk systems and require the owner to produce the complete record (Article 12 logs, risk file, oversight design) within a week, in a form you hold yourself and could export the day you change vendors. Report the share that passed — and a target to raise it.

3. Make the contract carry the liability back-to-back — never mistake a certificate for your bond. Put audit rights, evidence access, indemnity and record-portability/exit into every AI vendor contract; treat a certificate as necessary but never sufficient; price the 3%-of-turnover exposure of a record you cannot produce. Report the share of AI vendor contracts carrying back-to-back liability, audit and exit.
