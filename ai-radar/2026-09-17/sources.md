## The story — a governed office is not a name on the bond: the law now makes the deployer personally answerable, so name a surety of your own, hold your own producible record, and contract the liability back-to-back without discharging your duty

### The reality — the accountability regime is live (the datable hook)

- EU AI Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj (via search summary / prior-verified radar context — **[Source: entered into force 1 August 2024; application staggered "6 to 36 months"; prohibited practices and AI-literacy duties from 2 February 2025; general-purpose-AI-model obligations and the governance/penalty machinery from 2 August 2025; the broad body of high-risk-system obligations from 2 August 2026; AI as a safety component of, or itself, a regulated product (Annex I) from 2 August 2027]**)
- European Commission, "AI Act" (implementation timeline and application dates) — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai (via search summary — application-date timeline; European AI Office role for general-purpose AI)
- "The 2026 in artificial intelligence" / "General-Purpose AI Code of Practice" / "European Artificial Intelligence Office" (encyclopaedic background surfaced in search) — corroborating the 1 Aug 2024 entry into force, the 2 Aug 2025 GPAI application date, and the GPAI Code of Practice released 10 July 2025

Claims sourced here (as reported): the EU AI Act's central high-risk-system obligations applied from 2 August 2026 — for a high-risk system the deployer owes a running risk-management process, automatic logging and record-keeping (Article 12), effective human oversight (Article 14), data governance and accuracy/robustness (Articles 10, 15) — as duties distinct from and in addition to the provider's; AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) follows in August 2027. Carried here as "the accountability regime is now live," the datable hook for "the surety."

### The penalties — a missing or false record is a balance-sheet event

- EU AI Act, Article 99 (penalties) and Article 101 (fines for providers of general-purpose AI models) — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj (via search summary / prior-verified radar context — **[Source: up to €35,000,000 or 7% of total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year (whichever is higher) for prohibited practices; up to €15,000,000 or 3% for non-compliance with other obligations, including high-risk-system and deployer obligations; up to €7,500,000 or 1% for supplying incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to notified bodies or competent authorities; SME/start-up caps applied to the lower of the amount/percentage]**)

Claims sourced here (as reported): penalties up to €35M/7% (prohibited), €15M/3% (high-risk and other obligations) and €7.5M/1% (incorrect information to a regulator) of global annual turnover. Carried here as the reason the bond is now a balance-sheet matter.

### The non-delegability — provider vs deployer (the radar's reading of the Act)

- EU AI Act, Articles 3 (definitions of "provider" and "deployer"), 16 (provider obligations), 25 (responsibilities along the value chain; when a deployer becomes a provider), 26 (deployer obligations) — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj (via search summary / prior-verified radar context)

Claims sourced here (as reported, with the emphasis being the radar's framing): the Act binds the deployer as well as the provider; a provider's conformity does not discharge a deployer's duties; and a deployer that materially modifies a high-risk system, or places it on the market/puts it into service under its own name or trademark, can itself become a provider (Article 25). The argument that "you can rent the steward's office but not the surety" — that accountability is personal and cannot be outsourced to a platform vendor — is the radar's editorial synthesis of this provider/deployer architecture and the standing vendor-lock-in theme of prior editions ("The Steward," 16 Sep; "The Tide Table," 9 Sep). It is not a quotation from, or a claim about the stated intent of, any specific company.
