The Free Well

Wednesday, 4 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

The season's first hard data grades the commoditization thesis: intelligence got cheaper still (open-weight ~$0.13/M, 19% of tokens) — yet enterprises poured more of their coin than ever up one frontier hill (63% of spend, up from 35%). The free well runs in the square; why does the town still queue on the hill? Meter the well against the hill, draw a real test bucket, watch the concentration.

1 · Executive Summary

Six weeks ago the radar said the model was a cheap, swappable stove and told you to shop ruthlessly. This morning the market's own scoreboard grades that homework — and it cuts both ways: the water commoditized exactly as predicted, and almost no one captured the saving.

1. The well is real and cheap. Menlo Ventures' "2026: The State of Enterprise AI" (3 November 2026, ~400 U.S. enterprise decision-makers) reports the average open-weight token fell ~3.3× in price to about $0.13 per million and open-weight's share of enterprise tokens rose from 11% to 19% over the year to Q3 2026; Mistral Large 4 shipped 29 October (Menlo; FourWeekMBA, as reported). The cheap stove the radar predicted is now a public well.

2. The valley poured its coin up the hill anyway. The same report finds frontier models' share of enterprise AI spend rose from 35% to 63%, their token share from 18% to 42%, and frontier labs' share of deployment spend from 50% to 69% — while open-weight captured only ~4% of spend at 19% of tokens; mid-tier closed "workhorse" models collapsed from 29% to 13% of tokens, and one supplier reportedly holds ~55% of enterprise API share, ~6× Google's (Menlo; FourWeekMBA, as reported). The free well runs; the town queues on the hill.

3. The answer — meter, test, and watch. Meter the well against the hill per workload; draw a real test bucket (run open-weight on a live task and measure the gap against a 10–50× price spread); and watch the concentration, so two-thirds of a growing bill up one hill is a choice you can defend and reverse, not a default.

Bottom line: the radar called the commoditization right — the water is $0.13 and a fifth of all tokens — but the market's scoreboard shows the valley found the free well and kept its money flowing uphill. None of this means the hill is wrong: frontier quality genuinely earns its price on the work that matters. The danger is the unexamined climb — a cheap well no one carries a bucket from, a saving someone else banks while you pay full price up the hill. Pay up the hill for the work that earns it; drink from the well for the work that doesn't; and prove which is which.

2 · Allegory — The Free Well

A town had learned, over a hard year, to be careful about its water — every carrier named and scoped, one honest ledger, the mayor's name on the bond, and above all no marrying a single well: you rented your supply and switched when a cheaper source appeared. This season the bet came true. A free well opened in the public square — good water, cheap as coppers — and by autumn the town drew nearly a fifth of all its water from it. The old prophecy had come to pass.

But then the season's accounts came in, counting not the buckets but the coin — and for all the free water in the square, the town was sending more of its money than ever up the long road to the private waterworks on the hill. A year ago half the water-coin climbed that hill; now it was better than two-thirds, and rising. The free well, a fifth of the buckets, took barely a twentieth of the coin. The mid-priced middle had all but dried up, and of the silver that climbed, more than half flowed to a single waterworks whose pipes ran under half the houses.

Now hold two thoughts at once: the hill-water was genuinely better for the work that mattered, and a wise town should pay up the hill for the delicate tasks. The mistake was not the climb — it was climbing without ever asking which buckets needed the hill, pouring two-thirds of a growing bill up the road out of habit and the carriers' word, never once carrying a test bucket from the well to a real task. So the wise houses learned three things: meter the well against the hill (reserve the dear water for the work that earns it, send the ordinary drawing to the well); draw a real test bucket (measure the gap, because when the hill costs 10–50× the well, the hill must prove the gap is worth it); and watch the hill (two-thirds of your coin up one road, half of it through one works, is a dependency whether you named it or not).

The question it forces: The free well is real — a fifth of our tokens at a tenth the price. So why is two-thirds of our AI bill, and climbing, going up one hill — and can we show, task by task, which coins the hill genuinely earned and which we sent uphill from habit, having never carried a test bucket from the well?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Free Well

Every prior control governs what you run; the free well is a spending discipline. The price of intelligence fell for the whole industry, and most enterprises captured none of it — because a cheap option you admire but never route your work to is a saving someone else banks while you pay full price up the hill. The well: capable open-weight is now a genuine production option at ~$0.13/M (19% of tokens), but capturing it needs a routing layer you own, and "cheaper for the industry" is not "cheaper for you." The invoice: spend barbelled to cheapest and dearest and the middle died (mid-tier closed models 29%→13% of tokens); the frontier premium is often earned (production-grade, agentic work) but only sometimes — the waste is paying it for the ordinary drawing the well would serve. The answer: meter (route each task to the cheapest model that clears its quality bar), test (measure the open-weight-vs-frontier gap against a 10–50× spread), and watch (name and price the 63%/69% frontier spend and the ~55% single-lab API share, and keep a funded, exercised alternate).

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Meter the well against the hill. The water commoditized (open-weight ~$0.13/M, 19% of tokens) but the dividend landed in almost no one's bill (frontier took 63% of spend; open-weight ~4%). Inventory your top AI workloads by token volume and stakes, and for the high-volume, low-stakes tier route traffic to a capable open-weight model behind an abstraction you own — reserving the frontier for the high-value, agentic work that earns it. Report the share of your token volume (and bill) matched to need versus sent to the frontier by default.

2. Draw a real test bucket. The spread between well and hill is 10–50×, and the only honest way to justify the premium is to measure it. Pick one live workload and run a head-to-head — a capable open-weight model (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, Mistral Large 4) against your frontier incumbent, scored on your own evals, under real load. Report the measured quality gap and cost delta, and let that evidence — not the vendor's word — set the routing policy.

3. Watch the concentration. Two-thirds of enterprise spend and 69% of deployment spend now go to the frontier, and ~55% of the enterprise API reportedly runs through one lab. Map your AI spend by provider, identify your single largest concentration, and stand up a funded, exercised path to a second source for at least one critical workflow — so the climb stays a choice you can defend and reverse. Report the share of critical AI spend exposed to a single provider and your tested time-to-switch.
