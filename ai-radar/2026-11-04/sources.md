### Headline figures (year to Q3 2026, as reported)
- Frontier models' share of enterprise AI **spend**: 35% → 63%.
- Frontier models' share of **tokens**: 18% → 42%.
- Open-weight **token** share: 11% → 19%; only **~4% of spend**; average open-weight token price fell **~3.3×** to **~$0.13/M**.
- Smaller closed "workhorse" models: **29% → 13%** of tokens (the middle hollowed out).
- Frontier labs' share of **deployment spend**: 50% → 69%.
- One supplier reportedly **~55% of enterprise API share, ~6× Google's**; only ~10% of builders post-train open models to cut cost.
- Survey base: ~400 U.S. enterprise decision-makers.

### Regulatory figures (as reported)
- GPAI enforcement powers live since **2 August 2026**; fines up to **3% of worldwide turnover or €15M**, centralized in the Commission's **AI Office** (Article 88 / Chapter V).
- **Digital Omnibus** (May 2026) pushed most standalone high-risk (Annex III) obligations to **2 December 2027** but left GPAI enforcement untouched.

## Editorial framing (the radar's own)
The "free well" allegory — intelligence commoditized at the token layer (a cheap public well)
while enterprise spend concentrated harder on the frontier (the hill), so the commoditization
dividend reaches only the houses that route their work to the cheap option, and a house must
**meter the well against the hill**, **draw a real test bucket**, and **watch the
concentration** — is expressly the radar's reading, not a sourced claim about any specific
company beyond the reported Menlo Ventures, Mistral and regulatory figures. Continues the
"commoditization" thread from the 22 September edition ("The Foundation").
