The Watch

Saturday, 26 September 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

You perfected the one servant and owned the ground it stands on. This morning the number itself breaks the frame: you no longer run a servant but a legion. Anthropic disclosed ~30,000 internal agents watched inline, act by act; Dataiku shipped the cross-platform instrument to run one. A master watches a servant by eye. Nobody watches a legion by eye. Post the watch, keep the muster, respect the arithmetic.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from perfecting one servant to the number of servants itself — because one lab just showed what running a legion looks like, and one vendor started selling the instrument every other house now needs.

1. The proof of scale — a legion, watched inline. On 17 September 2026 Anthropic published "Measurements for understanding the pace of AI development inside frontier labs," disclosing ~30,000 agents doing research and engineering at any one time (August); 100% of actions pass an online monitor before execution, with an offline monitor reading all of it (~100,000 records/week flagged, ~50 escalated to human review); and across >1 billion August decisions it blocked 0.002% — about 1 in 47,000 (Anthropic, as reported). Claude now leads 26% of its AI R&D. Read the number right: 1-in-47,000 across a billion acts is ~20,000 interventions a month.

2. The market instrument — cross-platform oversight as a product. On 24 September 2026 Dataiku launched "Agent Management," which inventories agents across AWS Bedrock, Databricks, Google Vertex, Microsoft Copilot Studio/Azure Foundry, Salesforce Agentforce, Snowflake Cortex and custom (OpenTelemetry) stacks, tiers them by risk, and keeps self-rerunning certification tests for the highest-risk tier (GA October). Help Net Security's headline: it "reveals unmonitored agents" (Dataiku; SiliconANGLE, as reported).

3. The answer — post the watch, keep the muster, respect the arithmetic. Inline oversight on every high-risk act; one cross-platform, risk-tiered roll of the whole workforce with self-current evidence; and capacity sized to rate × volume — because 31% of firms already run agents in production (47% in banking/insurance), 88% reported an agent incident last year, and Gartner sees 15% of GenAI apps hitting a major incident yearly by 2029 (S&P Global; Gartner, as reported).

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each governed a countable staff. The watch is what you post when the staff stops being countable. Anthropic showed a legion can be watched act-by-act and its faults caught by the tens of thousands; Dataiku made the roll buyable. Post the watch, keep the muster, respect the arithmetic — because rare times a legion is frequent.

2 · Allegory — The Watch

A great house had spent a careful year making its one trusted servant excellent — named, booked, bonded, waged, sent to reap, funded with a scoped purse, and at last set on ground the house owned. The master could watch him with his own two eyes. Then, over one season, the house hired more — not ten, but thirty thousand hands at work at once, in wings the master never entered — and his old habit of watching by eye simply broke in his hands. A master can watch one servant, or ten. He cannot watch thirty thousand, and no amount of care makes him able to.

A larger neighbour had crossed that line and left a map. It did not watch its legion by eye; it posted a watch — tireless overseers at the threshold of every act, letting no hand finish a deed before a watcher had glanced at it, all in a breath, with a second set reading everything back afterward. Its legion had done more than a thousand million things in a month; the watch had stopped one in forty-seven thousand. The small master scoffed — until the neighbour finished the sum: "one in forty-seven thousand of a thousand million is twenty thousand stayed hands in a month. Call it rare if you like. We call it Tuesday." At the scale of a legion, "rare" and "never" are strangers.

Worse, when the master tried to write down who worked for him, he could not — each wing had hired through a different agent and kept its own scrappy list, and no one held the whole. A travelling merchant arrived with a promise: one muster roll of every hand in every wing and every borrowed platform, ranked by danger, with tests re-run on a schedule — and, its crier said plainly, its chief use was to reveal the hands already at work that no one was watching at all. So the wise houses learned three things: post a standing watch on every act before the act; keep one muster roll of the whole workforce; and respect the arithmetic — budget for the twenty thousand stayed hands, because a house that plans for perfection at legion scale is overrun the first busy month.

The question it forces: We have perfected the one servant and owned the ground. But we no longer run one — how many agents work under our roof, on how many platforms, and could we produce the roll this afternoon? Is there a tireless watch on every act before it acts, or only a quarterly sample and a hope? And have we done the sum — our failure rate times our volume — and staffed the watchers and human judges to carry it?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Watch

A legion of agents is not a bigger version of one agent; it is a different governance object, where a negligible per-action risk becomes a large standing risk purely through multiplication, and where oversight that isn't inline and a roll that isn't complete simply fail to see the harm. The proof: oversight must be inline and per-action (a monitor that gates before execution prevents the deed; a log reviewed later does not), carried in two tiers (a fast online gate plus an offline reader feeding human escalation), with the blocking rate treated as a staffing input — 1-in-47,000 across a billion acts sizes your intervention and review capacity. The instrument: the roll must span platforms because your agents do (the ungoverned agent is the one on a platform your central team doesn't watch), and its certification evidence must re-run itself so it is current when — not after — an auditor asks. The answer: post the watch (inline, two-tier), keep the muster (one cross-platform, risk-tiered roll with self-current evidence), and respect the arithmetic (size capacity to rate × volume).

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Keep the muster. The dangerous agent is the one already running that no one is watching. Produce a single inventory of every agent across every platform (Bedrock, Vertex, Copilot Studio, Agentforce, Snowflake Cortex, custom/OpenTelemetry), tag each by what it can touch, and rank by risk. Report how many agents you run and how many are in the highest-risk tier with a current certification on file.

2. Post the watch. Anthropic gates 100% of ~30,000 agents' actions before they run, then reads everything offline and escalates the hardest cases to humans. Move your highest-risk agents from sampled log review to an inline preventive gate on every action, backed by an offline reader feeding classifier-triaged human escalation. Report the share of high-risk actions checked inline before execution.

3. Respect the arithmetic. 0.002% of a billion acts is ~20,000 interventions/month; "rare" is never "never" at legion scale. Compute your own error/intervention rate times your monthly agent-action volume and staff automated interventions and human reviewers to carry it. Report the expected monthly count of interventions at your scale and whether your capacity matches it.
