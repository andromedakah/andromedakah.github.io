The Badge

Monday, 23 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

The agents became staff. At Microsoft Ignite (17 Nov), Nadella said "we already have 50 million agents in Agent 365" and unveiled a control plane that badges every agent — including local agents (Claude Code, GitHub Copilot CLI) and third-party agents (Google, Salesforce, OpenAI). It lands because non-human identities now outnumber humans ~144:1 while only 21% of orgs govern them. You can name your forty. Can you name the six thousand?

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from owning the ground to counting the hands standing on it — because the agents are no longer a handful you can name; they are a workforce, and this week a vendor put a number on it and offered the enterprise a front desk.

1. The news — the agents became staff, and someone counted them. At Microsoft Ignite 2026 (17 November), Nadella said "we already have 50 million agents in Agent 365" and pitched Agent 365 as the control plane for every agent an org runs. Entra Agent ID now issues managed identities to discovered local agents (GitHub Copilot CLI, Claude Code) and third-party agents (Google, Salesforce, Databricks, Snowflake, Anthropic, OpenAI) via secretless workload-identity federation; agents get an email, a Teams presence and an org-chart seat; "Agentic Users" join a team under their own identity with an accountable owner (Microsoft; AV Network; Cloud Wars; Windows Forum, as reported). The control plane is a badge office.

2. The reason — the arithmetic turned while you weren't counting. Non-human identities now outnumber human ones by a reported ~144:1 in the average enterprise (up from ~92:1 a year earlier); they outnumber humans in 83% of organizations but only 21% have governance controls; 91% use AI agents while only ~10% have an NHI-identity strategy (JumpCloud Q3 2026; Cloud Security Alliance, as reported). This is the bill for the cheap stove: Claude Fable 5.2 cut to $8/$40 on 9 November (a ~20% drop from $10/$50), GPT-6.2 GA on 17 November — cheap, swappable intelligence produces thousands of agents, not one careful one.

3. The answer — one badge office, a named master, revoke-on-exit. Issue every agent's identity from a single authority you control (first-party, third-party and local); bind each to an accountable human owner and a scoped mandate; and automate joiner-mover-leaver so the badge is revoked on exit — because the shadow agent and the dead agent's badge, not the agent you governed, are where the breach walks in.

Bottom line: the livery named your own hands; the census counted them — all of it assumed a workforce small enough to picture. The badge is for the morning you cannot. A vendor says it manages fifty million agents, and non-human identities now outnumber your humans better than a hundred to one while only one org in five governs them. Don't out-discipline the flood by hand: stand up one badge office, put a named master on every badge, and run joiner-mover-leaver so a badge is taken back the hour the work ends.

2 · Allegory — The Badge

A great house had at last put its own servants in order — named, liveried, booked, bonded, sent to reap, funded, and standing on ground it owned. The master knew his forty servants by name and slept well. Then the steward came with a troubled tally: "I have tried to count the hands at work in the house, and I cannot. Your own forty I know. But the hall is full of others — the neighbour's clerk, day-labourers from the hiring fair, hired men from six trading companies who came for the harvest and stayed, and three I have never seen who came in on the carpenter's writing-desk. For every one of your named servants there are near a hundred and fifty other hands, and I do not know whose they are, who let them in, or whether they'll be here tomorrow."

It was not that the strangers were wicked — most worked honestly, and the house ran better for them. It was that the master had built his whole discipline for a household of forty he could picture, and now had a workforce of thousands he could not; his liveries and book and bond governed the forty and said nothing about the six thousand. And the cause was his own economy: the fire had gotten cheap, so he lit every hearth and took every hand the fair would send. The cheap stove had filled his house with cooks. A kitchen with a hundred and fifty cooks to every one you can name is not governed by knowing your forty well — it is governed, or not, at the door.

So the guild offered the right thing: one badge office at the front gate, and no hand works without a badge struck there — yours, the neighbour's, the trading companies', even the three on the writing-desk. And three things made the badge worth more than a name on a list: one office, not twelve (a single authority that issues to all, so there is one place to see and to revoke); a named master on every badge (no badge without a person who answers for it — a desk and a letterbox don't make a servant its own master, they make it someone's responsibility); and take the badge back the hour the work ends (for the breach is never the honest worker you badged, but the day-labourer whose badge still opens the cellar three months on). The badge does not govern the agent; it makes the agent governable — and only if the office knows its count and actually revokes.

The question it forces: We can name our own forty — but can we name the six thousand? Does every working hand on our premises — ours, our vendors', and the ones no one remembers hiring — carry a badge from one office we control, with a named master and a lifecycle that takes it back the hour the work ends? Or have we let a hundred and fifty un-badged hands to every one of ours work the back rooms, each with a key we never issued and cannot revoke?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Badge

Every prior control was designed for a workforce you could picture; this week a vendor priced it at fifty million. The control that now decides your exposure is not how good your best agent is or even whether you own your infrastructure — it is whether every agent has a badge from one authority you control, a named human master, and a lifecycle that revokes. The news: Agent 365 / Entra Agent ID issues identity to local agents (Claude Code, Copilot CLI) and third-party agents via secretless workload-identity federation — identity must be centralized or it is not revocable, and local/third-party agents are the hard part because your directory never saw them. The reason: non-human identities outnumber humans ~144:1 while only 21% govern them — the predictable second-order effect of cheap intelligence (Fable 5.2 −20%; GPT-6.2 GA); creation has outrun governance. The answer: one issuing authority, a named owner and scoped mandate on every agent, and an automated joiner-mover-leaver lifecycle with short-lived, secretless, federated credentials that revokes on exit.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Stand up one badge office. You can't govern the workforce by knowing it; non-human identities outnumber your humans ~144:1 and only 21% of orgs govern them. Route every agent identity through one authority you control, and produce a first full inventory of non-human identities — first-party, vendor-embedded, and local agents on laptops. Report your real non-human-to-human ratio and the share issued from a single revocable authority.

2. Put a named master on every badge. An org-chart seat and an email don't make an agent self-governing (Agentic Users still carry an accountable owner). Require a named human owner and a scoped, least-privilege mandate on every agent identity, and flag every credential that has neither — those are your shadow workers. Report the share of agents with a named owner and a scoped mandate.

3. Prove revoke-on-exit. The breach is the departed agent's badge that still opens doors. Pick one high-reach class of agents, move it to short-lived, secretless, federated credentials, and run a real decommission test — measure the time-to-revoke when an agent is retired, a project ends, or a developer leaves — then hunt stale credentials. Report the measured time-to-revoke and the count of still-live credentials belonging to agents, projects or people already gone.
