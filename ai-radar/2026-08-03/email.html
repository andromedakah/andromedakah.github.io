2 · Allegory — The Customs House

In any old trading port the grandest building on the waterfront is the customs house — built in stone while the merchants' sheds were timber, because it was where the state's authority met the merchant's cargo. When it opened, two things changed forever. It never closed again: clearing customs became a daily condition of business, not an event survived once. And everything funneled through one gate, where an officer checked your manifest against your hold, made you declare your cargo, and could inspect for contraband. The gate was powerful and perilous for the same reason: everything of value flowed through it, so an unwatched gate exposed the whole port. The discipline was never "build more warehouses" but "man the gate, check every manifest, never let the one door go unwatched."

Yesterday the enterprise crossed a wall — enforcement began. But what opened Sunday is a customs house, and today is simply the first morning it is open for regular business; it will be open tomorrow, forever ("from preparation to daily administration"). And the industry pointed to exactly where it stands: not at the model, but at the action layer — the instant an agent reaches through a tool into a real system — whose single gate is the MCP plane. 80%+ of enterprise cloud already funnels agent actions through it; its servers pool the keys to every connected system into one door; and the Act's duties — log every crossing (keep it six months), human-oversee the consequential ones, govern the data, secure the boundary — bite there, on every agent in the chain. The officer will not ask the caliber of your model; he will ask for the manifest of what your agents did.

The question it forces: For every autonomous agent, can we name the single gate its actions pass through — the MCP servers, the action layer — and prove, today and every day the customs house is open, that every crossing is logged and kept, every credential scoped to one lane, every high-risk action disclosed and human-overseen, every contraband path inspected for? Or is our gate unmanned while we admire cargo that just got cheaper?

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Map the gate — build the manifest for your action layer. The regulator stands where your agents act. Inventory every MCP server and tool each agent can reach; record its credentials and reachable systems. Then turn on the manifest: a per-action log of every crossing, retained ≥6 months (Art. 26), high-risk actions flagged. This is your enforcement-day evidence — and lets you answer "what did our agents do last week?" on any open day, which is now every day.

2. Man the narrows — scope credentials, isolate tenants, inspect for contraband. An MCP server pooling the keys to everything is a single point of failure. Scope each credential to one lane; isolate tenants; verify the OAuth issuer on the 2026-07-28 spec; scan for the OWASP MCP contraband. Pair it with Article 50 disclosure and a human on the consequential crossings. The boundary extends to every agent in a chain — man each link that takes a high-risk action.

3. Keep the gate model-neutral — rent the cargo, own the customs house. The cargo keeps getting cheaper (DeepSeek V4-Flash-0731 is the newest free hull; Opus 5/Gemini/GPT-5.6/Kimi K3 a swappable menu). Build logging, credential-scoping, disclosure, oversight and inspection as action-layer infrastructure that doesn't depend on any one model — so swapping the engine never leaves a crossing unlogged. The durable, auditable asset the regulator measures every day is the customs house you own.
