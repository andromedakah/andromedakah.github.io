The Deed

Friday, 16 October 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

A lord offers to give you the well — Meta's Muse Spark Open, a reported 816B-parameter open-weight model you can run on ~2 Mac Studios. It is the strongest "own the floor" move there is. But the deed is a lease that calls itself a deed: "open weight" is not "open source." Read the covenants before you tear up the ground.

1 · Executive Summary

Last month the radar set three disciplines for the ground — own the floor, keep the larder, light a second fire. This morning the market offers the sharpest tool yet for owning the floor — and tests whether you can tell a deed from a lease.

1. The reality — the lord offers the well, not just the stove. On 15 October 2026 Meta released Muse Spark Open — a reported 816B-parameter open-weight Mixture-of-Experts (~42B active/token), 1M-token context, multimodal, "the most capable open-weight model from a U.S. lab," runnable on ~2 Mac Studios (MarkTechPost; SiliconANGLE; unite.ai, as reported). Open weights are the strongest "own the floor" move there is — no vendor gate, a pinned version — and the enterprise is already taking them: 34% of tokens, 42% in production, forecast 41% within a year (ETR Sept 2026).

2. The collision — the deed is a lease with covenants. The "Muse Model License" is not open source: a mandatory "Built with Muse Spark Open" label and a separate Meta agreement for any API host above $20M revenue (FourWeekMBA; unite.ai, as reported). The frontier claim is bounded (AA Intelligence Index ~39, behind GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3), and a self-hosted model's safety and security are now yours — the vendor's "full evaluation is still to come."

3. The answer — read the deed, deepen the floor, match it to the room. Read the license like a contract and price the covenants; use open weights to strengthen the abstraction you own without retiring the larder and the second fire; and sink the well where sovereignty, residency or export exposure demand it, keeping the rented stove where you only need cheap, swappable inference.

Bottom line: the foundation told you to own the ground; the deed is the morning a lord offers to give it to you outright — and the discipline of reading what "outright" means. Take the well — the valley is right to dig. But "open" is the word on the lord's lips and "license" is the word on the parchment, and only the house that reads the second knows which one it took home. Never let a lease that calls itself a deed persuade you that you finally own the land.

2 · Allegory — The Deed

A great house had learned the lesson of the foundation: it kept the model a component, kept its grain and recipes in its own larder, and kept a second fire lit. Then a lord rode up with a wagon — not a stove this time but a well, water and all. "You've been renting your water by the bucket. I'll give you the well itself — set it in your own yard and no merchant stands between you and your thirst." The master's heart leapt: this was the very freehold the foundation had taught him to want. A neighbour swore the well ran on two ordinary pumps you could stand in a scullery, and half the valley was digging.

But the steward read the parchment twice before the master signed. "The water is real and the well is ours to run — but this is not the freehold you're calling it." Three lines the master's joy had skipped: we must nail the lord's name to our door ("Watered by the lord's well") on every room the water touched; if we ever sold water above a certain measure, we owed a separate tribute — a gift until we prosper, a lease the day we do; and the most expensive line was unwritten — a well in your own yard is yours to keep clean, mend and guard, and the lord said the full reckoning of the water's safety was "still to come."

So the wise houses learned three things, sitting atop the foundation's three: read the deed like a contract (price the name you must hang, the tribute that wakes when you grow, the labour you inherit); let the well deepen the floor but keep the larder and the fire (one well you own can run dry as surely as a rented gate can close); and match the well to the house (freehold where the crown asks where your water comes from; a rented bucket where you only need cheap water). The lord was not wicked and the well not a trick — the danger was a house so hungry to stop renting that it hears "given" and stops reading.

The question it forces: A lord offers to give us the well — the strongest "own the floor" move there is, and half the valley is digging. But have we read the deed, or only heard "given"? Do we know a true freehold (a genuinely open license, no name to hang, no clause that wakes when we prosper) from a lease that calls itself a deed? And are we sinking this well where sovereignty truly demands it — or only because, after a year of renting, "given" was the word we most wanted to hear?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Deed

When a vendor stops renting you intelligence and offers to give you the model itself, the control that matters moves from "do we own our floor?" to "is this grant a freehold or a lease — and can we carry what owning it costs?" The reality: a model you host is the truest abstraction you can own (no vendor gate, a pinned version), and the MoE shape — 816B total but ~42B active/token — is what lets a frontier-class model run on two workstations; a third of enterprise tokens already run open. The collision: "open weight" is not "open source" — a brand you must display and a $20M revenue trigger make it a lease, the frontier claim is bounded (behind GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3), and self-hosting moves safety, security and patching onto your side of the line ("full evaluation still to come"). The answer: read the license as a contract and price the covenants; deepen the floor without retiring the larder or the second fire; match the deed to the room.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Read the deed. Treat every "open" model as a license to read and price, not a gift to grab. Have legal and architecture classify each open-weight model as a true freehold (permissive/OSI, no branding, no revenue trigger) or a lease in a deed's clothes, and write the covenants — labeling, the $20M clause, redistribution limits, the maintenance burden — into the decision record. Report the share of your "open" usage on a genuinely permissive license versus an encumbered one.

2. Deepen the floor. A model you host is the truest own-the-floor move — but one self-hosted model is still one point of failure, and its safety and security are now yours. Stand up one self-hosted open-weight model behind the abstraction you already control, keep data, context and evals portable across it and your managed APIs, keep a tested second source lit, and prove you can fail over under load. Report the measured time-to-switch and the staffed owner for its security and evaluation.

3. Match the deed to the room. Sink the well where sovereignty, residency or continuity dominate; keep the rented stove where you only need cheap, swappable inference. Map your workloads on a two-axis grid — sovereignty/sensitivity vs. volume/cost — and assign each to "freehold well" or "rented stove," with license covenants and maintenance cost attached to every well. Report the map and the net cost (invoice saved minus maintenance added) for each proposed self-host.
