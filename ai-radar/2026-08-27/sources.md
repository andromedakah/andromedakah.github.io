- Wikipedia, "Agent2Agent" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent2Agent

Claims sourced here (as reported): In the last week of August 2026, Google's Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol formally joined the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), placing the open standard for agent-to-agent communication under the same vendor-neutral governance as Anthropic's Model Context Protocol (MCP), which standardizes how agents connect to tools and data. A2A handles agent-to-agent discovery, tasking and collaboration; MCP handles agent-to-tool/data connections; the two specifications remain distinct and are not merged or interchangeable, but now share a neutral governance home no single lab controls. A2A reached v1.0 in April 2026, is used by 150+ organizations, ships in major cloud platforms and is in enterprise production; IBM's Agent Communication Protocol (ACP) merged into A2A in 2025. The AAIF, launched December 2025, has grown from fewer than 40 members to more than 250, with backers including Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Anthropic, OpenAI, Bloomberg, Block and Shopify. Linux Foundation Executive Director Jim Zemlin said: "By joining the Linux Foundation, A2A is ensuring the long-term neutrality, collaboration and governance that will unlock the next era of agent-to-agent powered productivity." A2A is framed as empowering developers "to build agents that seamlessly interoperate, regardless of platform, vendor or framework." These are the "lingua franca" facts: the two core agent standards, an open tongue for agent-to-agent and an open tongue for agent-to-tool, now sit in one neutral library.

Claims sourced here (as reported): Gartner projects that by 2028 the average Global Fortune 500 enterprise will run more than 150,000 AI agents in use (up from fewer than 15 in 2025), while only 13% of organizations believe they have the right governance in place to manage those agents; Gartner outlines six steps to manage "agent sprawl" (governance and policies, a centralized agent inventory, agent identity and lifecycle, AI information governance, behavior monitoring, and a culture of responsible use). SAP LeanIX's 2026 agentic AI survey found that 98% of companies have deployed AI agents or plan to do so, but that fewer than half of organizations surveyed have visibility into an inventory of AI agents. These are the "scale" figures: the population of agents that must interoperate is heading to six figures per large enterprise, and bespoke N×M integration does not survive that.

### The direction — agent identity and security move into the wire protocol

- Model Context Protocol Blog, "The New MCP Roadmap" (22 August 2026) — https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/mcp-roadmap/
- Model Context Protocol Blog, "The 2026-07-28 MCP Specification Release Candidate" — https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/

Claims sourced here: The Model Context Protocol roadmap published 22 August 2026 names five focus areas, among them "agent identity and enterprise security" — establishing standardized ways to recognize and trust agent identities via standards such as Demonstrating Proof of Possession (DPoP) and Workload Identity Federation — alongside HTTP-native transport unification, agentic messaging primitives, improved tool-result primitives, and SDK developer experience. Cited as the "direction" the standards are moving: agent identity (yesterday's passport) is being folded into the interoperability protocol itself.
