The Threshold

Tuesday, 10 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

A year of work made the servant excellent in the courtyard. The anniversary asks the one question none of that answers: has any of it crossed the threshold into production? A year on from the EU's Apply AI Strategy, the research houses agree — adoption is near-universal, production is rare. Stop counting pilots; count crossings.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from perfecting the servant in the courtyard to whether it has crossed the threshold into production — because the occasion is an anniversary, and anniversaries audit you.

1. The occasion — a one-year anniversary that forces a stock-take. On 17 November 2026 the European Commission convenes the Apply AI Summit in Brussels (hybrid; ~900 in person, up to ~2,500 online), marking one year of the Apply AI Strategy and closing European AI Innovation Month (European Commission; BDVA, as reported). It lands at the exact moment the research houses publish their year-one reckonings.

2. The reckoning — near-universal adoption, rare production. Deloitte (State of AI in the Enterprise 2026, 3,235 leaders, 24 countries) finds only ~25% have moved 40%+ of AI pilots into production; Gartner projects >40% of agentic projects canceled by end-2027 and only ~28% of use cases fully met ROI; McKinsey finds ~44% say AI is scaling yet only ~40% report measurable EBIT impact (as reported). A courtyard full of drilled servants; a street still mostly empty.

3. The answer — count crossings, not pilots. Name "in production" in writing, drive a small cohort all the way across with a real P&L owner and a redesigned workflow, and weigh each by measured ROI/EBIT — because "if 2025 was pilots, 2026 is workflows" (Deloitte's Jim Rowan) is a promise the data says most houses have not yet kept.

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each perfect the servant inside the courtyard — the threshold is the separate, decisive step of crossing into production, where value is earned, not demonstrated. A year in, only ~25% have crossed, >40% of agentic projects will be scrapped, and barely a quarter of use cases met ROI. Name the threshold, send a cohort across, weigh what comes home. A pilot repeated is still a pilot.

2 · Allegory — The Threshold

A great house had spent a careful year making its servants excellent in the courtyard — named, booked, bonded, waged, sent to reap, funded, the ground beneath them kept their own. Then the year turned, a year to the morning since the master had vowed this would be the year the servants did the real work of the town. So he called the auditor, expecting praise for his courtyard. But the auditor did not count the servants, nor test their drill. He asked one question: "How many have crossed the threshold — gone out into the street, and come home with the day's work actually done?"

The master gestured at the splendid yard. The auditor turned his back on it and looked at the street — and the street was nearly empty. Only one house in four had sent even a fair portion across; of the errands begun, more than two in five would be abandoned; of those sent, barely a quarter came home able to lay real coin on the table. The steward offered the year's motto, "If last year was rehearsals, this year is work," carved proudly over the door. The auditor pointed at the carving, then at the empty street: "A rehearsal you repeat is still a rehearsal. The carving is a promise. The street is the proof. And the two do not yet agree." The house had mistaken perfecting the servant for sending it.

So the wise houses did three plain things: name the threshold (write what "crossed" means — real street, a named hand that answers, measured by what it brought home); send a cohort, not a fleet (a few errands that matter, each with a hand that owns its profit and a route redrawn for the real street); and weigh what comes home (make each returning servant lay its coin on the table). For a house can spend forever getting ready to cross, and call the getting-ready progress — and the anniversary is a mercy precisely because it asks not how ready you are, but what you shipped.

The question it forces: A year on from the day we declared this the year of real work, how many of our agents have actually crossed the threshold into production — serving real load, owned by a named team, measured by what they earned — and how many are still, however beautifully, rehearsing in the courtyard?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Threshold

Every radar discipline governs the servant inside the courtyard; the threshold is the act of leaving. Readiness is not production, demonstration is not value, and a governance program that perfects the servant without ever crossing has built a magnificent cost center and called it an AI strategy. The occasion: the EU Apply AI Summit (17 Nov) gives the field a shared, dated moment to compare strategy against shipped reality — and the comparison is unkind. The reckoning: the threshold is a systems problem, not a model problem (real load, integration, workflow redesign, ownership, monitoring — none solved by a better model), and the cancellation rate is a concentration signal (the thinly spread feed the 40%; survivors have a P&L owner and a redesigned process). The answer: name the threshold (a written bar for "in production"), send a cohort across (a few use cases, P&L owner, redesigned workflow), and weigh what comes home (each crossing tied to measured ROI/EBIT).

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Name the threshold. Write a one-page, testable bar for "in production" — real load, a named accountable owner, a specific business metric, the production-grade controls it carries — and audit every AI use case against it. Report how many agents are genuinely in production by that definition versus how many are pilots, separating the ~25% who have crossed from the three in four who have not (Deloitte).

2. Send a cohort, not a fleet. The cancellation rate punishes the thinly spread: >40% of agentic projects will be scrapped by 2027, and the survivors have a P&L owner and a redesigned workflow (Gartner). Pick three to five high-value use cases, give each a named P&L owner and a redrawn end-to-end workflow, set a kill/scale decision at a named milestone, and concentrate spend on getting them over the threshold. Report the cohort, each owner, each crossing date.

3. Weigh what comes home. ~44% say AI is "scaling" yet only ~40% report EBIT, and only ~28% of use cases met ROI (McKinsey; Gartner); "we have agents" is not a result. Attach each in-production agent to a real number — cost saved, revenue added, cycle time cut, EBIT moved — owned by the same P&L owner, and bring that number, not the pilot count, to the next review. Report the measured value of each crossing and a dated plan for the next cohort.
