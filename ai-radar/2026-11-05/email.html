The Millstream

Thursday, 5 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

You owned the floor — now see what it's made of, and it's not software. The frontier labs stopped selling a model and started mortgaging a millstream: public capital (Anthropic's S-1) and twenty-year power leases (Google backstopping Anthropic's ~$19B / ~401MW Hawesville lease). The model is a commodity; the water that turns the wheel is the moat and the risk. Read the ledger, map the water, keep a second stream.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves beneath the software floor to the physical and financial substrate — power, capital and twenty-year supply. The model has commoditised; the thing that now constrains you is the millstream that turns the wheel.

1. The act — labs mortgage the millstream, not the miller. On 4 November 2026 TeraWulf disclosed that Google will credit-backstop Anthropic's data-centre lease at Hawesville, Kentucky — 20 years, ~401 MW, ~$19B contracted, first capacity H2 2027 (Data Center Knowledge; Securities.io; Yahoo Finance, as reported). Days earlier, Anthropic's 26 October S-1 revealed ~$16.29B of H1-2026 revenue (up ~1,304%), an $80.7B net loss (~$72.4B non-cash), ~85% gross margin, and a plan to list on Nasdaq as ANT (CNBC; Yahoo Finance, as reported).

2. The shrug — the model keeps commoditising. In the same fortnight Gemini 4 Carbon, a cheaper GPT-6.1 Terra, Tencent's Hy4 (~$0.42/$1.25 per M tokens) and Flower's Endeavor 1.1 all arrived — to a shrug (as reported; names/dates vary). Milling skill is now a commodity; the scarce, financed thing is the water.

3. The answer — diligence the counterparty, not just the model. Read the supplier's balance sheet and power contracts; trace each critical capability to its physical substrate (region, grid, campus, backer, on-line date); and keep a second stream, not just a second model — because the supply curve is now "pre-sold at gigawatt scale," set years out in capital and power markets (J.P. Morgan; Morgan Stanley; Data Center Knowledge, as reported).

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation governed the servant and the floor — the millstream is what that floor is made of, and it is not software. Your AI supplier is now a capital-and-power enterprise carrying an $80B paper loss and leasing gigawatts on twenty-year terms, some not switched on until 2027. Shop millers ruthlessly — but read the ledger, trace the water, and keep a second stream.

2 · Allegory — The Millstream

A valley full of mills had always judged a mill by its miller. The radar's own house had learned two hard truths already: a miller can be hired cheaply and swapped in an afternoon, so never fall in love with one; and the real danger is letting the man who rents you the cheap miller pour the foundation of your mill-house. The house owned its floor. It thought it understood the ground.

Then the great mills did something new. One went down to the money-changers and offered to sell shares in the mill itself — its books showing custom grown thirteen-fold in a year beside a loss so vast it took the breath away, most of it an accountant's mark, but a number that announced: this is no longer a workshop; it lives and dies by capital. And another signed a twenty-year lease on the millstream — the river that turns every wheel — with a greater house co-signing the water-rent, the new wheels not even built yet, the first not running until the summer after next.

And the millers? Never better, cheaper, or more plentiful — four new ones that fortnight, one at a fraction of the old toll, met with a shrug. There was the whole lesson: the thing everyone competed over (the miller) was now pennies; the thing nobody thought to compete over (the water) had quietly become the only thing that mattered — bought up twenty years at a time, with capital the ordinary house could not raise. So the house asked a colder question: whose water do we grind on, who co-signed the rent, what do their books look like, and when do the promised wheels actually turn? It had been reading the miller's testimonials when it should have read the mill-owner's ledger and the deed to the river.

So the wise houses learned three things: read the ledger (diligence the supplier like a bank — losses, debts, backers, leases); trace the water (which river, whose weir, which greater house holds the rent, when the wheels turn); and keep a second stream (a fallback on a different river and a different balance sheet, not the same water renamed).

The question it forces: We can swap the miller in an afternoon — four arrived this fortnight to a shrug. But do we know whose water we grind on? Have we read our AI supplier's ledger the way we'd read a bank's? Have we traced each capability to the river beneath it — grid, data centre, backer, on-line date? And is our fallback a genuinely different stream, or the same river wearing a second name?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Millstream

Every prior control stops at software; the millstream is about the physical and financial substrate under it. The thing that commoditised (the model) is not the thing that now constrains you (power and capital). The act: the labs raised public capital (Anthropic's S-1) and signed gigawatt-scale, decade-plus power leases backstopped by hyperscalers (Google–Anthropic–TeraWulf) — so a supplier's unit economics are now power- and capital-bound, supply is committed years out, and vendor diligence must include the balance sheet. The shrug: cheap, abundant models (Tencent Hy4 ~$0.42/$1.25; a cheaper GPT-6.1 Terra) are exactly what make the physical substrate the binding constraint. The answer: read the supplier's filings and power contracts, trace each capability to its substrate (region, grid, campus, backer, on-line date), and hold a genuinely independent second supply — different infrastructure and a different balance sheet.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Read the ledger. Your principal AI suppliers are now capital-markets, capital-hungry entities (an S-1 showing ~$16.29B revenue against an $80.7B net loss; 20-year leases backstopped by hyperscalers). Have finance or credit produce a one-page counterparty read on each — funding runway, capital structure, major leases and backers — and put it before the board beside the model-performance review. Report the share of critical AI spend with suppliers you've assessed as counterparties, not just models.

2. Map the water. The supply curve is "pre-sold at gigawatt scale," set years out, with some capacity not live until H2 2027. For your top AI-dependent workloads, document the region, grid, campus, capital backer, contracted capacity and on-line date beneath each — and flag any plan that depends on capacity or funding not yet built. Report the share of critical workloads for which you can name the substrate.

3. Keep a second stream. A fallback on the same hyperscaler region, power market and capital backer is no fallback. Pick one critical AI workflow and stand up a genuinely independent alternate supply — different infrastructure and a different balance sheet — then run a timed cut-over under load. Report the measured time-to-switch to a separate stream and a dated plan to extend it.
