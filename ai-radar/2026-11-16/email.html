The Sheath

Monday, 16 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

The blade got cheaper again — and the smith sold the same edge two ways: the same model with its safeguards on (Fable 5.2) and with the cyber and biology safeguards struck off (Mythos 5.2, for the vetted). The capability is a commodity now; the restraint around it is the product — and when you take the bare blade, the vendor's liability comes home with it. Find which blades run bare, forge your own sheath, fit it to your own hand.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from the ground the house stands on to the edge in its hand — because a vendor just unbundled the guard from the blade, making the restraint an option you select rather than a fixture you inherit.

1. The reality — the blade got cheaper, and the smith sells the same edge sheathed or bare. On 9 November 2026 Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.2 at $8/$40 per million tokens (a 20% cut from 5.1's $10/$50) with safeguards on, and Claude Mythos 5.2 — reported as the same model with the cyber and biology safeguards removed, for verified organizations only: "Same Model, Different Safeguards" (CloudZero; TechBriefly; MarkTechPost; mlq.ai, as reported). Google's Gemini 4 Carbon went GA at $2/$10 the next day. The capability is a commodity; the sheath is the product.

2. The collision — the bare blade carries the smith's liability to your books, just as the law arrives. A vendor's safeguards are the vendor carrying part of the cyber- and bio-misuse risk; strip them (Mythos-style, open weights, tuned-down guardrails) and the risk moves onto you. The EU Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744) deferred high-risk duties to 2 December 2027 but "removes no obligation," kept the Article 50 transparency/marking duty at 2 December 2026, and extended the Article 5 bans (CSA; usercentrics; i-scoop; DLA Piper, as reported). And ~88% of enterprise AI-agent pilots never reach production — you can't sheath an edge you haven't found.

3. The answer — inventory bare blades, own the sheath, keep it portable. Map every AI system by its safeguard configuration and who is vetted to wield it; forge your own restraint layer for each bare edge and prove it; and make the sheath a property of your own hearth (the floor you own), not of any one vendor's model, so the cheap, swappable blade never strips your guard.

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each governed the servant or its ground — the sheath is about the edge itself. The market proved the blade is a commodity twice over (Fable 5.2 −20%; Gemini 4 Carbon $2/$10); then the smith offered the same blade bare, guard struck off, to the vetted few, and sold it as a feature. Buy the cheap blade — it should be cheap — but never carry a bare edge you cannot answer for, and never let the smith decide who holds your sheath.

2 · Allegory — The Sheath

A great house had made its servants excellent and learned to own the very ground they stood on — the floor its own, the larder in its own pantry, a second fire kept lit. It bought its blades from the smith, and the blades were better and cheaper every season. This week the finest cost a fifth less than last year's. Then the smith did a new thing: he held up two blades that were the very same steel, keen to the same edge, and sold them two ways — sheathed, with a guard fixed over the edge, for most; and, to a vetted few who could prove they knew how to carry naked steel, the very same blade bare, the guard struck clean off.

And the master saw that the thing he was choosing was not the blade — that was a commodity now, identical in either hand — but the sheath: the restraint around the capability. The smith was honest about the catch: take the bare blade, and he handed over, with it, every liability the guard used to carry. "While the guard is on, a cut is partly my affair. Strike it off at your request, and a cut is your cut, wholly." A bare blade was not merely a sharper tool; it was a transfer of responsibility, dressed as an unlocking of power. And when the careful houses counted their own armory, most could not say which blades were carried bare, by whom, or who held the sheath — they had governed the smith's steel and never once the restraint.

So the wise houses learned three things: know which blades are carried bare, and by whom; forge your own sheath for every bare edge (a tested restraint of your own, since the liability came home with the bare blade); and keep the sheath a thing of your own hand, fitted to the handle, not one smith's blade, so a cheaper, keener edge next market-day takes your own guard. And the king's inspector, who had promised a public guard by law, had pushed the heavy rules a full year down the road — but struck off no duty, and kept, unmoved, the simplest rule of all: any blade carried in the open must be marked as what it is, due in a fortnight, not a year.

The question it forces: The blade is cheap and getting cheaper — Fable 5.2 down a fifth, Gemini 4 Carbon at $2/$10 — and we barely blinked. But the smith is no longer selling us a blade; he is selling us a choice about the sheath, and handing us his liability when we take the edge bare. Which of our blades are carried bare, and by whom? Have we forged our own guard for every naked edge — or are we holding the smith's liability with no sheath of our own, a fortnight before the law's plainest mark falls due?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Sheath

Every prior control governs the servant or its ground; the sheath is about the edge itself. The thing that commoditized (the model's capability) is not the thing that now carries the risk and liability (the safeguard configuration) — and confusing the two is how a house ends up handing its servants naked steel while congratulating itself on a cheaper, keener edge. The reality: the safeguard configuration is now a first-class deployment variable — the same weights present as a guarded or un-guarded object, so "which model" no longer tells you the risk; "which safeguard configuration, for whom" does, and the liability follows the guard. The collision: a deferral is not a dispensation — the Omnibus moved the heavy dates to 2 Dec 2027 but the duty to risk-manage, document, oversee and mark did not move, and Article 50 is a fortnight out; meanwhile ~88% of agent pilots never reach production, so the restraint problem hides behind an inventory problem. The answer: build a safeguard-configuration inventory, forge your own restraint layer per bare edge (classifiers, eval gates, human checkpoints, logging) and test it, and make the restraint a property of the abstraction you own so it fits any model.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Find the edges. A vendor just made the guard an explicit choice (Fable 5.2 vs Mythos 5.2), and open weights and tuned-down guardrails are the same move — yet ~88% of agent pilots never reach production, so most houses cannot say which edges run bare. Build a one-page register: every model in use or in pilot, tagged full-guard / reduced-guard / open-weight / tuned-down, with a named vetted operator for each un-guarded edge. Report the count of un-guarded models in service and how many have a named owner for their restraint.

2. Forge the sheath. Take the bare blade and the vendor's cyber/bio/abuse liability moves onto your books — and the law kept the Article 50 transparency/marking duty at 2 December 2026, a fortnight out, deferral of the heavy duties notwithstanding. For every un-guarded edge, stand up and test your own restraint layer (input/output classifiers, eval gates, human checkpoints, immutable logging), and ship transparency marking on AI-generated/assisted customer-facing output now. Report the share of un-guarded deployments with a tested, house-owned restraint.

3. Fit the sheath to your own hand. The capability is cheaper again (Fable 5.2 −20%; Gemini 4 Carbon $2/$10) and you will re-source it; a sheath wired to one vendor's model is stripped the day you switch. Move your restraint layer in front of the abstraction/gateway you already own (the floor), so it fits any model and no vendor's safeguard choice is your only guard — then prove it by swapping the underlying model behind one workflow with the guard intact. Report the measured result: the same restraint, held across a model swap.
