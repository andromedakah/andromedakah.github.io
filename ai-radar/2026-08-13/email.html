The Blossom Price

Thursday, 13 August 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

Yesterday the radar watched compute cross from an expense into a financed asset class. Today the camera turns from the road to the vehicles that run on it — the agents — and the price the market is putting on them. On 12 August, Cognition (the maker of Devin) was reported in talks to raise more than $1B at ≥$40B — up from ~$26B three months earlier — on a run-rate only now approaching $1B: roughly a 40× forward-revenue multiple, doubled in a single quarter. It lands on the same hard floor: PwC finds 56% of CEOs saw no measurable AI ROI last year. The board's question: are we pricing AI by the blossom on the branch, or the fruit in the barn?

1 · Executive Summary

For a month this radar has argued the model is a commodity and the advantage is the layer you own around it; yesterday it added the financed road of compute beneath the stack. Today the story moves to what the market is willing to pay for the things that drive on that road — and this week the answer is: a great deal, in advance, on the strength of the bloom.

1. Agents are priced by the blossom, not the fruit. On 12 August, Cognition (maker of Devin) was reported in talks to raise >$1B at ≥$40B — up from a ~$26B valuation three months ago (a $25B pre-money round on ~$492M revenue) — on a run-rate now approaching $1B. A ~$40B tag on a ~$1B run-rate is an implied ~40× forward-revenue multiple: the market paying today, in full, for a harvest not yet picked. That romance sets the price you pay to renew every agent vendor.

2. The trust layer went live under the froth. The AI Trust and Security Consortium (AITSC) launched 12 August (~50 enterprise security leaders setting peer-defined standards); the EU AI Act's transparency duties have been enforceable since 2 August (disclose AI, machine-readable labels, fines up to €15M or 3%); and the US is gating frontier-model access. An agent you cannot audit or disclose is not board-deployable.

3. The engine keeps commoditizing beneath the priced layer. Opus 5, GPT-5.6, Gemini 3.6 Flash and open leaders like Kimi K3 — with DeepSeek V4 Flash at ~$0.14 / $0.28 per 1M tokens — push the brain toward commodity; MCP's 2026-07-28 spec went stateless and its SDKs cleared 1B installs. The brain and rails get cheaper; the agents get pricier.

Bottom line: the market is pricing agents — vendors and, by contagion, your own projects — by the blossom: growth, benchmark, TAM. Buy the fruit instead. Underwrite every agent by realized value per workload, price the vendor's durability as coldly as any 40×-forward counterparty, and treat provable trust as part of the return — because a business that pays the blossom price will own a beautiful spring and an empty barn.

2 · Allegory — The Blossom Price

Picture an orchard town that once valued its orchards the plain way — by the fruit in the barn: bushels harvested, reliably, at a knowable cost. Then, almost overnight, buyers began pricing orchards by the blossom instead — bidding in spring, on trees still in flower, for the harvest the bloom promised. One young orchard was bid at nearly double in a single year on nothing but a heavier bloom and a better story — men no longer buying apples, but the promise of apples, at forty times what the trees had ever yielded.

But blossom is a promise; fruit is a fact, and a long, uncertain season stands between the two — frost, drop, a thin set. The whole valley had just lived that lesson: for all the white bloom, more than half the owners could not yet show a coin of profit. And a new question now met every barrel at the market gate: whose orchard is this, is the fruit labeled and sound, will its books survive an audit? The fruit buyer did the harder thing — she priced by the yield she could verify, treated the bloom as a reason to watch the tree, not to pay for it, and when the frost finally came she still had a barn full of apples while her neighbors had a hillside of petals.

The question it forces: the market is pricing AI agents by their blossom — growth, benchmark, TAM, a valuation that doubles in a quarter on a promise. Are we pricing the agents we buy and build the same giddy way — or underwriting them by the fruit actually in the barn: realized value per workload, the vendor's durability if the valuation resets, and whether the output is one we can audit, label and defend under the trust rules now live?

3 · Technical — Price the Fruit, Not the Blossom

The roots — the brain (cheap): Opus 5, GPT-5.6, Gemini 3.6 Flash, Kimi K3 (open leader), DeepSeek V4 Flash (~$0.14 / $0.28 per 1M) — commoditizing, not where the $40B is. The rails — the plumbing (free): MCP's 2026-07-28 spec went stateless, with ~500M SDK downloads/month and TS & Python SDKs each past 1B — standard rails enable a premium, they don't command one. The blossom — the agents (priced for the harvest): Cognition/Devin ~$40B (implied ~40× forward, doubled in a quarter); Salesforce Agentforce ~$1.2B ARR +205% (Q1 FY27); Microsoft ~$37B AI run-rate with ~1 in 3 GitHub PRs involving an agent. Value and price move in opposite directions down the tree: roots and rails toward zero, blossom toward the sky.

Value an agent by growth, benchmark and TAMValue it by realized business value per workload.
Renew a vendor on its story and its valuationRenew on audited harvest — sold, paid, repeatable.
Single richly-valued vendor across core flowsVendor durability stress-tested; exit priced.
Deploy first, worry about trust laterAudit trail, disclosure and labeling from day one.

On legacy: build a one-page "harvest sheet" for every agent — realized value per workload, cost per successful task, production reliability (not demo pass-rate), payback. Price vendor durability (model the reset), price your exit and switching cost, and wire trust in from the start. A bigger rented brain is not an agent strategy.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Build a "harvest sheet" for every agent — price the fruit, not the blossom. For each agent you buy or build, put one page in front of the board recording the harvest, not the flower: realized value per workload, cost per successful task, production reliability, payback. Report one number: what share of our AI spend is tied to an audited, repeatable return? PwC's 56%-no-ROI figure is what an org looks like when nobody keeps a harvest sheet.

2. Underwrite vendor durability as coldly as any 40×-forward counterparty. Model the reset — a down round, tighter funding, an acquisition. Answer three questions: our exposure (how deeply is this vendor wired into core flows?), our exit (switching cost, the stickiness behind a "swappable" agent), and our continuity (what breaks, and how fast can we recover, if their season turns?). Avoid concentrating a critical workflow on a single, richly-valued, cash-burning counterparty without covenants.

3. Wire trust in now — it is part of the return, and it is already law. Turn on the audit trail over agent sessions, disclose AI to customers where the EU AI Act now requires it, and label AI-generated content machine-readably. Track the AITSC standard as it forms. An agent you cannot audit, disclose or defend cannot be deployed at board scale — its benchmark score won't save you in front of a regulator.
