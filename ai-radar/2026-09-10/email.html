The Provenance

Thursday, 10 September 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

Yesterday the story was the tide table — build one quay any ship can dock at. This morning the frame turns from how fast the ships arrive to what is inside the cargo and whether you can prove where it came from. OpenAI says a swarm of ~10,000 agents produced a candidate proof of the 90-year-old Navier–Stokes problem in ~88 hours (~130B tokens) — unverified, and within hours disputed over whether it used two researchers' private work (OpenAI "can't rule out" a de-identified benefit). The same day, the mirror image: NSA/CISA/FBI advisory AA26-251A accuses six China-based firms of "industrial-scale" distillation of Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok. When value is made by ingestion at machine scale, the papers that trace what went in — and the watch on what is taken out — are the asset.

1 · Executive Summary

The two loudest developments in the window are two faces of one problem: AI now produces value by ingesting knowledge at machine scale, and the ability to account for what went in — and what is taken out — has become the asset and the risk.

1. The event — a swarm produced the masterwork, and no one can produce the papers. OpenAI says an internal model directing ~10,000 coordinating agents produced a candidate proof of a 3-D Navier–Stokes singularity — a $1M Clay Millennium Prize Problem — after ~88 hours, ~3M messages and ~130B output tokens; Altman called it "one of the most amazing moments" in OpenAI history. Within hours it became a provenance dispute: NYU's Buckmaster and Anthropic's Alpöge had a related result weeks earlier; OpenAI denied direct access but "can't rule out having benefited from de-identified data derived from their use of its products," and the Clay Institute has not verified it (CNBC, Fortune, VentureBeat, as reported).

2. The mirror — the same problem, pointed the other way. NSA/CISA/FBI advisory AA26-251A (8 Sep) names DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI for "aggressive, malicious, and targeted distillation … at an industrial scale" — billions of tokens across millions of exchanges from Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok since late 2024, with DeepSeek's $5.6M training cost called "misleading" (CISA, Reuters, as reported). What you make is copied out; what you consume must be accounted for.

3. The answer — hold the provenance. A brilliant artifact you cannot trace is a liability, not a moat. Attach a reconstructable record of inputs, data lineage and rights to every agentic output; guard the model boundary both ways (zero-data-retention and private processing inbound; extraction monitoring, rate limits and watermarking outbound); and treat independent verification, not applause, as the test of an AI result.

Bottom line: the week that shipped four frontier models now shows you what to fear about them — not their speed, but the opacity of what they eat and what is eaten from them. Do two unglamorous things: make provenance a requirement (a reconstructable record of what went into every output and the right to use it, in logs the agents cannot alter), and guard the door in both directions. You held the tide table yesterday; today, keep the provenance.

2 · Allegory — The Provenance

A great house of art grew famous, and in the old days it kept a dull discipline no visitor ever noticed: for every piece, it kept the papers — where it was made, by whose hand, what its maker had studied and by what right. The papers were why a work on the wall was worth what the wall said: the house could prove it was genuine, prove it was theirs to sell, and prove it had not been lifted from another's vault. Then a workshop opened that worked at a speed no one had seen — a thousand hands at once, all night, until by morning a finished masterwork stood in the courtyard and the whole town gasped. But a quieter master from the next street saw, in one solved problem of light, his own unpublished study, and asked the only question that mattered: whose hands went into this, and what did they study? Show me the papers. And the workshop could not — a thousand hands and no ledger. It could not prove it had not, in the rush of the night, copied the neighbor's private sketch. The magnificent thing could not be sold, trusted, or defended — not because it was ugly, but because it had no provenance.

And the house learned the same lesson from the other side: forgers had stood at its windows for two years, copying its masterworks stroke by stroke to sell cheap, boasting their imitations cost almost nothing — true only because they had taken, for free, the years of work on the wall. The house had watched the front door and never the window. So the wise houses rebuilt the dull discipline for the fast age: provenance as a requirement of every piece, however fast it was made; judging a work by whether they could account for it, not by how it dazzled, and letting nothing onto the selling wall until an independent eye had verified it with its papers; and guarding the door in both directions — the front door against what came in dirty, the window against what was copied out. The value was never in the making. It is in being able to prove where it came from.

The question it forces: For every dazzling thing our agents produce, can we produce the papers — a record of what data and tools went into it and by what right — or do we have a thousand hands and no ledger? Can we prove our work did not, in the rush, copy a neighbor's private sketch? Do we trust a result because it dazzles, or only once an independent eye has verified it with its provenance? And is anyone watching the window — is our data and our models' output being copied off the wall and sold cheap, and would we even know? A house that cannot produce the papers cannot own what it made, cannot defend it, and cannot see what is being copied off its walls.

3 · Technical — From the Tide Table to the Provenance

When a swarm of thousands of agents can generate a masterwork overnight by consuming enormous, only-partly-traceable inputs, an output you cannot account for is not a moat — it is an exposure, on both the way in and the way out. The event: ~10,000 agents, ~3M messages, ~130B tokens, ~88 hours → a candidate Navier–Stokes proof, unverified; the unit of AI work is now the orchestrated swarm, and its inputs are hard to fully trace — which is why OpenAI could deny direct access to two researchers' work while conceding it "can't rule out" a de-identified benefit. The mirror: distillation is the same gap outward — a model's value reconstructed from its outputs and carried off (NSA/CISA/FBI, 8 Sep). The answer: provenance as a first-class property — a reconstructable record of inputs, tools, data lineage and rights per output, in append-only logs, plus data-boundary controls both ways. MCP 2026-07-28 (EMA stable, CIMD) is the single point to govern what agents consume and emit.

On legacy: the instinct is to celebrate the output and move on; in a swarm world the thing you cannot see is what the swarm consumed and what your models leak. Record: a reconstructable provenance per material output, in append-only logs. Restrict: data-boundary controls on what agents ingest; demand zero-data-retention and private processing. Verify: independent verification as the gate before any output is relied on, sold or filed. Watch: assume distillation — monitor egress, rate-limit interfaces, mark what is yours. The census is the roll, the inspector asks you to produce it, the keyring is which key each agent holds, the locksmith reads your own locks, the tide table is the quay any ship can dock at — and the provenance is why you keep the papers that trace what went into the work and guard the window as jealously as the door.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Make provenance a requirement, not a nicety. Require that every material AI work product carries a record of what data and documents it consumed, which tools and models it called, whose data lineage it carries, and the right to use them — in append-only logs the agents cannot alter. Report the share of production agent outputs whose full inputs we can reconstruct and prove our right to — and the target.

2. Guard the door in both directions. Inbound: data-boundary controls on what agents may consume; demand zero-data-retention and private processing. Outbound: per the NSA/CISA/FBI advisory, monitor exposed model interfaces for large-scale extraction, rate-limit them, and mark what is yours. Report our ZDR coverage on sensitive workloads and whether we'd detect industrial-scale extraction of our own models' outputs.

3. Verify before you rely — treat the dazzle as a claim, not a conclusion. Put independent verification and a provenance check in the path before any AI output is relied on, sold or filed — the "ear" applied not just to whether the answer is right, but to whether we can account for how it was made. Report the share of high-stakes AI outputs that pass an independent verification-and-provenance gate — and a target to close the rest.
