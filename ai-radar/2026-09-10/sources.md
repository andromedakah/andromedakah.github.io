Claims sourced here (as reported): OpenAI says an internal model "significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra," directing a swarm of roughly 10,000 coordinating agents, produced a candidate analytical proof that the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations can develop a singularity in finite time — one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's six remaining $1M Millennium Prize Problems. The effort is reported at roughly 3 million inter-agent messages and ~130 billion output tokens over ~88 hours; the agents are reported to have finished around Saturday 5 September, with the announcement following on 8–9 September. Sam Altman called it "one of the most amazing moments for me in OpenAI history." The Clay Mathematics Institute has not independently verified or accepted the proof. Carried here as "the provenance": the swarm is the new unit of AI work, and a system that consumes inputs at this scale is one whose provenance is, in practice, hard to fully trace.

- Fortune (as above) — Buckmaster accusation, "intimidation," Tao lament (via search summary)
- Tom's Hardware, "OpenAI's breakthrough solution … overshadowed by plagiarism controversy — researcher says OpenAI scraped Codex session and issued career threats" — https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/ (via search summary)

- Terry Tao, "Finite time blowup with smooth forcing term for the incompressible porous medium, Boussinesq and incompressible Euler equations" (7 Sep 2026) — https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/07/ (via search summary)
- officechai, "Terence Tao Calls Buckmaster & Alpöge's Fluid Dynamics Proofs 'A Remarkable Achievement'" — https://officechai.com/ai/ (via search summary)

Claims sourced here (as reported): NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge achieved a closely related breakthrough — finite-time blowup with smooth forcing for the 3-D Euler equations, the frictionless counterpart to Navier–Stokes — around 15 August 2026, formalising it in Lean around 22 August, building on a forcing technique developed by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa. After OpenAI's announcement, Buckmaster questioned whether OpenAI's models had accessed or been trained on their private Codex sessions; OpenAI denied direct access to the two researchers' private work while conceding it "can't rule out having benefited from de-identified data derived from their use of its products." Terence Tao praised the Buckmaster–Alpöge work as "a remarkable achievement," and — writing on Mathstodon — warned of "the opportunity cost in converting a historically productive and motivating problem (such as Navier-Stokes regularity) into a mere viral social media post advertising some benchmark progress, rather than actually advancing the field," cautioning that even a complete AI-generated solution would hold little value if the process behind it remained opaque to outside scrutiny. Carried here as the inbound face of provenance: "we made it" is not the same as "we can account for what went into it."

Claims sourced here (as reported): a joint cybersecurity advisory from the NSA, CISA and FBI (AA26-251A, 8 September 2026) accuses six China-based firms — DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI — of "aggressive, malicious, and targeted distillation activities at an industrial scale that extract restricted proprietary functionalities and capabilities of U.S. frontier AI models," extracting billions of tokens across millions of exchanges from variants of Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok since at least late 2024. DeepSeek's publicly quoted $5.6M training cost is called "misleading" because it excludes the value of data acquired through distillation. The agencies recommend behavioural monitoring, quietly modifying/degrading outputs in response to suspected distillation, and coordinating threat intelligence. Carried here as the outbound face of provenance: what your models produce can be reconstructed and carried off unless you watch the interface.
