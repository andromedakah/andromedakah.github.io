## The story — the proving ground: the EU AI Act's Article 57 regulatory-sandbox mandate came due 2 August 2026

- Outlex, "EU AI Act Sandbox 2026: Free AI Testing for Startups" — https://outlex.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-regulatory-sandbox-guide

Claims sourced here: Under Article 57 of the EU AI Act, each Member State must ensure that its competent authorities establish at least one AI regulatory sandbox at national level, which shall be operational by **2 August 2026** (as documented). The obligation may be met jointly with other Member States or by participating in an existing sandbox providing equivalent national coverage; the Commission may provide technical support, advice and tools (as documented). An AI regulatory sandbox is a controlled, supervised environment to develop, train, test and validate innovative AI systems — with a focus on high-risk systems — before they are placed on the market, under the guidance of the competent authority; participants receive guidance on regulatory expectations, and participation ends in an exit report usable as evidence toward conformity. Good-faith participants who follow the agreed testing plan and the authority's guidance are protected from administrative fines for infringements identified during the sandbox (legal certainty); SMEs and start-ups receive priority and, in many schemes, free access (as reported). The date coincides with the start of GPAI enforcement on 2 August 2026.

### Coverage / state of play — most Member States are behind

- European Parliament (EPRS), "AI regulatory sandboxes: State of play and implementation challenges" — https://epthinktank.eu/2026/04/01/ai-regulatory-sandboxes-state-of-play-and-implementation-challenges/
- EU Artificial Intelligence Act, "AI Regulatory Sandbox Approaches: EU Member State Overview" — https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ai-regulatory-sandbox-approaches-eu-member-state-overview/

Claims sourced here (as reported): Implementation of Article 57 is uneven. Spain runs an operational national AI sandbox (established under Royal Decree 817/2023) and is among the most advanced; most Member States are behind, facing challenges of funding, staffing, expertise and capacity, and the Commission's implementing acts detailing sandbox operation were still pending as of mid-2026. The characterization that the sandbox "fence is only half-built" is the radar's framing.

## The failure economics — why a proving ground is the missing control

- Gartner, "Gartner Predicts Over 40% of Agentic AI Projects Will Be Canceled by End of 2027" — https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027
- Forbes, "Why 40% Of Agentic AI Projects May Be Canceled By 2027" — https://www.forbes.com/sites/robertszczerba/2026/07/07/why-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-may-be-canceled-by-2027/
- DigitalApplied, "Why Agentic AI Projects Get Canceled (and How to Ship)" — https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/agentic-ai-project-cancellations-gartner-40-percent-2026
- DigitalApplied, "AI Agent Adoption 2026: 120+ Enterprise Data Points" (S&P Global / McKinsey production figures) — https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-agent-adoption-2026-enterprise-data-points
- Prefactor, "AI Agent Adoption Statistics from Gartner, McKinsey & PwC" — https://prefactor.tech/learn/ai-agent-adoption-statistics

Claims sourced here (as reported): Gartner projects that more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027, driven by escalating costs, unclear business value, or inadequate risk controls. Multiple 2026 enterprise surveys find that only about 11–14% of AI agent pilots reach production at scale, and up to 54% stall between three and nine months after an apparently successful pilot; a pilot costing roughly $200k to build can require $1–2M annually to run reliably at scale. McKinsey estimates AI agents could add $2.6–4.4 trillion in value annually. Roughly 31% of enterprises have at least one AI agent in production (S&P Global Market Intelligence / McKinsey), with banking and insurance leading (~47%) and healthcare (~18%) and government (~14%) trailing.

Claims sourced here (as reported): The SANDBOX Act (S.2750), introduced by Senator Ted Cruz, would direct the White House Office of Science & Technology Policy (OSTP) to establish an AI regulatory sandbox program granting AI developers two-year waivers from certain federal enforcement, licensing or authorization requirements — renewable for further terms up to roughly ten years — in exchange for agreements to mitigate consumer and safety risks and to report back to Congress; the program would sunset in 12 years unless renewed. Singapore established an AI-agent sandbox in 2025 (as reported).

On 4 August 2026 France's CNIL issued formal information requests to 14 financial institutions running credit-scoring algorithms, demanding Article 11 technical documentation; the CNIL specifics rest on secondary coverage.
