### The keyring pattern — Anthropic's Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (your keys, your logs)

- Unite.AI, "Anthropic Announces Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, Customer-Held Data" — https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-announces-enterprise-frontier-safeguards-customer-held-data/ (via search summary — **[Corroborated across Unite.AI, MarkTechPost, Qz, Neowin]**)
- MarkTechPost, "Anthropic Introduces Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS): Zero-Data-Retention Privacy Plus Cross-Session Misuse Detection" (2 Sep 2026) — https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/09/02/anthropic-enterprise-frontier-safeguards-efs/ (via search summary)
- Qz, "Anthropic replaces data retention policy with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards" — https://qz.com/anthropic-enterprise-frontier-safeguards-data-retention-090226 (via search summary)
- Neowin, "Anthropic fixes Fable's biggest enterprise privacy problem with new safeguards" — https://www.neowin.net/news/anthropic-fixes-fables-biggest-enterprise-privacy-problem-with-new-safeguards/ (via search summary)

Claims sourced here (as reported): Anthropic introduced Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS) on 1 September 2026 — combining zero data retention with automated cross-session misuse detection by storing activity data in cloud infrastructure controlled by the customer (Amazon S3, Azure Blob Storage, Google Cloud Storage) under the customer's own encryption keys and access policies, with automated safety monitoring but no human review by Anthropic employees. It is designed to replace a data-retention policy that drew complaints from regulated buyers, letting organizations in regulated sectors deploy Claude Fable models while maintaining zero-data-retention compliance. Developed in collaboration with 100+ customers, including roughly a quarter of the Fortune 100 and all U.S. systemically important banks, alongside Comcast, KPMG, Mastercard, Salesforce and Visa; phased rollout beginning later in fall 2026. Carried here as "the keyring pattern": custody of keys and logs stays on the customer's side of the API, with misuse watched in place.

### The vacuum — the non-human-identity governance gap (why it is a board problem)

- Cloud Security Alliance, "The Non-Human Identity Governance Vacuum" (whitepaper, non-human identity / agentic AI governance) — https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-whitepaper-nonhuman-identity-agentic-ai-governance-v1-cs/ (via search summary — **[Search-extracted]**)
- Non-Human Identity Management Group, "Agentic AI attacks expose the real identity control gap in 2026" — https://nhimg.org/articles/agentic-ai-attacks-expose-the-real-identity-control-gap-in-2026/ (via search summary)
- Non-Human Identity Management Group, "GitGuardian Report: Non-Human Identity Sprawl as Primary Security Risk for Enterprise Infrastructure 2026" — https://nhimg.org/nhi-news/non-human-identity-sprawl-enterprise-security-2026 (via search summary)
- O'Reilly Radar, "The Identity Crisis No One Planned For: Governing Non-Human Agents at Enterprise Scale" — https://www.oreilly.com/radar/the-identity-crisis-no-one-planned-for-governing-non-human-agents-at-enterprise-scale/ (via search summary; framing)
- Okta, "AI Agents at Work 2026: Securing the agentic enterprise" — https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/ai-agents-at-work-2026-agentic-enterprise-security/ (via search summary — **[Corroborated with MSSP Alert]**)
- MSSP Alert, "Okta Wants AI Agents Treated Like Identities. Here's Why That Matters." — https://www.msspalert.com/news/okta-wants-ai-agents-treated-like-identities-heres-why-that-matters (via search summary)

Claims sourced here (as reported): non-human identities already outnumber human users roughly 100-to-1 in typical enterprises, reaching ~144:1 in cloud-native environments (CSA / GitGuardian); a 2026 CSA analysis of token sprawl found more than 16% of organizations do not track the creation of AI-related identities at all; while ~91% of organizations already use AI agents, only ~10% have a well-developed strategy or roadmap for managing non-human identities (CSA / nhimg), and a WEF analysis found ~51% of organizations report no clear ownership of AI identities. Okta's *AI Agents at Work 2026* (Okta Platform data plus a survey of 200+ executives) reports that only ~34% of executives say their organization always applies the same security controls to its digital labor force as to its human labor force, that ~90% of organizations have no way to govern what agents in production are actually doing, and that ~54% have already suffered a security incident related to an agent acting unexpectedly; the report's framing line is "Agentic AI readiness is identity readiness." Carried here as "the vacuum": granting outran governing — a key is cut for every agent, but the ring is not kept. The specific percentages are search-extracted from analyst, standards-body and vendor summaries and should be treated as soft until verified against the primary publications.

## Standing context (background)

### The standards floor — EMA and CIMD are the keyring standard (verified at primary source in prior editions)

- Model Context Protocol, "MCP specification 2026-07-28" — https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/ (**[Verified — read directly at primary source in prior editions]**)
- InfoQ, "MCP promotes Enterprise-Managed Authorization to stable" — https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/07/mcp-ema-enterprise-auth/ (egress-blocked; corroborating)

Claims sourced here (verified at the MCP primary source): the 2026-07-28 MCP specification promoted Enterprise-Managed Authorization (EMA) — centralized, IdP-based access to MCP servers, replacing per-server consent prompts — to stable, and adopted Client ID Metadata Documents (CIMD) in place of Dynamic Client Registration, atop a stateless protocol core. The post reports Tier-1 SDKs at roughly half a billion downloads per month, with the TypeScript and Python SDKs each past one billion total downloads. Referenced here as "the keyring standard": the plumbing to name and centrally govern which client holds which key exists — adoption is the gap.

### Enforcement is live — the EU AI Act GPAI regime (standing)

- European Commission, "Enforcement of the AI Act" — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/enforcement-ai-act (via search summary — **[Corroborated for 2 Aug 2026 enforcement]**)
- MediaLaws, "EU AI obligations for GPAI providers: compliance, enforcement, deadlines 2025–2027" — https://www.medialaws.eu/eu-ai-obligations-for-gpai-providers-compliance-enforcement-deadlines-2025-2027/ (via search summary)
- mcpmanager.ai, "EU AI Act: Enterprise AI Agents & Compliance Requirements" — https://mcpmanager.ai/blog/eu-ai-act/ (via search summary; agent audit-surface framing)

Claims sourced here (as reported): EU AI Act enforcement powers over general-purpose AI models became operative on 2 August 2026. Penalties are tiered — up to €35M or 7% of global turnover for prohibited practices; up to €15M or 3% for high-risk or GPAI non-compliance; up to €7.5M or 1.5% for other infringements. Agentic systems add tool calls and API actions to the audit surface: if an organization cannot list what its agents access and who approved each workflow, it is behind the compliance clock. Carried here as "the inspector is already asking": an estate that cannot list its keys cannot answer the regulator.

- McKinsey & Company, "The State of AI: Global Survey 2026" — https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai (primary; egress-blocked; via secondary coverage — **[Corroborated across multiple outlets]**)

Claims sourced here (context, as reported): McKinsey's State of AI 2026 finds that the enterprise differentiator is proprietary data and redesigned process, not the model, and that ~80% of firms report productivity gains while only ~37% report any EBIT impact. Referenced here as the standing frame: the keyring is how you keep that data yours — an ungoverned agent key is the shortest path out of your larder.
