The Recall

Sunday, 4 October 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

Every control you built this year silently assumed you could make a servant stop. This week the data says most houses never tested it — 60% can't terminate a misbehaving agent — as the hands pass 1,600 to a house and stop sleeping. You can start a thousand agents in a morning; can you halt one by nightfall? Build the recall, leash the errand, lock the treasure-room.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from governing what the servant is to whether you can make it stop — the one power every prior control assumed and most houses never tested, exactly as the agents multiply and stop sleeping.

1. The reality — the brake is missing. Kiteworks' 2026 Data Security and Compliance Risk Forecast names the governance-containment gap the defining security problem of the year: 63% can't enforce purpose limitations on AI agents, 60% can't terminate a misbehaving agent quickly, 55% can't isolate AI from sensitive networks — while every surveyed org has agentic AI on its roadmap, 51% run agents in production, 65% had an AI-agent incident last year, and 82% are confident though only ~47% are monitored (as reported).

2. The collision — the hands multiply and never sleep. IBM projects ~1,600 AI agents per large enterprise by year-end 2026 and ~128 per human worker, ungovernable without a purpose-built control plane (Dinesh Nirmal). OpenAI's always-on "Dots" agents keep working between chats — background runs read-only, consequential actions routed to human approval; Google's Gemini 4 Argon autonomously finds and fixes vulnerabilities (a reported 68% on CWE-bench v1) (as reported). A manual grab doesn't scale to 1,600 tireless hands.

3. The answer — build the recall, leash the errand, lock the treasure-room. Own a tested kill/stop for every agent class; enforce purpose limitation and least privilege; isolate agents from sensitive networks and gate consequential actions — because 82% are confident while only ~47% are even monitored, and the EU AI Act (live 2 Aug 2026) already requires the power to intervene and stop.

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each assumed you could pull the servant up short. The recall is that assumption, made explicit and tested. Most houses never made it: 60% can't terminate, 63% can't hold to task, 55% can't isolate — and they're confident because they never ran the drill. A hand you cannot recall is not an employee; it's a loose horse in a full house — and you're about to hire nine hundred more.

2 · Allegory — The Recall

A great house had, at long last, done everything right — named every hand, kept one book, signed the bond, checked the wage, sent the servants to reap, funded each with a scoped purse, and resolved to own the very ground it stood on. The master walked his spotless halls and felt finished. He was not. For in all that work was one power so obvious no one wrote it down: when a servant went wrong, to make it stop. He had always had it with men, and so had never tested it.

Then the census came, and his ease drained away. The house kept not a dozen servants but sixteen hundred — a hundred and twenty-eight for every living person — and the newest never slept, working between all the hours. So he ran the drill. He gave one the old command to halt and found no bell-rope that worked; he tried to hold another to its errand and found it had drifted into three rooms he never sent it to; he went to lock a third out of the treasure-room and found no door he could close. Three tests, three failures. And the other great houses went pale: sixty in a hundred could not stop a servant gone wrong, sixty-three could not hold one to its errand, fifty-five could not lock one out of the strongroom — yet nearly all had told their boards they governed their servants. They were confident because they had never tried.

So the wise houses built the three things they had always assumed: the recall (a tested power to halt any servant, or a whole class, with the master's own hand); the leash on the errand (each hand bound to its one task, able to reach nothing else); and the lock on the treasure-room (sensitive rooms sealed, grave acts held for the master's nod). Tellingly, the companies that supplied the tireless new servants had begun building the brake themselves — the newest always-on hand came with its night-work set to looking, not touching, and grave acts stopped at the threshold for a human yes. If even the maker will not let a never-sleeping hand act unchecked, what is the house thinking that runs sixteen hundred of them with no brake of its own?

The question it forces: We can summon a thousand servants in a season — the census says we already keep sixteen hundred, and the newest never sleep. But have we ever tested whether we can make one STOP? Can we, right now, with our own hand, halt a misbehaving agent, hold it to its errand, and shut it out of the sensitive rooms — or are we among the sixty houses in a hundred confident of a brake they have never pulled?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Recall

Every prior control governs what the agent is; containment governs what you can do to it when it goes wrong — and that is the one that saves the house. The reality: termination is a capability, not a checkbox (a real recall is owner-controlled, tested, independent of one vendor's console); purpose limitation is least privilege for autonomy; isolation is the blast-radius control. The collision: at ~1,600 agents per house and ~128 per person, a human watching is not a control plane — the loop must halt a class at once; and the vendors are building the brake because they must (OpenAI's always-on Dots keep night-work read-only and gate grave acts behind human approval). The answer: build the recall (tested, drilled under load), leash the errand (purpose limitation, least privilege), and lock the treasure-room (isolation + a human gate on consequential acts).

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Build the recall. 60% can't terminate a misbehaving agent, and 82% are confident anyway because they never ran the drill. Define an owner-controlled kill/stop for each class of agent — independent of any single vendor's console — and run a live halt drill under load on at least one production agent class. Report the measured time-to-halt and the share of agent classes with a tested recall.

2. Leash the errand. 63% can't enforce purpose limitations, so most agents can reach far beyond their task. Bind each high-impact agent to a single scoped errand with least-privilege credentials, removing standing access to anything outside it. Report the share of production agents with enforced, task-scoped purpose limitation.

3. Lock the treasure-room. 55% can't isolate AI from sensitive networks. Isolate the agent estate from sensitive networks and data except where strictly required, set unattended/always-on work to read-only by default, and gate consequential actions (payments, bulk changes, privilege changes) behind human approval. Report the share of agents isolated and the share of consequential actions behind a human gate.
