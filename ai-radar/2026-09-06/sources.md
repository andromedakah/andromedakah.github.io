Claims sourced here (as reported): McKinsey's State of AI 2026 reports that roughly 40% of large organizations (>$1B revenue) are now scaling AI agents (up from ~27% a year earlier), and that ~80% of firms report productivity gains but only ~37% report any EBIT impact; roughly 93% of enterprise AI teams are over budget and roughly 60% of agentic-AI cost goes to "response refinement." PwC's 29th Global CEO Survey (4,454 CEOs) finds only ~12% report both cost and revenue benefits from AI, while ~56% report no significant financial benefit (January 2026 data). Carried here as "the ledger": the storehouse has not moved as fast as the bell — productivity is everywhere, earnings are not.

### The locks — a live enforcement regime meets a dual-use model (why it is a board problem)

- European Commission, "Enforcement of the AI Act" — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/enforcement-ai-act (via search summary — **[Corroborated for 2 Aug 2026 enforcement]**)
- European Commission, "Guidelines for providers of general-purpose AI models" — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-gpai-providers (via search summary)
- EU Artificial Intelligence Act, "Implementation timeline" — https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/ (via search summary)
- MediaLaws, "EU AI obligations for GPAI providers: compliance, enforcement, deadlines 2025–2027" — https://www.medialaws.eu/eu-ai-obligations-for-gpai-providers-compliance-enforcement-deadlines-2025-2027/ (via search summary)

Claims sourced here (as reported): EU AI Act enforcement powers over general-purpose AI models became operative on 2 August 2026 (GPAI obligations applied from 2 August 2025; enforcement from 2 August 2026). Models trained above the 10²⁵-FLOP threshold are presumed to carry systemic risk and owe additional duties — model evaluation, adversarial/red-team testing, serious-incident reporting to the AI Office, and cybersecurity measures. Penalties for breaching GPAI obligations reach up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher; the AI Act's top fine tier (for prohibited practices) reaches €35 million or 7%. Carried here as "the locks": a computer-using, cyber-capable "AGI-era" model is exactly what the systemic-risk regime was built to govern.

**Correction of record:** Earlier editions of this radar referenced a "15 September 2026" GPAI systemic-risk *filing* deadline and an "€35M / 7%" GPAI fine tier. Research for this edition could not verify the 15 September date against any official EU source (it surfaced only on a low-quality aggregator); the verifiable operative milestone is **2 August 2026 enforcement**, and the **GPAI-specific fine tier is €15M or 3%** (the €35M / 7% tier applies to prohibited practices, not GPAI obligations). Both are corrected in this edition.

## Standing context (background)

### The standards baseline — the enterprise plumbing beneath the agents (verified at primary source)

- Model Context Protocol, "MCP specification 2026-07-28" — https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/ (**[Verified — read directly at primary source]**)
- InfoQ, "MCP promotes Enterprise-Managed Authorization to stable" — https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/07/mcp-ema-enterprise-auth/ (egress-blocked; corroborating)

Claims sourced here (verified at the MCP primary source): the 2026-07-28 MCP specification is a major enterprise-readiness release — a stateless protocol core (deployable behind standard load balancers), Multi Round-Trip Requests, header-based routing, cacheable list results, and authorization hardening (RFC 9207 issuer validation; Client ID Metadata Documents replacing Dynamic Client Registration). The Enterprise-Managed Authorization (EMA) extension — centralized IdP-based access to MCP servers, replacing per-server consent prompts — was promoted to stable. The post reports Tier-1 SDKs at roughly half a billion downloads per month, with the TypeScript and Python SDKs each past one billion total downloads. Referenced here as the standards baseline: agentic capability is real because the plumbing beneath it is now enterprise-grade. (Note: the spec is dated late July, not this week, but it is the current baseline.)
