2 · Allegory — The Pilot Plant

Every chemical engineer learns the humbling lesson early: a reaction that works beautifully in a beaker will, more often than not, fail when you run it by the ton. At scale, everything the flask hid comes for you — heat that cooks the batch, mixing that becomes fluid dynamics, trace impurities that poison the catalyst. This is why industry invented the pilot plant: a small production line built not to make product cheaply but to learn how the process breaks before you commit a factory. The word we use for every first try — a pilot — comes from exactly this.

The pilot plant is not the reaction — the molecule is a commodity anyone can buy. It's the engineering around it: the control loops, the sensors and interlocks, the sampling regime that catches a bad batch, the operating procedure. Skip it — read a 95% beaker yield and order the factory — and your plant never runs. Scale-up has a name for where these projects die: the valley of death. This week: the reaction is spectacular and nearly free (a #1 model for five dollars, an open weight, a universal connector), and 88% of pilots still never reach production — not because the reaction failed, but because nobody built the plant. IBM's tell: the governance is now what's slowing the reaction, because firms poured the reagents in before building the vessel.

The question it forces: We have a beaker full of a beautiful reaction and a PoC that demos like a 95% yield. But 88% of pilots like ours never reach production, and the failure is the layer around the model, not the model. Have we built the pilot plant — process, evaluation harness, interlocks, inventory — or did we mistake a working beaker for a running plant?

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Audit the beakers. Pull every agent PoC into one table — in-production (with a P&L owner) vs. stalled — and tag each stalled one's scale-up cause: broken process, no eval harness, no governance sequencing, no owner. IDC says four in 33 make it; stop funding beakers you won't scale.

2. Build the evaluation harness and the agent inventory this month — they are your 2 August evidence. Copy DoorDash: automated evals on real sessions (catch a confident hallucination before it ships), plus a complete inventory of every agent, what it can touch, and what it did. Only 18% can do the second today.

3. Own the pilot plant — don't rent scale-up PoC by PoC. Take the vendor help, but build the plant as an owned, model-neutral asset — platform, eval gate, central data governance, control room. Then swapping models or connectors is a governed change through your plant, not a fresh trip through the valley of death.
