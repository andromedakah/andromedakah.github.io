The Reckoning

Wednesday, 18 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

A year of governance told you what the servant is, does and spends. This morning the vendor shipped the control none of that touched: the day the books come due. Microsoft's Agent 365 Business Analytics is an "ERP for AI" that grades "return on intelligence" — useful, and conflicted, because the company selling you the agents now keeps the ledger. Grade value not adoption, keep your own meter, kill what doesn't earn.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from governing the servant to settling its account — because the day Microsoft shipped the ledger is the day the house can no longer not-look.

1. The reality — the vendor built the scorecard and named the metric. At Microsoft Ignite 2026 (17 Nov) Nadella unveiled Agent 365 Business Analytics — "your ERP system for AI… connect spend to business value and continuously optimize their return on intelligence" — with Agent 365 Cost Management (preview), >50M agents claimed on the platform, and seven cheaper MAI models (MAI Code 2 ~98% of GPT-6.1 Sol at ~40% price); GPT-5.6 is GA in Foundry and Copilot is "the new OS for work" (Microsoft; Nadella on X; TestingCatalog, as reported). The labs now sell the measurement of whether capability paid off.

2. The collision — the scorecard lands on a house that isn't earning. 56% of CEOs report zero measurable ROI (PwC); only 23% see significant ROI from AI agents and 79% struggle to scale (WRITER, 2,400 leaders); 95% of pilots show no P&L (MIT/Domino); ~6% are high performers (McKinsey); and 90% expected agent ROI in 2026 while 94% will keep investing even if it doesn't pay off (BCG), as reported.

3. The answer — own the reckoning. Grade value not adoption (return-on-intelligence per workflow); keep your own meter on spend-to-outcome in a layer you control; and run a reckoning with the authority to defund — because a scorecard the vendor keeps will never tell you to spend less with that vendor.

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each governed what the servant is and does — the reckoning is about what it earned. Microsoft made the question unavoidable by shipping the books (an "ERP for AI" that connects spend to value, a cost meter on 50M agents) while making the agents cheaper. Genuinely useful — you cannot manage what you cannot measure — but a reckoning kept by the vendor you pay will always find more to sell and never less to buy. Measure ruthlessly; keep the books yourself.

2 · Allegory — The Reckoning

A great house had spent a disciplined year doing everything right — named every servant, kept one book, signed the bond, checked the wage, sent them to reap, funded a scoped purse, and refused to let the trading company pour the foundation. And the master now had a great many servants — fifty thousand, the steward said, hired cheap and cheaper every season, the way a man eats salted nuts. "Fifty thousand hands!" he boasted at dinner.

Then one grey morning the steward brought him the one page he'd never asked for in all that careful year: the account. On the left, what the house had spent; on the right, what the servants had brought in; and a gap so wide the master put the page down. No control had touched it: the livery told him who, the surety whose name, the two harvests whether they reaped, the purse what they spent — none told him whether the whole costly household was worth its keep. And the valley's tallies looked back: more than half the houses could name no coin earned; nineteen in twenty saw their experiments move nothing; only six in a hundred could point to real profit. He had counted hands; he had never weighed the harvest against the wage.

And the sharp part: the same company that sold him the cheap servants arrived that morning with a gift — a counting-house. "Let us keep your books, connect every coin you spend with us to the value you receive, and tell you your return on cleverness." But a ledger kept by the man you buy from will tally generously and find, every morning, one more servant worth hiring — the one entry it will never print is "buy less from us." It is the butcher offering to audit your diet. So the wise houses kept their own books: weigh the harvest, not the hands; keep your own meter (so it can say "switch, kill, spend less"); and end what does not earn — for the ninety-four in a hundred who vowed to keep spending regardless weren't brave, they'd just decided never to read the account.

The question it forces: We can count our agents — the vendor just counted fifty million for us. But can we weigh them? Do we know our return on intelligence — real value against real spend, workflow by workflow — in a book we keep, with the nerve to kill what doesn't clear the bar? Or have we let the company that sells us the cleverness keep the ledger that says whether it was worth it?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Reckoning

Every prior control governs what the agent is, does, serves or stands on; the reckoning is about what it earned. The thing that is easy to measure (adoption — agents, seats, tokens) is not the thing that matters (return — value per dollar). The reality: Microsoft shipped the ledger (Agent 365 Business Analytics, "return on intelligence") and a cost meter in the same keynote it made agents cheaper (seven MAI models) and counted 50M of them — so measurement is the new lock-in surface, and the denominator must be yours. The collision: 56% of CEOs see zero ROI, 95% of pilots show no P&L, ~6% are high performers, 94% will spend anyway — you cannot optimize what you don't meter, and a reckoning without a kill switch is theatre. The answer: instrument spend-to-outcome in a layer you own (gateway cost tags, outcome evals, a Finance-owned AI P&L), report return-on-intelligence not seat counts, and run a quarterly reckoning that can defund.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Grade value, not adoption. Adoption numbers are the hall; value-per-dollar is the barn (56% of CEOs see zero ROI; 95% of pilots show no P&L). Pick your top five AI-touched workflows and compute measured value created ÷ fully-loaded cost for each, and put that ratio before the board in place of seats, agents or tokens. Report the share of AI spend for which you can state a measured return-on-intelligence.

2. Keep your own meter. The vendor selling the agents is offering to keep the books; its scorecard will never say "spend less with us" (94% will keep investing regardless — BCG). Stand up spend-to-outcome instrumentation you own — gateway cost tags per workflow, outcome evals, a Finance-owned AI P&L — so your scorecard can say switch this model, switch this vendor, spend less here. Report the share of AI spend measured by a meter you control.

3. Kill what does not earn. A measurement that cannot end a project is a decoration. Run one quarterly AI reckoning with real authority: rank every initiative by measured return-on-intelligence, defund the bottom tier that cannot clear a stated bar, and redeploy the budget — including the savings from cheaper models — to what earns. Report the number of initiatives ended and the spend reallocated.
