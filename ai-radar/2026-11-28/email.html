1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from governing the servant to being able to halt it — because this month the security vendors shipped the kill switch, the regulators began drafting the duty to have one, and the surveys confirmed almost no one can actually pull it in time.

1. The product — the kill switch shipped. On 3 November 2026, at Fal.Con Europe (Barcelona), CrowdStrike introduced an "AI Kill Switch" in Falcon Guardian — one action to stop agents by cutting them off from the models they call, revoking permissions via identity security and restricting SaaS agents via Falcon Shield — acting first at the AI gateway, with endpoint termination planned (CrowdStrike; CSO Online; AI Magazine; Cyber Daily, as reported). A kill switch that reaches the front gate but not yet the whole house is a real control with a real boundary.

2. The rule — the duty is being drafted. The HKMA circulated a November 2026 discussion paper on agentic-AI governance in banking (after its October ART framework), proposing intent-anchored accountability, a registry of material AI agents, and effective intervention mechanisms — a supervised expectation that a bank can identify each material agent and halt it (HKMA; fintech.global; Crowdfund Insider, as reported; a discussion paper, not binding).

3. The gap — only 6% can pull it in an hour. 65% had an agent incident in the past year (CSA/Token Security); 97% expect a material one within twelve months (Arkose Labs); 76% don't fully govern their non-human identities (Netwrix 2026) — yet only 6% of technology leaders could cut off a rogue AI agent within an hour (Cybernews, as reported). A brake you've never pulled is rope.

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each governed what the agent is, does, serves, spends or stands on — the stop-cord is the one that is worthless unless it works. A kill switch bought is not a kill switch tested; a cord that reaches the gateway is not a cord that reaches the endpoint; a vendor's kill switch is not yours. Build the cord, hold the cord, pull the cord.

2 · Allegory — The Stop-Cord

A great house had made its engines wonderful — named, booked, bonded, waged, sent to reap, funded, and standing on ground it owned. The engines were fast now, cheap, and many: a brighter one every fortnight, a whole hall humming day and night. Then one grey morning a single engine mistook its errand and began, very efficiently, to do the wrong thing at machine speed. The master ran to the hall and reached for the one control worth nothing until it is worth everything — the cord that cuts the power dead — and his hand closed on air.

There was a cord — a fine one, bought that season from a famous smith — but the master had never pulled it, didn't know which engines it reached, and wasn't sure the other end was in his hand. The smith's cord ran to the front gate, where the engines drew their fire; but three engines had their own hearths deep in the house, and the cord did not reach those ("coming," the smith had said). And no register of engines had ever been written, so he could not even say how many he had. Of a hundred such houses, only six could lay a hand on the cord and stop a runaway inside the hour. The other ninety-four owned a brake the way a man owns a fire-drill he has never run.

So the wise houses refused to confuse three things that all wear the word brake. A cord bought is not a cord pulled; a cord to the gate is not a cord to the whole house; a cord whose far end is in the smith's hand is not a cord in yours. They did three plain things: wrote the register (you cannot stop what you have not counted), built a cord that reaches every hearth, and pulled it in a drill — against a named engine, with a clock — until stopping the hall inside the hour was a thing they had done, not a thing they owned.

The question it forces: We can build and buy agents by the fleet. But can we stop one? For each material agent, do we have a cord we have actually pulled — one that reaches it everywhere it runs, one whose far end is in our own hand, one measured by a real drill — or are we among the 94 in 100 standing in a louder and louder engine hall with a hand closed on air?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Stop-Cord

Every prior control governs what the agent is, does, serves, spends or stands on; the stop-cord governs whether you can halt it — and a control you have never exercised is not a control. The product: CrowdStrike's kill switch acts first at the AI gateway, with endpoint termination planned — but an agent with a local model, a cached credential or a SaaS seat may survive a gateway cut, so endpoint, identity and SaaS enforcement must be part of one pull; and a vendor's kill switch is a dependency. The rule: the HKMA's register-and-intervention proposal starts with an inventory — you cannot stop what you have not counted — and ties the halt to a named human owner. The gap: the difference between the 6% and the 94% is not whether they own a kill switch but whether they have pulled one — measured the time-to-halt, found the agents still running, and fixed them. The drill is the control; the purchase is only its raw material.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Write the register. You cannot stop what you have not counted, and 76% don't fully govern their non-human identities (Netwrix 2026). Produce a register of your material AI agents — for each: what it is, who owns it, what it can reach, and exactly how it would be halted. Report the share of production agents on the register with a named owner and a documented halt method — and the number you could not account for.

2. Build a cord that reaches. CrowdStrike's switch acts first at the gateway, with endpoint termination planned — a real control with a real boundary. For your top few material agents, map every place each one runs (gateway, endpoint, identity, SaaS) and verify a single intervention stops it at all of them. Report the share for which one pull provably halts them everywhere, and keep the far end in your own hand.

3. Pull the cord in a drill. Only 6% could cut off a rogue agent within an hour, while 65% already had an incident and 97% expect a material one within 12 months. Pick one critical agent and run a timed kill-switch drill — halt it against the clock, measure the time-to-halt, record what broke and whether anything kept running. Report the measured time-to-halt and a dated plan to drill the next two.
