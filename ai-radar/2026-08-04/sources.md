Claims sourced here (as reported): From 2 August 2026 the European Commission's AI Office, together with national competent authorities (approximately 24 designated), began actively auditing AI systems and exercising enforcement powers over general-purpose AI models and prohibited practices — requesting technical documentation, evaluating models, requiring corrective measures, restricting availability and issuing fines. On 4 August 2026, France's data protection authority (CNIL) issued formal information requests to 14 financial institutions operating credit-scoring algorithms (an Annex III high-risk use), demanding the Article 11 technical documentation required for high-risk systems; three institutions requested extensions, which CNIL denied, citing the roughly two-year preparation window since the Act passed in 2024. The penalty ceiling is the higher of €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover (Article 99), with €7.5 million or 1.5% for supplying incorrect, incomplete or misleading information. Deployers of high-risk systems must retain the automatically-generated logs for at least six months (Article 26). The CNIL specifics are relayed from secondary August 2026 coverage as reported; readers requiring a primary source should consult the CNIL directly.
