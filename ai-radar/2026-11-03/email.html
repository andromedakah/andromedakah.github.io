The Hallmark

Tuesday, 3 November 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

In four weeks — 2 December 2026 — the EU AI Act's Article 50(2) marking obligation closes its grace period, and generative AI must mark its output in a machine-readable, robust, interoperable way. "The Omnibus delayed everything" is a mis-filed deadline: the Omnibus moved high-risk to 2 December 2027, but Article 50 was never touched. Mark what you make, read what you receive, file the right date.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from governing the servant and owning the ground to the mark you must strike into the work your machines make — because the law just set a date on it, and a widespread misreading is quietly mis-filing it.

1. The deadline — machine-readable marking by 2 December 2026. Under Article 50(2), providers of generative AI must mark synthetic output (text, image, audio, video) in a machine-readable, detectable way — effective, reliable, robust, interoperable — and pair it with a working detector. Most of Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026; the grace period for generative systems placed on the market before then ends 2 December 2026 — about four weeks out (European Commission AI Act FAQ; Baker Botts; Cookiebot, as reported).

2. The misreading — the Omnibus delayed high-risk, NOT Article 50. The Digital Omnibus (in force 27 July 2026) postponed stand-alone high-risk Annex III obligations to 2 December 2027, but did not amend Article 50 — disclosure ("you are dealing with AI") and deepfake labelling have applied since 2 August 2026 (Gibson Dunn; Jones Walker; Legalithm, as reported). "It's all delayed to 2027" is a mis-filed deadline, and the duty reaches providers and deployers outside the EU wherever output is used in the Union.

3. The answer — mark what you make, read what you receive, file the right date. Embed a robust machine-readable mark in the work itself (not only a strippable manifest) and prove a detector reads it; treat deepfake/AI-content labelling as your own deployer duty; and correct the "delayed to 2027" memo — Article 50(2) lands on 2 December 2026, with penalties to a reported €7.5M / 1.5% of global turnover.

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation governed the servant and the ground — and the provenance kept the papers that trace the work. The hallmark is the mark struck into the work itself, so the world can tell machine-made from hand-made even when the papers are gone. The law set the date (2 December 2026) and the "Omnibus delayed everything" rumour is the error that catches those who believed it. Strike the mark into the metal, not onto a label that peels; and file the near December, not the far one.

2 · Allegory — The Hallmark

A great house had made its servants excellent, bought the ground beneath them, and learned to keep honest papers — a provenance folded in beside every thing of worth it shipped. But papers have one weakness no honesty can cure: they can be separated from the thing. A buyer three towns over crops the picture, melts the cup, copies the copy — and the folded paper slips out and is lost, and now a true thing and a false thing look alike in the hand. For a hundred generations the silver trades had known the remedy, and it was not better paper: a hallmark struck into the metal itself, that stayed through every melting and polishing, so any stranger, the papers long gone, could still read from the body of the thing: this is true metal, and this is the house that made it.

A magistrate rode in with a plain ordinance and a date: every house making wares by the new machines must strike into each a mark the machines made it — in the metal, not on a label; surviving melting and polishing; readable by any assayer's glass — and those already making such wares have four weeks to bring old stock into line. He named the day: the second of December. Then the valley made its lazy mistake. Weeks earlier the same office had granted the heaviest trades an extra year, to a December two winters off. Hearing "an extra year," the taverns agreed "everything's pushed to the far December" — but the extra year was only for the heavy trades, and the marking of the machine-wares had never been moved at all. The houses that filed it under "not until two winters hence" had mislaid the one deadline that was four weeks away.

So the wise houses read the ordinance twice and found three duties: mark what you make (strike the mark into the metal, not a paper tag that slips off at the first polishing — then hand it to an assayer and watch the glass find it); read what you receive (the ordinance bound sellers too — a house passing a machine-ware along as hand-work could not hide behind "the maker should have marked it"); and file the right date (pin the near December to the gate, and strike out the comfortable lie that the far one covered all). For a mark that lives only in a folded note beside the work is not a hallmark; it is a provenance wearing a hallmark's name, and it fails in the very moment it was made for — when the work is cut loose from its papers and sent out alone.

The question it forces: We can tell our own house what our machines made — but can an outsider? For every generative system we ship or operate, is there a robust mark struck into the output itself, readable after a crop and a re-compression — or only a manifest that peels at the first handling? And have we filed the right date: do our clerks know marking lands on 2 December 2026, and that the Omnibus's "extra year" belongs to the high-risk regime, not to this?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Hallmark

A manifest beside the work is a provenance; a mark inside the work is a hallmark — only the second survives the cropping, re-compression and metadata-stripping that separates content from its papers in the wild. The deadline: Article 50(2) requires generative output marked in a machine-readable, detectable way (robust, interoperable "as far as technically feasible"), paired with a working detector — grace period ends 2 December 2026. Marking is not labelling, and neither alone is enough; robustness is the whole game (a watermark stripped by a crop fails when it's needed); interoperable means a standard (C2PA-class), not a house secret. The misreading: the Omnibus moved high-risk (Annex III) to 2 December 2027 but left Article 50 untouched — the deadlines are layered, not one 2027 date, and the duty is inbound (deployers must label deepfakes) as well as outbound, and extraterritorial. The answer: mark what you make (in-content signal + manifest, detector-verified), read what you receive (disclose as a deployer), file the right date (disclosure now; marking 2 Dec 2026; high-risk 2027).

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Mark what you make. Article 50(2) requires generative output marked in a machine-readable, robust, interoperable, detectable way; the grace period for pre-August systems ends 2 December 2026. Inventory every generative system you ship or operate, confirm each carries an in-content signal (watermark) and an interoperable manifest (C2PA-class), then hand a cropped, re-compressed copy to a detector and verify the mark still reads. Report the share of your generative output that is marked and survives a realistic handling test.

2. Read what you receive. Deepfake and AI-content labelling is a deployer duty a provider's mark does not discharge, and it reaches you wherever your output is used in the EU. Map where your house republishes, labels or relies on AI content, assign an owner for the visible disclosure, and build the detection path — because "the provider should have marked it" is not a defence. Report the share of inbound/republished AI content carrying a disclosure you control.

3. File the right date. The Omnibus moved high-risk to 2 December 2027; it did not touch Article 50, whose disclosure duties are live and whose marking grace period ends 2 December 2026. Re-issue your AI Act compliance calendar as a layered timeline — disclosure "in force," marking "2 Dec 2026," high-risk "2 Dec 2027" — and brief legal, product and comms so no one ships on the assumption that everything waits until 2027.
