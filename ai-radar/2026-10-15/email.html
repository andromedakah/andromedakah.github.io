1 · Executive Summary

Three weeks ago the axis moved from the servant to the ground — the model had commoditized into a swappable stove, the lock-in migrated to the foundation. This morning a newcomer proves it from the other side.

1. The event — a fourth lab reaches the frontier, and the newcomer draws a shrug. On 12 October 2026 Meta's Superintelligence Lab shipped Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full — its first frontier-class models, which Meta's own card places comparable to Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra, headlining a self-built MemoryBench (Meta; TechCrunch; The AI Insider, as reported). But independent testing was cooler — Artificial Analysis scored Wonder Full 56 and Wonder 52, a notch behind Opus 5.5 at 58 — at a higher reported $4/$20 per million tokens. The coverage caught it: "Scores Big and Delivers Little" (DEV); "Reaches the Frontier. Can Meta Sell It?" (Futurum, as reported). Reaching the frontier no longer wins.

2. The signal — capability is table stakes; distribution is the moat. The same week, Google's Gemini 4 Argon went GA inside GitHub Copilot (13 Oct, launch $2/$10 then $4/$20) — a frontier model served where developers already work; Atlassian put OpenAI's models inside Rovo via the Teamwork Graph; AMD's Helion routes across models; Cisco added agents to Webex (as reported). One lab was asked "can you sell it?"; the other simply appeared in millions of workflows. The moat moved from the model to the kitchen it is served in.

3. The answer — own the floor, keep the larder, light a second fire. Four-plus labs at parity is a buyer's gift — more leverage, more price pressure — collected only by the house that can audition any new model behind an interface it owns, so every entrant is a component, not a migration. Governance note: the EU's Digital Omnibus deferred the high-risk AI Act deadlines (standalone to 2 Dec 2027, embedded products to 2 Aug 2028) while GPAI enforcement stays live since 2 Aug 2026 (as reported).

Bottom line: the foundation taught that the model is a swappable stove and the lock-in lives in the ground beneath. Meta, with all its might, reached the frontier — and was asked "can you sell it?" Because the frontier is crowded (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, now Meta), capability is the entry fee, and the prize goes to whoever already stands in the customer's kitchen, through a channel they already use and a floor they already poured. Own the floor, keep the larder, light a second fire — so a crowded frontier is a banquet you shop, not a trap you build around yourself.

2 · Allegory — The Latecomer

A great valley of kitchens had learned, over a long season, to name its hands, keep its book, own the ground its house stood on. Then, over the hills, came a latecomer — a great and famous house, richer than any in the valley, that had never sold a stove before. It wheeled its very first stove into the market square, with a placard that read as fine as the best stoves in the valley, and waited for the square to gasp.

The square did not gasp. It shrugged at a giant. The cooks looked the magnificent stove up and down: "A fine stove. About as good as the ones we already have." The independent taster, who scored by the eating rather than the placard, set it a notch below the best — and it cost more. One wit chalked on the wall: "Scores big on the placard; delivers little in the pot." Another only asked the question that undid the newcomer: "It reached the frontier — but can it sell it?"

Meanwhile a rival maker, who already supplied the valley's largest guild, wheeled no stove into the square at all. It simply built its newest frontier stove into the workbenches the carpenters already used — so a man reached for his plane and found the fire already lit at his elbow. By supper it cooked in ten thousand kitchens, unchosen, while the latecomer's stove stood admired and unbought. The lesson: reaching the frontier no longer won anything; "as good as the best" had become the toll to enter, not the prize. What won the kitchen was the door you already walked through and the floor you already stood on.

The question it forces: Four makers can now build a stove as good as the next. When the latecomer's dear one, the incumbent's cheap one, and the one delivered straight to our bench all arrive in the same week, can we taste each on its merits and swap in an afternoon — because we own the floor, keep the larder, and have a second fire lit — or will we read all this choice as a reason to pick one convenient maker and settle in, turning the buyer's banquet back into the maker's trap with our own hands?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Latecomer

When a company with Meta's resources can reach the frontier and still be asked "can you sell it?", capability has stopped being the differentiator — "as good as the best" is the toll to enter the market, not the prize for winning it. The event: benchmark leadership has decoupled from adoption (select on cost-per-useful-task against your own evals, not leaderboards); vendor model-card claims and independent measurement diverge (card "comparable"; index a notch behind — run your own evaluation); a newer entrant priced above the pack ($4/$20) must justify itself on fit, not frontier status. The signal: distribution now beats capability — a frontier model served into an existing workflow (Gemini 4 Argon in Copilot, OpenAI in Rovo) appears in millions of kitchens unchosen, but a capability bundled into one vendor's tool may be one you cannot disable or replace independently — the channel is the new lock-in. The answer: own the floor (a model-routing gateway you control), keep the larder (portable data, context, evals so adoption is a config change), and light a second fire (a qualified alternate, tested under load) — then four-plus labs at parity is a price war you win, not a menu that paralyses you.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Own the floor. Four-plus labs at parity, and a newcomer reached the frontier only to be asked "can you sell it?" — proof capability is the entry toll, not the prize. Route your top AI workloads through a gateway/abstraction you control, so adopting any new model (Muse Wonder, Gemini 4 Argon, the next entrant) is a config change audited against your own evals, and route per task on value across at least two vendors. Report the share of critical AI spend behind an abstraction you own versus wired into one vendor's tool.

2. Keep the larder. Meta's card said "comparable to the best"; independent testing put it a notch behind, at a higher price — the gap between the placard and the eating. Stand up a task-specific eval suite you own and run it against every candidate, and inventory where prompts, context and retrieval data live so adoption is a config change, not a migration. Report the share of your AI knowledge assets that are portable by design.

3. Light a second fire — and audit the convenient door. Frontier models are arriving bundled into tools you already use (Gemini 4 Argon in Copilot, OpenAI in Rovo) — the easiest adoption and the easiest capture. Qualify and actually run one alternate supplier under load, and map every place a frontier capability enters bundled into a vendor's tool — confirming for each that you can disable or replace it. Report the measured time-to-switch and the count of bundled AI channels confirmed swappable versus load-bearing.
