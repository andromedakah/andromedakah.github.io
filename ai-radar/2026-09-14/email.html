The Looking-Glass

1 · Executive Summary

Most organisations answer "how do we know we're whole?" by consulting their own confidence — the least reliable instrument in the house, and this week's data shows it runs backwards.

1. The event — the flattering glass. AvePoint's "State of AI 2026" (third annual; Osterman Research; 750 global IT leaders in financial services, healthcare and government) finds 88.4% of organisations suffered an AI-agent-related breach and 89.5% a GenAI breach in the past 12 months (up from 75.1% in 2025); top incidents data leakage (50.1%) and manipulation by malicious/untrusted inputs (49.6%). The paradox: 82.7% feel confident they can prevent unauthorised access, yet nearly nine in ten of them were breached — and the more confident the firm, the more likely it had already been breached (62–72% of high-confidence firms hit). Simberkoff: "the biggest gaps are visibility, control, and trust" (AvePoint / Osterman Research; PRNewswire, as reported).

2. The reckoning — the blind spot is widening. Organisations unable to detect unsanctioned AI use nearly tripled (6.3% → 17.6%); 21.1% cannot account for unsanctioned agent activity; 46.9% of staff use agents weekly. A fresh report counts 17,800 unverified AI add-ons across 6.7M installs, some impersonating Anthropic and OpenAI and able to run code. You cannot assure what you cannot see (AvePoint; add-on report, as reported).

3. The answer — measure by an honest light. Instrument continuous visibility into every agent (sanctioned and shadow), its reach and its actions; make independent red-teaming and breach evidence the standard of proof; govern and revoke at one control point (MCP EMA, now stable, adopted by Anthropic, Microsoft, Okta). Tools are shipping — CrowdStrike launched Falcon Guardian to discover known and shadow agents and block unapproved ones. Confidence is not evidence.

Bottom line: the locusts were the swarm at your walls; the looking-glass is the instrument you judge yourself by. The danger is feeling safe because you've stopped looking — and this week the feeling runs backwards: the surer the house, the likelier the moth is already in the wool. Stop grading yourselves; be shown, by a light you don't control, exactly where you're not yet whole.

2 · Allegory — The Looking-Glass

A great house was famed for how well it kept itself — because a tall, flattering looking-glass hung by the door, and every morning the household dressed before it and asked, "Are we safe? Is all in order?" The glass always answered the same: you are the soundest, safest house in the valley. But the glass showed only the front, in a flattering light, at the one hour they chose to look — never the back stairs off the latch, the damp cellar, or the wardrobe where the moth had been at work in the good wool. And so a terrible pattern set in: the mornings the house felt surest were exactly the mornings it had stopped checking the latches and the linings at all. When the reckoning came — ruined coats, an open door a stranger had strolled through — the house was astonished: "but we were so sure." That was the whole of the problem.

The wise houses learned three things. Confidence is not a measurement — a house that grades itself always marks itself kind. You cannot keep what you cannot see — new rooms and hired hands had been added faster than anyone could map, some wearing trusted liveries but taking orders from strangers over the wall. And the only proof worth the ledger is proof from outside the house. So they threw out the flattering glass and carried an honest, outside light into the cellar and the back stairs at hours they did not choose, kept a true census of every room and hand, and held the mirror-makers to account.

The question it forces: When we tell the board "we're confident we can prevent unauthorised access," are we reporting a measurement or a feeling — and do we know that this season the feeling runs backwards? Can we see, tonight, every agent running — including the ones wearing a trusted name? And are we willing to be shown, by a light we don't control, exactly where we're not yet whole — or would we rather keep the flattering glass, and be astonished on the morning the moth is found in the wool?

3 · Technical — From the Locusts to the Looking-Glass

Self-assessed confidence is not a signal about your security posture — this week's data shows it is an inverse one — so assurance migrates from "we believe we're secure" to "we can measure, from outside, that we are." The event: confidence tracks inversely with breach, so any assurance process that ends in a confidence statement optimises the wrong variable; the mechanism is a degrading blind spot as adoption outruns observability. The reckoning: against an estate you cannot fully see — with shadow add-ons impersonating your own vendors — a confidence report is anti-evidence, a reading of the observer's comfort, not the system's state. The answer: discover every agent (sanctioned and shadow), trace prompts through tool calls to downstream actions, make red-teaming and breach telemetry the proof, and govern and revoke at one point (MCP EMA, now stable — Anthropic, Microsoft, Okta). 5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Stop grading yourselves — instrument the truth. Replace every "are we confident we're secure?" assurance step with an instrumented reading: continuous discovery of every agent (sanctioned and shadow), a map of its reach, and a trace of its actions — deploy agent-visibility tooling (e.g. Falcon Guardian) where you have none. Report the share of agents you can positively see versus your best estimate of the true total.

2. Make outside evidence the standard — light the blind spot first. Commission independent red-teaming and third-party audit of your highest-risk AI, and report breach-and-near-miss telemetry, not self-attestation; prioritise the shadow estate — unsanctioned tools and third-party "skills," especially any impersonating your own vendors. Treat rising confidence with falling visibility as a board-level red flag.

3. Govern the light at one point — help write the honest measure. Route every agent through one control point where you can see, authorise and revoke it (MCP EMA), and close the containment floor (60% cannot terminate a misbehaving agent). Engage the emerging external standard (the industry AI standards body; the EU AI Act's live regime) so your measure of "whole" is one an outsider would accept.
