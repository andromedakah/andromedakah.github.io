## The story — confidence is not a measurement: the more confident an organisation is in its AI security, the more likely it has already been breached; so measure by an honest, outside light

Claims sourced here (as reported): AvePoint's third annual "State of AI 2026" report (conducted with Osterman Research; survey of 750 global IT leaders across financial services, healthcare and government) found that 88.4% of organisations suffered at least one AI-agent-related security breach in the past 12 months and 89.5% a GenAI breach (up from 75.1% in 2025); the most common incidents were data leakage (50.1%) and manipulation by malicious or untrusted inputs (49.6%); 82.7% of leaders are confident they can prevent unauthorised data access, yet nearly nine in ten of those confident organisations were breached, and the more confident the organisation, the more likely it had already been breached (62–72% of even high-confidence organisations logged AI-related unauthorised-access incidents). The share of organisations unable to detect whether staff use unsanctioned AI nearly tripled, from 6.3% (2025) to 17.6% (2026); 21.1% cannot account for unsanctioned agent activity; 46.9% of employees now rely on AI agents daily or weekly; and 95.5% took at least one mitigating action, most commonly human-in-the-loop controls. Dana Louise Simberkoff (Chief Risk, Privacy and Information Security Officer, AvePoint) is quoted that "the biggest gaps are visibility, control, and trust — many organizations still don't know which agents are operating, what permissions they have, or how they're making decisions." Carried here as "the flattering glass": self-assessed confidence is not a measure of security and this window it is an inverse one.
