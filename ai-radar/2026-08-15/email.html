The Last Mile

Saturday, 15 August 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

Yesterday the value moved from the engine to the pilotage — the context and reliability that bring a model into your harbor. Today the market drew the map one mile further, to the layer everyone underestimates because it's unglamorous: distribution. On 13 August, OpenAI made two distribution moves in a single day — it poured its frontier models into IBM Consulting's ~150,000-person delivery army (a dedicated "OpenAI Practice," forward-deployed engineers into regulated clients), and it hired a hardened enterprise-sales CRO, Dali Rajic. The company with a billion weekly users and two million businesses is not short of a brain, or even pilotage. It is short of the last mile. The board's question: who actually carries AI into our operation — and do we own that mile, or rent it?

1 · Executive Summary

For a month this radar has argued the model is a commodity and the durable advantage is the layer you own around it. Yesterday it went one layer up from the engine to the pilotage — context and reliability. Today the market drew the map one mile further, to the part everyone underestimates: distribution.

1. Distribution beat product — and the model maker admitted it. On 13 August, OpenAI announced a strategic partnership with IBM to embed its frontier models — GPT-5.6, Codex, ChatGPT Work — into IBM Consulting Advantage, standing up a dedicated "OpenAI Practice," certifying tens of thousands of IBM's ~150,000 consultants, and sending forward-deployed engineers into financial services, government, telecom and retail. The same day it named Dali Rajic (ex-Wiz President & COO, ex-Zscaler) as Chief Revenue Officer — its second in under a year. The company with a billion weekly users and two million businesses rented someone else's channel and bought a distribution executive on a single day.

2. The gap that matters is pilot-to-production, and it's a delivery problem. OpenAI is "deepening partnerships with consulting giants to push enterprise AI beyond pilot." The wall isn't capability; it's the unglamorous work of redesigning a real workflow, in a regulated shop, and making it stick — done by people and process, not weights. Forrester's read: IBM "validates OpenAI's push-pull-partner enterprise strategy."

3. The engine keeps commoditizing beneath the priced mile. On 13 August alone, Gemini 3.7 Flash, DeepSeek V4-Pro-0813 and GPT-5.6 Sol Ultrafast all shipped; open-weight GLM-5.3 followed on the 14th. Three frontier engines in a day. The trust layer holds: EU AI Act transparency live since 2 Aug; AITSC launched 12 Aug.

Bottom line: even a grounded, reliable model does not deliver itself. The scarce layer is distribution — the channel, the consultants, the forward-deployed engineers and the enterprise trust that carry AI into a regulated operation and make it stick. OpenAI just proved it by renting IBM's mile and buying a CRO in a single day. Decide, deliberately, who walks your last mile — because the finest engine and the finest pilot still deliver nothing if no one can carry them to your dock.

2 · Allegory — The Last Mile

Picture a valley famous for a hundred years for the finest cloth in the world. Its master weavers cared about the loom, and were sublime at it — every season the bolts came off finer and cheaper, until cloth that had dressed only kings could be had by any householder. But there is a cruel fact about a bolt of perfect cloth: in the weaver's own village, it clothes no one. Between the loom and the buyer's back lies a long, unglamorous distance — a road, a customs house, a city of strangers who never heard the weaver's name.

And so the valley learned who grew rich. Not the finest weaver — the great trading house that owned the roads and the pack-trains, kept a factor in every distant city, and had spent two generations earning the one thing no loom can weave: the trust of the buyer. It wove not a thread. It owned the last mile, and that mile was where the money lived. Then, this season, the most celebrated weaving-house of the age did two things on one day: it handed its whole vintage to that trading house to carry the last mile into the guarded warehouses of the banks and ministries — and it hired away the most feared road-master in the realm to run its own wagons. A confession: we weave better than anyone alive, and it still isn't enough to reach the buyer's door. And a customs authority now stood at the gate, passing no unlabeled bolt or unlicensed carter.

The question it forces: the most celebrated model maker on earth rented another firm's ~150,000-person delivery army and hired a new revenue chief on the same day — because a billion users is not a deployment that sticks. For each AI bet, who walks the last mile into our operation: our own people, an integrator, or the vendor's forward-deployed engineers — and have we decided it on purpose, priced it, and kept the trust and the papers in our own hands?

3 · Technical — Own the Last Mile, Not Just the Engine

The engine — abundant, three-in-a-day: Gemini 3.7 Flash, DeepSeek V4-Pro-0813 and GPT-5.6 Sol Ultrafast all shipped 13 Aug; open-weight GLM-5.3 on the 14th, atop Opus 5, Grok 4.6 ($2/$6), Kimi K3. The rails — agent infrastructure (free): MCP's 2026-07-28 spec went stateless, ~500M SDK downloads/month, TS & Python each past 1B. The pilotage — context + reliability (priced): Databricks $190B, yesterday. The last mile — distribution (scarce): IBM × OpenAI (GPT-5.6, Codex, ChatGPT Work in IBM Consulting Advantage; ~150,000 consultants; forward-deployed engineers) plus OpenAI's CRO hire. Value has decoupled from the engine and attached to the mile to the customer.

Assume the best model deploys itselfResource the delivery: people, process, change management.
Judge a bet by the model's benchmarkJudge it by production deployments that stick, per unit cost.
Let the vendor own the client relationshipKeep the trust and the relationship in your own hands.
Treat pilot-to-production as a model upgradeTreat it as a distribution problem — because it is.

On legacy: name the carrier for every initiative (internal team, integrator, or the vendor's forward-deployed engineers) and price it; keep the trust and the know-how in your own hands; and license and log every party that walks the mile (disclose, label, audit). Keep the engine swappable underneath — the brain is the commodity, the delivery is the moat.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Name the carrier for every AI bet, and price the mile. For each material initiative, write down who actually carries it into production — internal team, integrator, or the vendor's forward-deployed engineers — and put a cost and an owner against that mile, reported to the board beside the model choice. OpenAI rented IBM's ~150,000-consultant channel and hired a CRO on a single day; an unnamed, unfunded last mile is why your pilots never reach production.

2. Keep the trust, the relationship and the know-how in your own hands. Where you use a partner's delivery force, structure it so the institutional knowledge and the client relationship stay yours. Don't let a vendor quietly become the trusted face of AI to your own customers and regulators. Rent the mile if you must, but own the map.

3. License and log every party that walks the mile — it's already law. Whoever carries AI into production — vendor, integrator or your own staff — falls under the EU AI Act's disclosure and labeling duties, and the audit log must name them. Track the AITSC standard as it forms. Make the last mile auditable before it touches the cargo.
