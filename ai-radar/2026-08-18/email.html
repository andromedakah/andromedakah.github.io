The Deputy

Tuesday, 18 August 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

Yesterday the radar counted the crossings — 95% of pilots never reach the far bank. Today it looks at the ones that did, and finds they've changed what AI is inside the company: no longer a clever adviser you consult, but a deputy that acts. OpenAI's 13 August report shows Codex — an agent — now generates 64% of combined enterprise output tokens, and frontier firms use 8.3× as much AI per user as typical firms (up from 2.6× in January). The moment AI stops advising and starts acting, the question flips: not "is the answer good?" but "who is this agent, what may it touch, and can we prove what it did?" The board's question: for every agent acting in our name, do we know its identity, its mandate and its audit trail — or have we pinned our seal on servants we cannot name?

1 · Executive Summary

For a month this radar has argued the model is a commodity and the moat is the layer you own; yesterday it showed how few finish the crossing to value. Today the map turns to what changes for the 5% who cross: AI has shifted from assistance to execution — from a thing that suggests to a thing that acts — and that shift, not the next model, is what should reorganize your controls.

1. The majority of enterprise AI output is now the agent's. OpenAI's 13 August report, "From assistance to execution," reports that as of June, Codex generated 64% of combined Codex-and-ChatGPT output tokens among enterprise customers, and "frontier firms" (top 10% by usage) now produce 8.3× as many output tokens per user as typical firms — up from a 2.6× gap in January. Execution is spreading fastest outside engineering: Codex weekly users up 108× in legal, 41× in sales, 26× in marketing since February, vs 5× in engineering.

2. When AI acts, identity becomes the control plane. An acting agent is a non-human identity with real privileges — and they already outnumber humans ~45:1 (up to 144:1 in cloud-native estates), each potentially holding CRM, email, cloud and payment credentials at once. That is why the key enterprise release is not a model but an identity layer: Google's Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform gives each agent a unique identity, tokens cryptographically bound to X.509 certs (unreplayable), no impersonation, no long-lived keys, per-action authorization and full audit.

3. The engine keeps commoditizing — and getting built for action. Gemini 3.7 Flash for agents (13 Aug), GPT-5.6 Sol Ultrafast at ~750 tok/s (17 Aug), open-weight GLM-5.3/Qwen3.8 (14 Aug), Grok 4.6 at $2/$6 (12 Aug), Grok 3 retired (15 Aug). The trust gate is live: EU AI Act transparency since 2 Aug (disclose AI, label, log the act); AITSC launched 12 Aug.

Bottom line: the model is a commodity, the moat is the layer you own — and the layer now doing the work is the acting agent. The shift from assistance to execution moves AI from advice you evaluate to action taken in your name, and makes agent identity, mandate and audit the control plane that matters. Name every deputy, bound what it may do, keep the ledger — because you can no longer un-take an action your agent has already performed.

2 · Allegory — The Deputy

For as long as the great house could remember, it had kept counselors. They gave advice; the master weighed it, and then he acted — or did not. A counselor's power ended at his lips: his worst counsel, declined, cost nothing. This season the house did something new. It pinned a deputy's badge on a servant and sent him out to act in the master's name — to buy, to sign, to open the counting-house — and soon there were forty deputies wearing the badge for every one member of the family, each able to touch the strongroom and the seal at once. Most of the day's business was now done not by the family but by badged servants on the family's authority.

And the moment the badge goes on, every question changes. A counselor you judge by his wisdom; a deputy you cannot, because by the time you hear of it the deed is already done. So the questions that keep a house of deputies safe are not of wisdom but of identity, mandate and record: which deputy is this, and can a stranger wear a borrowed badge? What did we authorize this one to do? And is there a ledger of every act done in our name? The wise houses struck badges that could not be forged or lent, wrote each mandate narrow and plain, and kept a ledger of every act — while a magistrate at the gate now required each deputy to declare himself and be logged.

The question it forces: the majority of our AI's output is no longer advice we weigh — it is work already performed by agents acting in our name, and they now outnumber our people many times over. For every one, can we say who it is beyond forgery, what we authorized it to touch, and produce the complete ledger of what it has already done — or have we pinned our seal on servants we cannot name, bound or account for?

3 · Technical — From Assistance to Execution

The engine — built for action: Gemini 3.7 Flash for agents (13 Aug), GPT-5.6 Sol Ultrafast ~750 tok/s (17 Aug), open-weight GLM-5.3/Qwen3.8 (14 Aug), Grok 4.6 $2/$6 (12 Aug), Grok 3 retired (15 Aug). The acting agent — where identity becomes the control plane: Codex is 64% of enterprise output tokens; agents are a new non-human identity (~45:1 vs humans, up to 144:1 in cloud). The control that answers it is identity, not intelligence — unique per-agent identity, least privilege authorized per action, and a full audit ledger with one-move revocation. That is the shape of Google's Agent Identity, and the frontier control for an enterprise whose agents now act.

On legacy: agents inherit human-shaped access — a shared login, a broad role, a long-lived key — then act at machine speed through it. Name every agent (unique, non-forgeable identity, no shared keys); bound every mandate (least privilege, authorize per action, gate the consequential steps); keep the ledger (log every tool call, make it inspectable and the access revocable in one move). Keep the engine swappable underneath — the brain is the commodity, the governance of the acting agent is the moat.

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Inventory every acting agent and give each a real identity — no shared keys. List every agent that can do something (not just answer): what it touches, whose credentials it uses, who owns it. Replace shared logins and long-lived service-account keys with a unique, non-forgeable, revocable identity per agent. Most of your AI output is already an agent's work — this is bringing your largest population of actors under management.

2. Bound the mandate and gate the consequential act. Scope access to least privilege and authorize per action, not per session — and put a human hand on the steps that move money, change a system of record, or touch protected data. Grade agents on staying inside their envelope under real conditions, not a clean demo.

3. Keep the ledger and rehearse the kill switch. Log every tool call, permission and change; make it inspectable; prove you can revoke an agent's access in one move; disclose and label agent action under the EU AI Act. Then run the drill: an agent acted outside its mandate at 3 a.m. — how fast can we say exactly what it did and stop it?
