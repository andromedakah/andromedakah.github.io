## The story — after a week building the agentic workforce, the board's question becomes counting it: you cannot govern a digital workforce you have never counted, and most enterprises cannot yet inventory the agents they run

- Gartner Newsroom, "Gartner Predicts 40% of Enterprise Apps Will Feature Task-Specific AI Agents by 2026, Up from Less Than 5% in 2025" (26 August 2025) — https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-26-gartner-predicts-40-percent-of-enterprise-apps-will-feature-task-specific-ai-agents-by-2026-up-from-less-than-5-percent-in-2025

Claims sourced here (as reported): Gartner forecasts that by 2028 the average Global Fortune 500 enterprise will run more than 150,000 AI agents, up from fewer than 15 in 2025; this figure was recirculated this week across the trade press (SAP News, CIO, Reworked) explicitly as a governance alarm — "AI agent sprawl is now a board-level issue." Gartner's own characterization: "Organizations don't intentionally create agent sprawl. Interestingly, it is an after-effect of successful AI adoption in the company, which also drives governance challenges," and (Max Goss, Senior Director Analyst) "Organizations need to find a balance where they can govern agents and manage sprawl, but also safely empower employees to innovate with these tools." Related Gartner forecast (Aug 2025): ~40% of enterprise apps will ship a task-specific AI agent by end of 2026, up from <5% in 2025. Microsoft (Feb 2026): ~80% of the Fortune 500 already run active AI agents. Carried here as the "population": agent creation is decentralized to every team and app, so the workforce arrives faster than any central headcount.

### The counting gap — the alarm itself: nobody holds the roll

- SAP News Center (as above) — the SAP LeanIX finding that fewer than half of organizations have visibility into an inventory of AI agents, and only ~13% believe they have the right governance in place.
- Kore.ai, "AI agent sprawl: What it is, why it happens, and how to stop it" — https://www.kore.ai/blog/what-is-ai-agent-sprawl
- Digital Thought Disruption, "The Agent Sprawl Crisis: How CIOs Should Register, Consolidate, and Retire Digital Workers" (20 August 2026) — https://digitalthoughtdisruption.com/2026/08/20/ai-agent-sprawl-lifecycle-management/
- CloudFuze, "150,000 Agents by 2028: What Gartner's Forecast Means for IT" — https://www.cloudfuze.com/ai-agent-lifecycle-management

Claims sourced here (as reported): only about 13% of organizations believe they have the right AI agent governance in place; per an SAP LeanIX survey, fewer than half can produce an inventory of the agents they already run. Agent sprawl is defined as agents being created, deployed or connected across systems faster than the enterprise can inventory them, assign ownership, control permissions, monitor behavior, and retire them. SAP frames a "three-way squeeze" on tech leaders: the workforce asking for more agent permissions, the CFO flagging token spend as too expensive, and the CEO demanding innovation and results. Carried here as the "counting gap": the population is arriving faster than the register.

### The register — the vendor response: name, own, scope, retire

- Okta Newsroom, "AI Agents at Work 2026: Securing the agentic enterprise" — https://www.okta.com/newsroom/articles/ai-agents-at-work-2026-agentic-enterprise-security/
- Okta, "Okta for AI Agents | Govern Agentic Identity" — https://www.okta.com/products/govern-ai-agent-identity/
- Digital Applied, "Run an Internal Agent Registry Before It Hurts: Template" — https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/internal-agent-registry-governance-template-2026

Claims sourced here (as reported): Okta's "AI Agents at Work 2026" report (based on Okta Platform data and a survey of more than 200 executives) finds 91% of organizations already using AI agents but only ~10% with a well-developed strategy or roadmap for managing the non-human identities (NHIs) those agents carry; 53% report an established AI-deployment strategy; 65% of executives say their AI usage policies are very clear versus only 43% of knowledge workers. The report's thesis: "agentic AI readiness is identity readiness." By mid-2026, Microsoft, AWS and Google Cloud had each introduced or expanded agent registry, identity, governance and lifecycle capabilities; a centralized registry acts as a single source of truth for all agents — sanctioned, third-party and shadow. Carried here as the "register": the census as an enforced control plane — no agent runs unless it is registered, owned and scoped.

### The bill — ROI reality, why the board is counting now

- Forbes (Sandy Carter), "Most Enterprise AI Is Live. Half Of Companies Can't Prove It Works" (6 August 2026) — https://www.forbes.com/sites/sandycarter/2026/08/06/enterprise-ai-roi-production-survey/
- Futurum Group, "Enterprise AI ROI Shifts as Agentic Priorities Surge" — https://futurumgroup.com/press-release/enterprise-ai-roi-shifts-as-agentic-priorities-surge/

Claims sourced here (as reported): survey coverage (Forbes/Sandy Carter, Aug 2026) reports that 74% of the world's largest enterprises run at least one AI solution in production, 52% of executives report deploying AI agents in production and 39% have more than ten agents running — yet half of production-stage companies cannot say whether any of it worked. Google Cloud's "ROI of AI" report is cited for 74% of production deployers achieving ROI within the first year, while ~22% of agent deployments report negative ROI at 12 months and only ~12% of CEOs report both revenue gain and cost reduction. The Futurum 1H 2026 Enterprise Software Decision Maker Survey reports direct financial impact nearly doubling to 21.7% as the primary ROI metric. Carried here as the "bill": uncounted agents are uncounted cost and uncounted liability — the census is how you find the negative-ROI and finished-task agents and retire them. (These figures rest partly on survey summaries relayed secondhand and are flagged accordingly.)

Carried as context: a workforce you cannot name is a workforce you cannot log to the Article-12 standard.
