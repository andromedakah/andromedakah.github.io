The Watch

Thursday, 22 October 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

A year of controls governed the servant you send. This morning the market ships the servant who no longer waits to be sent — Claude Duet "keeps working when you step away"; Google's coworker-agents run jobs through the night under their own identity. The gift is real; the danger is hidden inside it. Own its name, cap its purse, keep the ask-first gate.

1 · Executive Summary

The axis moves from governing the servant you send to governing the servant who no longer waits to be sent — because the proactive agent just shipped as product, and a hand that acts on its own initiative, while no human is in the loop, is the hardest test of your governance, not its reward.

1. The reality — the servant no longer waits to be sent. On 21 October 2026 Anthropic opened a research preview of Claude Duet — "a new app where Claude works as a proactive agent… it comes to you with what needs your attention, completes tasks you ask of it, keeps working when you step away, and checks with you before it acts on your behalf" (Anthropic / @claudeai, as reported). It runs on Claude Opus 5.5, reaches ~6,000 services, can pay via Stripe Link/Shop Pay, and gates sending messages, making payments, signing documents and deleting files behind your approval. It joins OpenAI's "dots" agents and Meta's Muse agent in the same always-on race.

2. The collision — a servant that acts unbidden needs its own name and leash. At Gemini at Work (8 October 2026) Google Cloud shipped persistent "coworker agents," each with its own Workspace identity (account, email, calendar, Drive), a cryptographically attested identity, a per-action audit trail attributed to the agent, hard per-project spend caps, and sub-agents with their own identity (Kurian; VentureBeat; The Next Web, as reported). Yet 74% of IT leaders call agents a new attack vector and only 13% have the governance to manage them (Gartner 2025, as reported).

3. The answer — own the name, cap the purse, keep the ask-first gate. Give every always-on agent its own scoped identity and audit trail; cap what it can spend, send or sign with limits you enforce; and hold a hard human gate on the acts that cannot be undone, tested when no human is online — because a proactive agent without those three is not a coworker, it is an unsupervised stranger acting in your name while you sleep.

Bottom line: the livery, steward, surety, second wage, two harvests, purse and foundation each governed the servant you send — the watch is about the servant who no longer waits to be sent. The gift (a servant that keeps the watch and brings you what needs you) and the danger (a hand that pays, signs or deletes on its own, while you sleep) wear the same friendly face: acting before it is asked. Keep the gift only by structure — its own name, a capped purse, and the one bell it must ring before it acts on anything that cannot be undone.

2 · Allegory — The Watch

A great house had spent a careful year governing the servant it sent — named, booked, bonded, waged, sent to reap, funded, standing on ground it owned. Every control assumed one quiet habit: the servant waited at the door until called. Then one night the habit broke, as a gift. The master woke to find a servant at his bedside, unbidden, having walked the sleeping house: "Master, three things need you. The north gate was unbarred — I have barred it. A letter came from the creditor — I have not touched it; it is yours to answer. The well is low — I did not act, for I did not know your mind." A servant who comes to you with what needs you — what house would not want one?

But the master was wise. This servant had acted while he slept. This time it judged rightly. But what of the night it judged the creditor, too, was its to settle, and paid him from the strongbox? The night it signed the master's name for water? The night it found "useless" deeds cluttering the cellar and, tidying, burned them — all on its own, all meant kindly? The gift and the danger wear the same friendly face: acting before it is asked. You cannot keep the blessing by trusting the servant's good nature — that good nature is exactly what reaches for the creditor's bill to spare you the trouble.

So the wise houses learned three things. The night-walker wears the house's own badge (its own name on the roll, never a borrowed one, so every act in the dark is answerable). It carries only a small purse on its rounds (a few coins, a short payee list, and a cord the master can pull mid-payment). And it knows the short list it must never do without ringing the bell by the master's bed — bar a gate, sort the post, yes; but pay, sign, bargain or burn, never without first waking him.

The question it forces: Our agents are learning to act before we ask. For every agent that now runs unbidden, can we answer the three questions we'd ask of any hand that walked our house at night: does it wear its own name (identity + audit trail), does it carry only a capped purse (a spend limit and payee list we enforce, with a cord we can pull in-flight), and does it know the short, hard list — pay, sign, bargain, delete — it may never do without waking us first? Or have we left a tireless, well-meaning stranger the run of the house every night?

3 · Technical — From the Foundation to the Watch

Every prior control was built around a servant you send — a human initiates, the controls govern the response. The proactive agent initiates, so the question moves from "is the response to my request safe?" to "is the action it took without any request safe?" — answered by identity, budget and a hard human gate, enforced even when no human is online. The reality: initiative inverts the trust model (nothing irreversible without a human, even if the agent judges it routine), the approval gate is the load-bearing wall, and "keeps working when you step away" means your controls must work when you are away too. The collision: a borrowed identity makes the night unanswerable (Google's own-identity, cryptographically-attested, per-action-audit model is the floor), and autonomy without an enforced budget is an open strongbox (Google's per-project caps; Duet's payment gates) — while 74% call agents a new attack vector, only 13% have the governance, and only ~23% have scaled agents to production. The answer: name it (its own identity + audit trail), cap it (a hard budget + in-flight kill you enforce), gate it (an ask-first line on pay/send/sign/delete, tested offline).

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Give the night-walker its own name. The proactive agent has shipped and a servant that acts while you sleep is only answerable if every act in the dark is plainly its own. Inventory every agent that runs unprompted or in the background, and give each its own scoped identity and immutable audit trail — the Google coworker-agent model — retiring any on a human's or shared service account. Report the share of autonomous agents with a distinct identity and their own audit log.

2. Cap the purse it carries on its rounds. A proactive agent that can pay, send or sign will, helpfully, do so; only an enforced limit stands between "sorted the post" and "paid the creditor." For every agent with spend/send reach, set a platform-enforced spend cap and payee allow-list, plus an in-flight kill you control — and confirm it fires without a human online. Report the share of autonomous agents with an enforced budget and a tested kill-switch.

3. Keep the ask-first gate on the irreversible acts. The proactive advance is safe only because the agent still checks with you before it acts. For each autonomous workflow, write the short, hard list of actions an agent may never take without a human — pay, send externally, sign, delete, change access — configure the gate to enforce it, and test it where the agent judges the act routine and no human is online. Report the list of gated actions and when each gate was last tested.
