## The story — the visibility blind spot: you cannot govern what you cannot see

### The measured blind spot — Snyk 2026 State of Agentic AI Adoption (covered 5 August 2026)

- Help Net Security, "Your enterprise AI footprint is about three times bigger than your model list" (5 August 2026) — https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/08/05/snyk-growing-agentic-ai-adoption-report/
- vmblog, "Enterprises Are Blind to Two-Thirds of Their Own AI Attack Surface. The Blind Spot Is Growing Fast." — https://vmblog.com/news/enterprises-are-blind-to-two-thirds-of-their-own-ai-attack-surface-the-blind-spot-is-growing-fast/
- IT Pro, "2026 State of Agentic AI Adoption: Anonymized Insights from 500+ Evo by Snyk AI Discovery Assessments" — https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/2026-state-of-agentic-ai-adoption-anonymized-insights-from-500-evo-by-snyk-ai-discovery-assessments
- Snyk, "2026 State of Agentic AI Adoption" (landing page) — https://snyk.io/lp/state-of-agentic-ai-adoption/
- Snyk, "What nearly 10,000 developer environments reveal about agentic development risk" — https://snyk.io/blog/agentic-development-security-ai-coding-risk/
- GovInfoSecurity, "2026 State of Agentic AI Adoption" — https://www.govinfosecurity.com/whitepapers/2026-state-agentic-ai-adoption-w-16256

Claims sourced here (as reported): Snyk's 2026 State of Agentic AI Adoption, covered on 5 August 2026, drew on roughly 3,044 enterprise environments and about 1.39 million code repositories. It found that the full AI attack surface is approximately three times what a model inventory shows — beyond the large language models sit agent frameworks, MCP servers, retrieval systems, vector databases, datasets and supporting tools — so most security programs, asked what AI they run, produce a list of models and miss about two-thirds of the picture; the ratio was reported constant across every region measured. It found that 46.9% of AI-using organizations run agentic architectures built on AI agents, MCP servers, or both, with more than half of adopters running the full stack, and that the full-stack share has nearly doubled since Snyk's January 2026 report. The quotation "As AI-driven development accelerates, human oversight alone simply cannot keep pace" is attributed to Gabriel Brolo Tobar, Senior Security Engineer, Yalo, in the report.

- TechCrunch (Gravitee), "AI agents just doubled inside the enterprise. Confidence rose faster than control did" — https://techcrunch.com/sponsor/gravitee/ai-agents-just-doubled-inside-the-enterprise-confidence-rose-faster-than-control-did/

Claims sourced here (as reported): Gravitee's State of AI Agent Security 2026 found that 88% of organizations reported a confirmed or suspected AI-agent security incident in the past year, while 82% of executives feel confident their existing policies protect them from unauthorized agent actions. Only 14.4% of organizations report all AI agents going live with full security/IT approval; only 21% have runtime visibility into what their agents are doing; mean monitoring coverage is about 52%, meaning roughly 48% of production agents run unsecured; and the enterprise agent estate roughly doubled in about four months while security coverage barely moved.

## The regulatory backdrop — EU AI Act enforcement, still live (carried forward)

- European Commission (Press corner), "Commission starts enforcing AI Act rules and new transparency requirements on 2 August" — https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1714
- European Commission (Shaping Europe's digital future), same title — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august
- Help Net Security, "EU begins enforcing AI Act, putting AI models under the microscope" (4 August 2026) — https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/08/04/eu-ai-act-enforcement-ai-models/
- Wilson Sonsini, "EU AI Act Enforcement Phase Begins" — https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-enforcement-phase-begins.html
- EU Artificial Intelligence Act, "Article 11: Technical Documentation" — https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/11/
- EU Artificial Intelligence Act, "Article 26: Obligations of Deployers of High-Risk AI Systems" — https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/

Claims sourced here (as reported): EU AI Act enforcement has been live since 2 August 2026, with the AI Office and national competent authorities exercising powers to request documentation, evaluate models, order corrections, restrict availability and fine. On 4 August 2026, France's CNIL issued formal information requests to 14 financial institutions operating credit-scoring algorithms, demanding the Article 11 technical documentation required of high-risk systems; three extension requests were denied. The penalty ceiling is the higher of €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover (Article 99), with €7.5 million or 1.5% for supplying incorrect information. Deployers of high-risk systems must retain the automatically generated logs for at least six months (Article 26). The CNIL specifics are relayed from secondary August 2026 coverage as reported.
