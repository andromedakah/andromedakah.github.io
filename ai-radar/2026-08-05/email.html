1 · Executive Summary

Yesterday's edition ended on the dossier — the provable record of what your AI does. Today three reports expose the precondition nobody costed: you cannot document, govern, or defend an estate you cannot see — and the enterprise can see only about a third of its own AI. Snyk's 2026 State of Agentic AI Adoption (covered 5 Aug; 3,044 environments, ~1.39M repositories) finds the full AI surface is ~3× a model inventory — beyond the LLMs sit agent frameworks, MCP servers, retrieval systems, vector databases and datasets no one tracks, so most security programs miss two-thirds of the picture, a ratio constant across every region. 46.9% now run agentic architectures, a full-stack share nearly doubled since January. SAP published "AI Agent Sprawl: Why AI Governance Is Now a Board-Level Issue" and shipped an AI Agent Hub; its LeanIX survey finds 98% deploy agents but fewer than half have an inventory. Gartner: 150,000+ agents per Fortune 500 by 2028, 13% governed.

1. The dossier presumes an inventory — and it's two-thirds dark. The Article 11 file, the six-month log, the sovereignty proof all rest on a map that omits most of the territory. You cannot write the manifest of a warehouse you haven't lit.

2. Confidence is inverted from control. Gravitee: 88% had an agent incident last year while 82% of executives feel protected; only 14.4% approved every agent, only 21% have runtime visibility, ~48% of production agents run unsecured. The estate doubled in ~4 months.

3. It grows fastest where it's darkest. Agents and MCP servers — the parts no one inventories — are the parts multiplying.

Bottom line: the market rewards a provable, governed AI layer and the regulator demands its dossier — but both assume you can see the layer, and two-thirds of it is unlit and growing. Light the warehouse before you promise the manifest: run discovery across the whole surface, assign an owner, and only then is the dossier a document rather than a hope. You cannot govern what you cannot see.

2 · Allegory — The Dark Warehouse

A prosperous merchant house keeps a neat ledger of the goods on its lit shop floor, and when the board asks whether the stock is in order, the owner says yes — the shelves match the book. A truthful ledger, and a small one: the shop floor was never where the house kept most of its goods. Behind it sits a warehouse three times the size, and dark. It filled without a plan — an agent here, an MCP server there, a retrieval store, a dataset copied and forgotten — stacked in the black beyond the last lantern. The owner isn't lying; he cannot see the warehouse, so he reports on the only lit room, and misses two-thirds of the building. Worse, the dark room is the one growing.

And recall who comes calling. The thief works the unlit warehouse, where a missing crate goes unnoticed for months (88 in a hundred such houses were robbed last year, while 82 in a hundred owners told their boards the locks were sound). The assessor — the one who came yesterday for the manifest of the whole building — will not accept a shop-floor ledger; she walks to the warehouse door, finds it dark, and notes the merchant cannot say what's inside. The lit ledger isn't wrong; it's simply not an inventory of the house. The only first move is to carry a lantern into the warehouse and count — every aisle, every crate, an owner for each — and keep the room lit. Only then does the ledger become a manifest, and the manifest a dossier.

The question it forces: When our board asks whether our AI is under control, are we opening the shop-floor ledger — the model list — and mistaking it for the house? If our real estate is 3× what we can name, and the unseen two-thirds is the part still growing, who carries the lantern — discovers every agent, MCP server and dataset, names an owner, and keeps the room lit — before the thief and the assessor find the dark warehouse for us?

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Light the warehouse — run discovery across the whole AI surface, not the model list. Your real estate is ~3× what you can name and two-thirds is dark. Stand up a scan that finds every model, agent, MCP server, retrieval store, vector database and dataset, and produce one authoritative inventory. Treat "could we hand a regulator a complete, current map of our AI this week?" as pass/fail — the dossier is written from this inventory.

2. Close the confidence gap — assume breached until proven seen. 82% feel protected while 88% were breached through an agent. For every agent, record security approval (14.4% do), runtime visibility (21% do), and an owner (fewer than half can say). Report the ratio of the estate you can actually see to the board — not the model count, which is the lit third and the reassuring lie.

3. Make discovery continuous and model-neutral — the warehouse fills nightly. The estate doubles in ~4 months, so an annual inventory is stale before it's filed. Build discovery and ownership that survive an engine swap — so adding DeepSeek V4-Flash-0731 or swapping Opus 5 for Gemini never returns a crate to the dark, and every new agent lands on the inventory with an owner the day it ships. Own the lantern; then the whole month's discipline rests on ground you can see.
