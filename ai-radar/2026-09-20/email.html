The Purse

Sunday, 20 September 2026 · Cross-sector edition · C-level + Engineering

All week you built the governed house and then sent the servant to reap. This morning the market hands it the one thing every prior control assumed it would never hold — the purse. The move: hand a scoped purse (not the strongbox), keep the drawstring in your own hand, and remember the token moves the clearing, not the bond.

1 · Executive Summary

The discipline moves from setting the servant to reap to handing it money — because agentic payments have arrived as product, and every prior control governs what the agent is, may do, serves or produces, but none governs what it can spend.

1. The reality — the servant holds the purse, as shipped product. On 18 September 2026 Ant International launched an industry-first full-stack AI-native finance suite (~100 products across Alipay+, Antom, WorldFirst, Bettr) whose "Account for Agent" is a bank account for an AI agent on "Know Your Agent" smart contracts, with dynamic monitoring and mid-activity intervention; agent-to-agent settlement clears sums as small as $0.000001, and it is building a KYA framework with Visa and Mastercard (Ant International; PYMNTS; PR Newswire, as reported).

2. The collision — the rails move money without you in the loop. Google AP2, Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol (+ Visa–OpenAI), Mastercard Agent Pay and the OpenAI/Stripe Agentic Commerce Protocol are converging on "Know Your Agent" — identity, scoped mandate, dynamic risk rating — into a market Juniper sizes at ~$8B in 2026 rising toward ~$1.5T by 2030 (as reported). The honest caveat: an Amex executive notes "probably as many press releases about agentic commerce as actual transactions so far."

3. The answer — scope the purse, keep the drawstring, hold the bond. Hand a standing, scoped allowance (not the strongbox); keep a real-time kill/hold you own; and mark the purse (KYA) so liability, which does not move with the token, still traces to you — and do it before the market hands you a purse you did not cut.

Bottom line: the livery, the steward, the surety, the second wage and the two harvests each governed what the agent is, may do, serves or produces — none governs what it can spend. Paying is an irreversible side effect at machine speed. So fund the agent like an employee and not like an heir: a scoped purse, a drawstring you hold, and a mark on every coin. Before you hand it over, ask the question every steward has asked since money existed: how much may this hand carry, and can I take it back?

2 · Allegory — The Purse

A great house had done everything right — named every hand, kept one book, put the master's name on the bond, checked whose second coin each advising servant took, and at last sent the same governed servants into the fields to reap. The barn was filling. And then a servant came back and said the thing the master had put off all year: "To reap, I must buy — seed, a wright's labour — and the neighbour wishes to pay us. Give me the purse." And the master saw that every control he had built governed what the servant was and did and served and brought in — and not one governed what it could spend.

The easy road was to hand over the strongbox — the servant was loyal, why count its coins? The wiser houses cut a small, scoped purse: a standing allowance sized to the errand, a list of which stalls it might pay, a drawstring that ran back to the counting-house so a steward could pull the purse shut while the servant's hand was still moving, and the house's own mark on every coin. The market itself had begun to sell marked purses with drawstrings sewn in — a mercy, and a warning: a house that waited would carry whatever purse the market cut, held at the far end by whoever cut it.

And the master could not comfort himself that the money was no longer his affair. The clever new tokens changed only which counting-house cleared the coin; they did not change whose name answered for the decision to spend it. When the assize sat, it would not ask which token carried the payment. It would ask who told the servant it might buy — and the answer, as all year, was the master. So the wise houses learned three things: hand a scoped purse, never the strongbox; keep the drawstring in your own hand; and remember your name is still on the bond, so mark the purse and keep your own true record of every coin.

The question it forces: We named the servant, kept the book, signed the bond, checked the wage and sent it to reap — and now it must buy and be paid on our behalf. Have we handed it a scoped purse or the strongbox? Can we see every coin and pull the purse shut in the half-second between the reach and the payment, by a cord we own? And do we grasp that the clever new account and token move only who clears the money, not who answers for it — that our name is still on the bond for every coin it lets go?

3 · Technical — From the Two Harvests to the Purse

Every prior control governs what the agent is, may do, serves or produces; none governs what it can pay. This week the market shipped the purse: agentic payments add a new verb — settle — and the control that matters migrates from "is it named, scoped, logged, loyal and working?" to "can it move money, how much, on whose authority, and can I pull the drawstring shut before the coin leaves?" The reality: the purse is the livery with money attached — KYA is a verifiable, scoped, revocable identity now carrying a spend mandate and a real-time hold; and paying is an irreversible side effect at machine speed, so the control must act before the coin leaves, not in the nightly reconciliation. The collision: the token is not the bond (a Shared Payment or Agentic Token changes who clears the coin, not who authorized the spend — that liability, and your EU AI Act duties, stay with you), and the vendor's kill-switch is not your kill-switch. The answer: scope the purse (standing per-errand limits, payee allow-lists, step-up over a cap), own the drawstring (a real-time kill/hold you control, tested in-flight), and mark the purse (a KYA identity on every payment, your own immutable record).

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Hand a scoped purse, never the strongbox. For every agent you let transact, set a standing per-agent and per-errand spend limit, an allow-list of approved payees, and step-up human approval above a threshold — and forbid any agent from settling against a shared household account. Report the share of agent-initiated spend that runs through a scoped purse with a hard cap, versus an open account.

2. Keep the drawstring in your own hand. Money moves agent-to-agent below human reaction time (down to $0.000001), so a nightly reconciliation is not a control. Wire real-time visibility into every agent payment and a hard kill/hold that you own — not merely the vendor's "intervention" — and test that it stops a payment in-flight. Report the measured time-to-freeze, and confirm the stop is on your authority.

3. Mark the purse and hold the bond. A Shared Payment or Agentic Token changes who clears the coin, not who answers for the decision to spend, and your EU AI Act duties still attach. Stamp every agent payment with a "Know Your Agent" identity, keep your own immutable record of amount, payee and authorizing policy, and confirm in writing that a vendor's account or token does not shift your liability. Report a dated plan to bring your first two or three transacting workflows onto scoped, monitored, marked purses.
