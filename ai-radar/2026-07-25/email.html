2 · Allegory — The Provenance

In the world of great paintings, the value of a masterwork lives as much in its paper trail as in its paint. A canvas can be dazzling — every expert agreeing it is first-rank — and still be nearly unhangable, because no one can prove where it has been. Provenance is that proof: the unbroken chain of custody from the studio to your wall. When the chain is whole, a museum hangs it, an insurer covers it, a buyer pays in full. When it breaks — a gap, a disputed attribution, a whisper the thing was looted or copied — the painting keeps its beauty but loses its title. And a masterpiece you can't prove you may lawfully own is one you can't safely hang.

The cruelty: the accusation needn't be proven to do its damage — the cloud itself freezes the sale. And provenance disputes cut both ways: some are open-and-shut theft, some are political or thin, collapsing the moment a scholar checks the dates. The serious collector treats both the same cold way — not faith, not reflex, but the file: demand the papers, read the dates, insure the title, and never let one contested canvas anchor the whole collection. This week the enterprise got exactly this in software: the open-weight fallback arrived with a cloud over its deed — a government alleging it was copied, a sanctions threat that would freeze the sale, scholars insisting the dates don't fit. You're not the court; you're the collector.

The question it forces: The fallback we were told to keep is a masterwork with a contested deed — and the accusation, true or not, is already a cloud. Do we hang it on faith, refuse it on a headline, or do what the great houses do — demand the chain of custody, price the title risk, insure what we can, and hold enough works of enough origins that no single disputed canvas takes the whole gallery down?

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Open the file. Make provenance a governed attribute: before any open model reaches production, document its chain of custody — who trained it, the license read in full (Modified MIT ≠ MIT), training-data claims, and the indemnity posture. Add "model provenance" to third-party-risk and procurement, next to cost and capability.

2. Diversify the gallery. Don't anchor a critical workflow on one open model, least of all one under a sanctions cloud. Qualify two self-hostable open models of different origin (MIT-licensed DeepSeek V4 alongside any Kimi K3 you vet), and keep context and eval gate portable — a switch, not a migration.

3. Price the title risk — rehearse the un-hang. Tabletop it: if a self-hosted model were sanctioned or IP-challenged tomorrow, could we name every workflow it touches and cut over in a week? Fold model origin and license into your EU AI Act evidence before enforcement lands in 8 days.
