1 · Executive Summary

For a week this radar argued a single thesis under every layer of the agent stack: own the knowledge, rent the model. This weekend the market stopped arguing and started proving it — at both ends of the stack at once. The shift is no longer "which layer do I buy?" but "now that the reasoning engine is a metered utility any rival can rent, and a frontier model can be dropped into the app you already run, where does my durable value sit?" The answer, stated by the vendors themselves: not in the engine, which is rented and everywhere — but in the repertoire only you can play.

1. The engine — metered to the metal. NVIDIA's Q2 FY2027 results (26 Aug) put revenue at ~$96.2B (up ~106% YoY), data center ~$89.0B (up ~117%), a Q3 guide of ~$108B, and a ~70% FY28 growth outlook. Jensen Huang's framing is the tell: "compute is revenue." The reasoning engine is now something you buy by the token; the money is in supplying it, not in owning any one model.

2. The repertoire — the frontier lab agrees. The same day, Salesforce and Anthropic announced "Claudeforce," making Claude the default reasoning model across Agentforce and Slack, plus a "Salesforce in Claude" surface (37 prebuilt skills, open beta ~Sept) inside the Salesforce Trust Boundary. Amodei's load-bearing line locates the value in your data: point Claude at "the customer information and business context they've been building in Salesforce for decades."

3. The demand — engines monetizing at scale. OpenAI enterprise revenue has reportedly overtaken consumer at a ~$40B run rate; Anthropic's run rate has reportedly passed ~$65B ahead of a possible fall IPO; Cognition is in talks near ~$40B. The engine vendors are the ones getting paid — which is exactly why the engine is not your edge.

Bottom line: the week built a stack; this weekend the market underwrote its thesis. When the reasoning engine is a rented utility (NVIDIA: compute is revenue) and a frontier model can be booked straight into the app you already run (Claudeforce), the durable asset is not the model — it is the repertoire only you can play: the proprietary data, workflows, governance and customer relationship you have built for decades. "We picked the best model" describes a touring virtuoso every rival can hire; "we own the data, the workflows and the audience, and we let the best rented engine perform our repertoire inside our boundary" describes a season worth subscribing to. Rent the engine, own the repertoire.

2 · Allegory — The Repertoire

A concert house had spent a fortune, season after season, chasing the greatest soloist it could hire — whichever virtuoso was the talk of the city, put on the marquee, told itself this was what filled the seats. Then two things happened in one week. Down in the pit, the company that supplied the power announced it had never sold so much, and that every hour of playing would now be metered and billed to the second. The engine was a utility. And at the marquee, the greatest touring virtuoso signed to be bookable into any house — your house tonight, your rival's tomorrow, even coming inside to play from your own stands. The star was no longer scarce; he was a service.

So every house faced the same expensive question: if the virtuoso plays everywhere and the pit is billed by the hour, what is left that is actually ours? The houses that panicked bid higher for the same star their rivals also booked, played the same public-domain program, and wondered why a full marquee no longer meant a full room. The houses that kept their footing saw what the star could not carry in his case: the repertoire. Decades of music no one else held, arranged for their own hall, and a room of subscribers who came for the music only heard here. So they handed the virtuoso their archive and let him perform their repertoire, inside their walls, under their rules — and kept the two things he would never bring: the music and the audience. The star was rented for the night; the season was theirs.

The question it forces: a frontier model can now be dropped straight into the apps we already run, and the compute beneath it is billed by the token. So what still makes a customer choose us? Is our distinctiveness the model we picked — which every rival can also pick — or the proprietary data, workflows and relationships we own? Are we spending on the marquee or on the repertoire? And when the best rented engine performs inside our house, do we still own the music and the audience?

5 · So What — 3 Moves for the Next 30 Days

1. Inventory the repertoire. The model is a metered utility; your edge is not. Name the three proprietary assets that actually differentiate you — the data corpus, the workflows, the customer relationships — and rate honestly how well-owned, structured and governed each one is. Report one thing to the board: the specific proprietary asset a competitor could not replicate by licensing the same model tomorrow.

2. Rent the engine, keep it swappable. Do not let a single vendor's model become load-bearing just because it is the default in an app you already run. Abstract every model behind your own interface, price reasoning as a variable per-token cost, and build on the open standards (A2A + MCP under the AAIF) so you can move engines without re-owning the integration.

3. Let the star perform inside your boundary. Claudeforce's design tell is the trust boundary: a frontier model reasoning over your data inside your governance, not your data shipped out to the model. Keep proprietary data, workflows and the audit trail inside a boundary you control — reconstructable to the Article 12 standard — and keep ownership of the customer relationship. Report: the share of AI value on assets you own, and the share of reasoning you could move to a different engine within 30 days.
